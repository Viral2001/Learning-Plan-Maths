## 1. Hard rules (gates enforce most; fix content, never a gate)
- Class 5 students, 10 to 11 years old: short plain sentences. Characters and settings are the book's: Preetha, Adrit, Trisha, their grandmother (kin word, no name token); the quilt, the lace reels, the rug, the four tables, Preetha's desk, rectangles A, B, C, the two gardens, the mats, the grids, the square tiles and the die. The plan's own WE use the same room (patches, trays, mats, tables). Do not invent facts or datasets: every number that is not a plain WE number comes from the book or from section 4. A number the plan adds is a "practice value" and the tool or the question says so (WE numbers are the plan's own and need no label).
- **Words:** the book says perimeter, area, border, tile, patch, lace, unit, unit square, square cm, square m, length, breadth, rectangle, square, triangle, circle, grid, region. Do NOT bring in other vocabulary in anything the student reads: no "pentagon", "polygon", "hexagon", "quadrilateral", "diagonal" (say "cut from corner to corner"), "regular", "formula" (say "rule"), "base", "height of a triangle", "volume", "decimal", "fraction", "hectare", "acre". Write the shape of Let Us Do Q1 as "the five-sided shape" and "the shape with 4 equal sides". The red and green triangles of the desk are "triangles".
- **No em dash and no en dash** in any string or comment (the book's dashes become commas, colons or "to"). Minus is U+2212 `−`. Use `×` and `÷`. Hyphen only inside words ("five-sided", "two-lace").
- **Units:** write `cm`, `m`, `square cm`, `square m`, `square units`, `units`. Never "cm2", "sq cm", "cm^2", "cm²". A unit is separated from its number by a space ("6 cm", "24 square cm"). The book prints "cm square" in the garden question; the plan writes `square cm` there too (same meaning; the converter handles `square cm` and not `cm square`).
- Never write "NCERT", "textbook", "page N", "Fig.", "Figure N" or exercise numbers in anything the student reads (names, teach lines, steps, questions, tool text, image text). Say "your maths book". The labels "Table 1", "Table 3", "Table 4", "garden A", "mat (a)", "shape (a)" and the rectangle letters A, B, C are the book's own picture labels and are allowed. Exception: the `textbook=[...]` argument of `A()` quotes the book verbatim (still no dashes).
- **Arithmetic must be true in every string.** The equation gate `eqcheck` ("plain") checks only whole-number chains (`a op b = c`). Keep every chain on one line and simple: ONE operator per chain, `15 + 10 + 15 + 10 = 50`, `6 × 4 = 24`, `42 × 30 = 1260`. A chain with two different operators (`2 × 15 + 2 × 10 = 50`) must be broken into two chains (`2 × 15 = 30`, `2 × 10 = 20`, `30 + 20 = 50`). Do not write an equation whose two sides count different things. Do NOT write units inside a chain (`6 × 4 = 24 square cm` is read as the chain `6 × 4 = 24`, which is fine, but `6 cm × 4 cm = 24 square cm` is how the BOOK prints it: write it only in a `textbook=[...]` quote or in the Example 1 answer, then test the build). Re-compute every number you write with a script.
- 100% coverage: every id in `content.NCERT_ITEMS` (section 3) is attached to a concept: a `text` id through `A(..., ncert=[...])` (or a WE's `ncert=`) or a DOER's `ncert=`, a question id as an `FIO` item of the DOER of its topic.
- **One video per concept.** A teaching concept `A()` has exactly ONE `WE` in `examples` (that WE is the concept video) with the PLAN's own numbers: never the exact number set of a book question. The WE seeds in section 2 are chosen to avoid every book number set; keep them or choose others that also avoid them (book number sets: 15 by 10 quilt, 4 and 5 equal sides, 26 and 18 perimeters, 15 by 6 rug, 9 by 6 desk, 3 by 4, 2 by 4, 2 by 5, 4 by 3, 5 by 2, 3 by 2, 9 by 2, 6 by 3, 6 by 6, 4 by 7, 12 by 4, 3 by 3, 6 by 5, 42 by 34, 64 and 16, 32 6 and 12, 5 by 5 and 6 by 4 of the printed examples). The hook concept has `video_focus=["HOOK"]`, `enrichment=True`, no WE. DOER concepts carry a recap, their question items and at most one tool, no WE.
- **Questions.** Every book question is a `FIO(cov, label, question, steps, answer, kind=..., q_figure=..., s_figure=...)` in the DOER of its topic, in the book's order; `core/qt.py` moves each into its own concept of the "Questions: ..." topic that follows. `question` is the book's wording (copy it from section 3; "textbook" never appears). `steps` = the numbered working, one operation per line, values before combining, a check as the last step; `answer` = short final answer, AT MOST 150 characters (the gate cuts the displayed LaTeX at 220; LaTeX adds characters, and the build measures it). Open-ended items get a labelled "Sample answer" built by reasoning, with the practice values said to be practice values, and end with "Your answer may differ." (in the answer if short, else in the last step). Add `q_figure` (one plain sentence: ONLY what the question gives, never the answer, a count that is the answer, or a hint) when the question card needs a drawing, `s_figure` when the solution card needs one (it may show the answer). One sentence each, no colon-lists longer than the sentence needs.
- **Labels (`book`) decide the Questions topic** (`core/question_naming.py`): a label starting `Let Us Do` goes to "Questions: Let us do"; a label starting `Example N` goes to "Questions: Example" (the number is the label's own, use exactly the numbers given in section 3); any other label goes to a topic named by the text BEFORE ITS FIRST COLON ("Tiles and Tables: how many tiles" goes to "Questions: Tiles and Tables"). Use exactly the labels given in section 3.
- Names: DOER name starts with `Practice: ` or `Pull it together and do it: `.
- An idea topic has 2 to 4 primary concepts INCLUDING its DOER (last). The intro T1 is `topic_type="framing"` with `""` objective, all concepts `enrichment=True`: C1 hook (`video_focus=["HOOK"]`) and C3 a DOER with `role="try_it"` and the free-try tool (the framing topic holds exactly ONE video, the hook, and no book question). Topic objective on each idea topic. Bloom words: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze.
- Misconception ids come from `content.MISCONCEPTIONS` (section 6); attach the relevant ones to teach concepts, DOERs and tools.
- `teach` lines: the first line is a prediction question ("Predict first: ..."), then the reveal, then the rule in one line. `minutes` 4 to 8.
- Tool specs (`T(...)`): section 5. Exactly 5 to 7 challenges, exactly 4 hints.
- LaTeX display text: `concept_plan.py` converts every FIO text with `ch_latex_house` then `latex_wrap`. Quirks: a part label glued to a number becomes one maths span (so labels get a colon: `(a): ...`); a blank standing alone is `___`; a unit letter glued to a number reads as a variable (always a space: `6 cm`). Write part labels as `(a)`, `(b)` before the part, once per part. After the build the lead scans the built plan for `$$`, stray letters and unbalanced `$`.

Teaching concept seeds. The WE is the plan's own; numbers below are verified (section 4) and avoid every book number set. Keep them. Write the teach lines yourself (prediction, reveal, rule).
- **T1.C1 hook:** Preetha and Adrit's grandmother made a quilt cover from old clothes and wants to put lace round it. She has three reels. Which one would go all the way round? No answer, no number of units, ends on the question. (book story text only.)
- **T1B.C1 perimeter:** the border is the line round the edge; its length is the perimeter; walk round and add the sides (a mat 8 patches long and 3 patches broad: 8 + 3 + 8 + 3 = 22); when all sides are equal, count the sides and multiply. WE: a mat with 5 equal sides, each 7 cm: 7 + 7 + 7 + 7 + 7 = 35 and 5 × 7 = 35: 35 cm.
- **T2.C1 tiles that cover without gaps:** a tile is a shape used to cover a surface; it tiles when there are no gaps and no overlaps; squares, rectangles and triangles tile; circles leave gaps. WE: a tray top: 12 square tiles in 3 rows of 4 cover it with no gaps; 6 round tiles in 2 rows of 3 leave gaps between them and at the corners (practice values for the tray).
- **T2.C2 area: the region covered:** the region the tiles cover is its area; count the tiles; say the tile in the unit ("24 square units", "48 triangle units"); the same region needs different numbers of different tiles. WE: a tray top covered by 24 square tiles in 4 rows of 6; cut every square from corner to corner into 2 triangles: 24 × 2 = 48 triangles: 24 square units is 48 triangle units.
- **T3.C1 comparing areas:** if one shape fits inside the other, the one inside has the smaller area; if neither fits inside the other, cutting and laying does not settle it, so cover both with the same small square and count. WE: mat P is 7 squares long and 2 broad, mat Q is 4 long and 4 broad: neither covers the other; P: 7 × 2 = 14 squares, Q: 4 × 4 = 16 squares; Q is larger.
- **T3.C2 unit square and counting squares:** the area of a square with sides 1 unit is 1 unit square; with sides 1 cm it is 1 square cm; the area of a shape on a grid is the number of unit squares it covers; two half squares make one whole square. WE: a triangle on a 1 cm grid covers 6 whole squares and 4 half squares: 6 + 4 ÷ 2 = 8: write as 4 ÷ 2 = 2 and 6 + 2 = 8; 8 square cm.
- **T4.C1 same area, different perimeter:** WE: three rectangles of 16 unit squares: 4 by 4, 8 by 2, 16 by 1: perimeters 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 = 16, 8 + 2 + 8 + 2 = 20, 16 + 1 + 16 + 1 = 34. Rule: the same area can have different perimeters (the long thin shape has the longest border).
- **T4.C2 same perimeter, different area:** WE: three rectangles of perimeter 20 units: 5 by 5, 7 by 3, 9 by 1: areas 25, 21, 9. Also: 1 by 8 has area 8 and perimeter 18; 3 by 3 has area 9 and perimeter 12: the area grew and the perimeter shrank, so a bigger area does not always mean a bigger perimeter.
- **T5.C1 area of a rectangle:** count by rows (rows × patches in a row), then see that the number in a row is the length and the number of rows is the breadth: area = length × breadth; in square cm when the sides are in cm, in square m when they are in m. WE: a rectangular patchwork of 5 rows of 8 patches, each patch 1 cm by 1 cm: 5 × 8 = 40; length 8 cm, breadth 5 cm, 8 × 5 = 40: 40 square cm.
- **T5.C2 perimeter of a rectangle:** the opposite sides of a rectangle are equal, so the border is length + length + breadth + breadth = 2 × length + 2 × breadth. WE: a rectangle 11 cm long and 4 cm broad: 11 + 4 + 11 + 4 = 30; 2 × 11 = 22, 2 × 4 = 8, 22 + 8 = 30: 30 cm.
- **T5.C3 squares:** a square is a rectangle whose length and breadth are equal: area = length × length; perimeter = 4 × length. WE: a square patch of side 7 cm: 7 × 7 = 49 square cm; 4 × 7 = 28 cm.
- **T6.C1 missing side:** area = length × breadth, so breadth = area ÷ length (check by multiplying back). WE: a lawn of area 72 square m and length 9 m: 72 ÷ 9 = 8; check 9 × 8 = 72: breadth 8 m.
- **T6.C2 side in parts:** a side can be marked in two parts; add them to get the whole side, then multiply; check by finding the area of each part and adding. WE: a rectangle 20 cm wide whose height is marked 5 cm and 7 cm in two parts: 5 + 7 = 12; 20 × 12 = 240; check: 20 × 5 = 100, 20 × 7 = 140, 100 + 140 = 240: 240 square cm.
- **T7.C1 outer edges of tiles:** every tile side is 1; the perimeter of a figure of tiles is the number of outer edges (edges that are not shared with another tile); a tile added along 1 edge makes the perimeter 2 more; a tile added in a corner where it touches 2 edges leaves it the same. WE: 5 tiles placed like the letter L (3 in the top row, 2 more below the left tile): count the outer edges: 2 + 2 + 3 + 2 + 3 = 12 (left tile of the top row 2 outer edges, middle top tile 2, right top tile 3, the two lower tiles 2 and 3); adding a tile at the end of the top row: 14; adding one in the corner where the arms meet: 12.
- **T8.C1 summary:** a walk round the patchwork room, one idea per stop, with a short check after each: perimeter (quilt 15 by 10: 15 + 10 + 15 + 10 = 50); tiles and area (24 squares are 48 triangles); the unit square (a 1 cm square is 1 square cm); area and perimeter differ (16 squares: perimeters 16, 20, 34); rectangle area and perimeter (8 by 5: 40 and 26); square (side 7: 49 and 28); missing side (area 72, length 9: breadth 8); tiles (outer edges). Every check uses only numbers of this list or section 4.
