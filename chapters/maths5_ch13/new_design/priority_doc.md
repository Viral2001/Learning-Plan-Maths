## SECTION A: Chapter Understanding

### A1. Chapter name
Animal Jumps (Class 5, Maths Mela, Chapter 13). The chapter is about factors, multiples, common multiples and common factors.

### A2. Main learning objectives
Students will be able to:
1. Say that a number a box multiplies by is a factor of every number that comes out, that the numbers that come out are multiples of it, and that more than one multiplier can fit a list of numbers (the hidden numbers, p. 164).
2. Make every array of a number with square tiles, write each array as a multiplication and list all the factors of the number, including 1 and the number itself.
3. Show that a number is a multiple of each of its factors, and that a number with only the array 1 × the number has only two factors (the book's prime numbers 13 and 37).
4. Find the common multiples of two numbers with two animals' jumps on a number line and with lists of multiples, name the first common multiple, and see that the others are its multiples.
5. Use jumps from 0 to tell when a jump size lands exactly on a number (the jump size is a factor of it), and find the common factors of two or more numbers.
6. Test statements about factors and multiples with examples, and decide when a number is divisible by 2, 5 and 10.
7. Use common multiples and common factors in the book's word problems (the cobbled road, the hunting days, Mowgli's trail).

### A4. Textbook activities to keep exactly
- The hidden numbers box with its three questions (p. 164), the arrays of 15 and 12 (p. 164), the four blanks of 12 (p. 165), Let Us Do on arrays with the prime numbers note (p. 165).
- The rabbit and frog number line, the spider and grasshopper number line, the lists of multiples of 4 and 6 (pp. 165, 166).
- The cobbled road (Q2) and Mowgli's trail with its friends (Q3), with every question printed on pp. 167 to 170.
- All eleven numbered questions with every part. The book prints no solved example with a number in this chapter, so there is no `Example N` question.

### A5. Natural learning sequence
The hidden box (a free try, then the names factor and multiple) -> arrays show factors -> a number is a multiple of each factor, and numbers with only two factors -> common multiples with jumps -> the pattern of common multiples -> jumps that land on a number -> common factors of two numbers -> checking statements and divisibility by 2, 5 and 10 with the book's last questions -> revision. This is the book's own order. The only change: the prime-numbers margin question (p. 165) gets its own short concept after the arrays, and Q8 to Q11 (pp. 169, 170) are grouped in one closing topic because they mix factors and multiples.

### A6. Points the textbook leaves unclear (to confirm; the plan does not guess)
1. **Let Us Do Q1 and Q7 print the letter (f) twice** (the third column starts again at (f)); each has nine pairs. The plan letters them (a) to (i) in the book's order.
2. **The last two stones of Mowgli's trail are both printed 59** (the trail has 61 stones). The plan reads the last stone as 60, so the trail runs from 0 to 60. No friend lives there; only the "numbers he touches" lines for jumps of 5 and 10 include 60.
3. **Sher Khan's house:** the picture prints 26 in red beside the tiger, but Q10 says Sher Khan's house is on 25, and the jumps of 5 (25, 30, 35, 50) need 25. The plan places him on 25; the red 26 is a printing slip.
4. **The book's answer lines are incomplete.** For jumps of 2 it prints 4, 12, 14, 50 (the red numbers in the picture are 4, 12, 26, 50) and for jumps of 3 it prints 9, 21, 39, 57; but Baloo (30) is also reached by 2 and by 3, and the frog (12) also by 3. The plan gives the full lists: jump 2: ant 4, frog 12, bird 14, Baloo 30, rabbit 50; jump 3: spider 9, frog 12, Kaa 21, Baloo 30, deer 39, monkey 57; jump 5: Sher Khan 25, Baloo 30, Akela 35, rabbit 50; jump 10: Baloo 30, rabbit 50.
5. **Friends and their places:** only some friends have a number printed. The plan reads: ant 4, spider 9, frog 12, bird 14, Kaa 21, Sher Khan 25, Baloo 30, Akela 35, deer 39, rabbit 50, monkey 57 (from the picture, Q10 and the jumps of 5).
6. **Q2, where the food is:** the food is drawn after stone 64 and has no number. The plan reads the end of the road as stone 64: Robby lands on it (4 × 16 = 64), Deeku does not (6 × 10 = 60, 6 × 11 = 66), so only Robby reaches the food and he is first. If the food were a stone further on, the answer changes.
7. **Q9, counting the days:** the book does not say whether the first day is day 0 or day 1. The plan counts the start as day 0, so they hunt together on days 15, 30, 45 and so on. With the first day as day 1 it would be days 1, 16, 31 and so on.
8. **The blanks on p. 168** ("Which numbers will he touch if he jumps by 5 steps? ___. 5 is a common factor of the numbers ___."): the plan answers the first blank with the numbers he touches on the trail and the second with the friends' numbers.
9. **Q8 (f), "0 cannot be a factor of any number":** the book gives no key. The plan answers True: a jump of 0 never leaves 0, and 0 times any number is 0. Whether 0 is a factor of 0 itself is not discussed.
10. **Q11, "divisible by 10 only":** no number can go there, because a number divisible by 10 is also divisible by 2 and 5. The plan says so (the answer is "none") and puts 90, 30 and 40 in the middle. The circles' overlaps of exactly two circles are also empty.
11. **Q1, Q4 (d), Q10 and the opener ask for "any" answers or open lists:** the plan gives one reasoned sample (the first common multiple and its multiples; the jumps 1, 6 and 12; the jumps 2, 3, 6, 10, 15 and 30) and says the student's answer may differ where it can. Q10 (b) "What number of jumps" is read as the jump size.
12. **The four numbers of the box** are only in the picture; the plan reads 28, 36, 48 and 72 off it. **Robby and Deeku** are named only in Q2; the plan lets the rabbit and the deer of the opener picture be Robby and Deeku in the hook.
13. **Prime numbers** are named (13 and 37) and "Why?" is asked, but the word is not defined. The plan answers from the arrays: each has only the array 1 × the number, so only two factors. It teaches nothing more about prime numbers.
14. **Q8 and Q11 need ideas the chapter does not teach in words before them.** The plan adds one short idea for each, using only the chapter's own words: checking a statement with examples, and divisible = a factor (the book says a factor "can divide completely").
15. **The unnumbered Let Us Do on arrays (p. 165)** is called "Let Us Do (arrays), Q1" in the plan, only to give it a place in the Questions topic; the book does not number it.
16. **Printing slips in wording:** "Multiple of 6" (p. 166) and "exactly divides each the numbers" (p. 168). The plan writes "Multiples of 6" and "each of the numbers".
17. The QR code on the opener page cannot be read from the supplied file; nothing is taken from it. No kit, sheet or cut-out is used in this chapter.

## SECTION C: Interactive Tool Specifications

Common to every tool (details in sections D and E): one activity at a time; Back and Next always in the same place; progress dots; Hint, Retry, Reset (and Undo where objects move); specific feedback (for example "4 rows do not use 18 tiles evenly"), never only right or wrong; the mathematical object is the largest thing on screen. Practice values the plan adds are labelled "practice values"; every other number is the book's.

### Tool 1. Hidden Number Box
- **Connected topic:** Find the Hidden Numbers (the opening topic, free first try right after the hook; the concept video follows it). Page 164.
- **Learning objective:** Students find the multipliers that fit a list of numbers that came out of a box, and see that more than one multiplier can fit.
- **Why a tool:** the idea only convinces when students try several multipliers themselves and see which ones give whole numbers for every number that came out.
- **Environment:** the stone clearing at the start of the cobbled road in Mowgli's jungle; Robby the rabbit and Deeku the deer watch the box.
- **Objects:** a cardboard box with an in arrow and an out arrow; four large number tags above the out arrow (28, 36, 48, 72 as in the book); a multiplier dial (1 to 12); an inside tray with four slots; a shelf for the multipliers that fit.
- **Controls:** plus and minus on the dial; Run backwards; Keep it; Put in (type a number, the box sends it out multiplied); answer cards.
- **What the student does:** The hook's box with 28, 36, 48, 72 flying out; one line: "The box multiplied each number that went in by a secret number. Try a multiplier." (1) the box gives out 10, 15, 20, 35 (practice values): which of 1, 2, 5 fit; (2) multiplier 5, put in 2, 3, 4, 7; (3) 18, 30, 42: find every multiplier; (4) 21, 28, 35: 3 does not fit, 7 does; (5) multiplier 6, put in 3, 4, 8 and say what you notice. "Can you guess the multiplier if you see the 4 numbers coming out of the box?", "Is there more than one possible multiplier?", "What numbers might have been put inside the box?" with 28, 36, 48, 72. Share your thoughts in the class.
- **Feedback and hints:** "28 ÷ 3 is not a whole number, so 3 cannot be the multiplier." Hints: what does the box do; try a multiplier and run backwards; try 2 then 4; bottom-out runs 4.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the dial and the tray of this card; Reset returns the card's first numbers.

### Tool 2. Array Maker
- **Connected topic:** Arrays Show Factors (with the Let Us Do on arrays). Pages 164, 165.
- **Learning objective:** Students make every array of a number with square tiles, write each as a multiplication, and collect the factors.
- **Why a tool:** trying 1 row, 2 rows, 3 rows and seeing which fill evenly makes "factor" a thing they do, and makes numbers with one array (prime numbers) stand out.
- **Environment:** a smooth stone terrace with a tile bag and a tile board.
- **Objects:** square tiles; a tile board where tiles snap into rows; a rows control; a factor shelf with factor chips; multiplication cards.
- **Controls:** plus and minus on rows; Save array; Turn round; Next number; answer cards.
- **What the student does:** The video's 3 rows of 5 tiles, 3 × 5 = 15; "Choose a number of rows and watch the tiles." Practice values: 8 tiles (1 × 8, 2 × 4); 9 tiles (1 × 9, 3 × 3, the factor 3 shows once); 11 tiles (only 1 × 11); find three numbers up to 30 with only one array. "Are there other numbers that are factors of 15? Try to make other arrays for the number 15."; "Make different arrays for the following numbers. Identify the factors in each case." (a) 10 to (i) 54; "Numbers like 13 and 37 are called prime numbers. Why?"
- **Feedback and hints:** "4 rows of 4 is 16 tiles and 5 rows of 4 is 20 tiles: 18 tiles do not fill 4 rows evenly." Hints: what does an array need; start with 1 row and add one; for 15 try 1 to 5 rows; bottom-out shows 1 × 15 and 3 × 5.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the board; Reset clears the shelf.

### Tool 3. Jump Together
- **Connected topic:** Common Multiples (with the Let Us Do on common multiples). Pages 165 to 167.
- **Learning objective:** Students find the stones two animals both land on, name them common multiples, and name the first common multiple.
- **Why a tool:** choosing the jump sizes and watching both animals land shows the common multiples appear, and how far apart they are.
- **Environment:** stepping stones across a jungle stream from 0 to 60 (and the winding cobbled road 0 to 64 for the book's question); Robby (blue) and Deeku (orange).
- **Objects:** the stone line; two jump dials; the stones' marks (blue ring, orange diamond, both gold); three lists (Robby lands on, Deeku lands on, Both land on).
- **Controls:** the two dials; Jump; Run; Lists; tap a gold stone to mark the first common multiple; Road.
- **What the student does:** Robby and Deeku from the video, each jumping from 0; "Set the jump sizes and press Jump." Practice values: 2 and 3; 5 and 10; 4 and 10; 6 and 9 (say the first common multiple before running). "12 is the first common multiple of 3 and 4. What are some other common multiples of 3 and 4? ..."; "Let us write the multiples of two numbers, 4 and 6. ... List a few more."; "Find 5 common multiples of the following pairs of numbers." (nine pairs); "Food is available at the end of a cobbled road. ... Will both Robby and Deeku reach the food? Who will reach first? How do you know? Explain your answer."
- **Feedback and hints:** "Both land on 12. It is a common multiple of 3 and 4." Hints: what does each animal do; find the stones where both land; list 3, 6, 9, 12 and 4, 8, 12; bottom-out shows 12, 24, 36.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the hops; Reset returns the jump sizes.

### Tool 4. Trail Jumper
- **Connected topic:** Common Factors (with the Let Us Do on jumps and factors). Pages 167 to 169.
- **Learning objective:** Students find which places a jump size lands on, and find the jump sizes that reach every number of a list (common factors).
- **Why a tool:** the idea "a jump size lands on a number only when it is a factor of it" is seen on the trail; picking numbers turns it into the common factors.
- **Environment:** Mowgli's winding trail of 61 stones from 0 to 60 with the book's friends at their places.
- **Objects:** the trail; Mowgli; the eleven friends (ant 4, spider 9, frog 12, bird 14, Kaa 21, Sher Khan 25, Baloo 30, Akela 35, deer 39, rabbit 50, monkey 57); a jump dial (1 to 30); target markers; a common factors board.
- **Controls:** the dial; Go; Pick numbers; Clear targets.
- **What the student does:** Mowgli's jumps from the video landing on 4, 8, 12; "Set a jump size and press Go." Practice values: jump 6 (frog and Baloo); jump 7 (bird, Kaa, Akela); pick 20 and 30 (jump sizes 1, 2, 5, 10); pick 15 and 16 (only 1). "Which of his friends will he be able to visit, if he jumps by 2 steps starting from 0?"; the jumps of 3, 5 and 10 lines; "Let us find some common factors of the numbers 24 and 36. ..." (a) to (f); "What are the common factors of 12 and 13?"; "Find which of the following numbers can be reached by jumps of 4 steps?"; "Find the common factors of the following pairs of numbers." (nine pairs).
- **Feedback and hints:** "4 does not land on 30. He lands on 28 and then on 32." Hints: what does a jump of this size do; is the number in the list of landings; try 2, 3 and 4 for 24 and 36; bottom-out shows the three landings.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the footprints and targets; Reset returns the trail.

### Tool 5. Number Sorter
- **Connected topic:** Factors and Multiples at Work (the last Let Us Do, Q11). Page 170.
- **Learning objective:** Students sort numbers by whether 2, 5 and 10 are factors of them, and find that no number is divisible by 10 only.
- **Why a tool:** dropping each card in a circle after testing it shows why the circle for 10 only stays empty.
- **Environment:** a jungle pond clearing with three overlapping circles of lily rings (as in the book).
- **Objects:** three circles (2, 5, 10) and their middle; green leaf-shaped number cards (the book's fifteen); a jump tester with three lights (2, 5, 10).
- **Controls:** drag a card; tap a card to run the tester.
- **What the student does:** From the video, divisible by 2, 5 or 10 means that number is a factor; "Tap a card and read the three lights." Practice values: 14, 15, 20; 12, 25, 40, 18, 65, 100; three numbers up to 60 for the middle; try to find a card for 10 only. "Sort the following numbers into those that are (a) divisible by 2 only (b) divisible by 5 only (c) divisible by 10 only (d) divisible by 2, 5, and 10." with the fifteen cards 90, 22, 38, 30, 75, 45, 66, 78, 62, 40, 84, 56, 25, 95, 55.
- **Feedback and hints:** "A number divisible by 10 is also divisible by 2 and by 5, because 10 = 2 × 5. It goes in the middle." Hints: what does divisible by 2 mean; tap the card and read the lights; for 45 check 2, 5, 10; bottom-out places 45 in 5 only.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry returns the cards of this card; Reset returns all cards.

## SECTION D: Shared Visual Language

- **Overall illustration style:** warm, colourful, polished educational-game illustrations; 3D or isometric where the scene holds objects, soft shadows, clear shapes, clean uncluttered compositions; gentle animation. The numbers, the tiles and the jumps are always the largest, clearest thing on screen.
- **Chapter theme and environment:** "Mowgli's Jungle Trail". The cobbled road and the trail of the book are the setting: the hidden box stands on a stone clearing at the start of the road, the tiles lie on a stone terrace, the number line is a row of stepping stones across a stream, the trail winds through the jungle with the friends beside their stones, and the sorting circles float on a pond. Each tool is a station on the trail. Nothing is added that does not help the mathematics.
- **Characters:** only the book's: Mowgli, Robby the rabbit, Deeku the deer, Sher Khan the tiger, Bagheera the panther, Baloo the bear, Kaa the snake, Akela the wolf, and the unnamed ant, spider, frog, bird, deer, rabbit, monkey and grasshopper. They appear to set a situation, never to teach.
- **Colour and visual direction:** warm greens and stone tones for the background. One colour per role: the first jumper (Robby) teal, the second jumper (Deeku) amber, stones or numbers both reach reddish purple (gold in the tools), correct green, "look again" soft amber (never red).
- **Main visual objects:** square tiles in rows; number lines with every whole number marked and large; jump arcs; the numbered trail; number cards (green leaf-shaped tags); three overlapping circles.
- **Animation style:** only meaningful movement: tiles snap into rows, an animal hops from stone to stone leaving a mark, a landing both animals share glows, a card slides into its circle. No decoration that moves.
- **UI layout and navigation:** one activity per screen; instruction strip at the top (one sentence, large); the working area in the middle; Hint, Retry, Reset (and Undo) in a fixed row at the bottom; Back and Next fixed at the bottom corners; progress dots under the instruction. Same placement in all five tools.
- **Common interaction patterns:** tap to select, drag to move, a stepper for the jump size or the number of rows, a Check button that gives specific feedback, a three-step hint ladder with a worked step at the end, Retry for the step, Reset for the tool.

## SECTION E: Learning and Platform Rules

1. Videos explain; tools let the student explore, apply and get feedback. No classroom teacher is given a teaching role anywhere.
2. Every tool has one distinct mathematical purpose; no interaction is repeated across tools without a learning reason. Tools 3 and 4 both use jumps on a line, but one finds the numbers two jumpers share (common multiples) and the other finds the jump sizes that reach several numbers (common factors).
3. Each tool has three phases, in order: Recall and get ready, Play and apply, NCERT Challenge (called Challenge in the tool texts).
4. A tool card that quotes the book uses the book's own questions, with their original wording, numbers and conditions. The plan does not change them and does not invent a challenge where the book has none.
5. Preserve the book's mathematics: its box numbers (28, 36, 48, 72), its arrays (15, 12 and the nine numbers), its pairs of numbers, the cobbled road (0 to 64), the trail and its friends, and the fifteen number cards. Any number the plan adds is labelled "practice values".
6. Do not introduce ideas outside the chapter: no LCM, HCF, prime factorisation, factor trees, composite numbers or tests of divisibility by other numbers.
7. One video per book question, on its answer, plus a question card and a solution card; one video per teaching idea; the hook comes first and does not explain the chapter.
8. Answers the book does not print are worked out from the chapter's own ideas and shown as the plan's reading (section A6).
9. Text hygiene: no em or en dash; minus is the true minus sign; students never read "NCERT", "textbook", page numbers or exercise numbers; say "your maths book".
10. Instructions are short, age-appropriate, one at a time; feedback is specific; hint, retry and reset are always present.
11. One visual identity (the jungle trail) and one navigation layout across all tools.
12. Verify page numbers, figures and numbers against the book's page images before any build.

### F2. Concepts and activities with no separate tool, and why
- **Checking a statement (Q8):** reasoning in words with a few examples; a tool would only repeat sorting cards into true and false, and the concept video with its practice set is enough.
- **Q9 (hunting days) and Q10 (jump sizes on the trail):** word problems that use Tool 3 (common multiples) and Tool 4 (common factors); each is a question card with a solution video and a solution card, and a tool for each would repeat those interactions.
- **Prime numbers (p. 165 margin):** a short concept video and one of the Array Maker's challenges (numbers with only one array); no separate tool.
- **Q5, Q6, Q7:** drill questions on common factors; each is also a card of Tool 4.
- **No puzzle video:** the chapter has no puzzle or investigation outside the questions above.
- **Chapter Revision:** a text summary with short checks (pipeline rule, see section B note).
- **The rule statements** (the product of factors gives a multiple, the number itself is a multiple of itself, 1 and the number itself are always factors, the definition of a common factor): stated in the concept videos and shown in the tools.
