Source: Maths Mela, Textbook of Mathematics for Grade 5, Chapter 10 (`textbooks/class-5/eemm110.pdf`, 6 PDF pages = printed pages 136 to 141; the opener page carries no printed number, it is page 136 by sequence because the next page prints 137).

## SECTION A: Chapter Understanding

### A1. Chapter name
Symmetrical Designs (Class 5, Maths Mela, Chapter 10).

### A2. Main learning objectives
Students will be able to:
1. Use a line of symmetry to cut a letter or a picture from folded paper by drawing only half (1/2) or a quarter (1/4) of it, and say which letters have a horizontal line, a vertical line or both.
2. Describe a quarter turn (1/4), a half turn (1/2), a three-quarter turn (3/4) and a full turn, and say whether a shape, letter or windmill firki looks the same after each of them.
3. Say that a shape has rotational symmetry when it looks the same after a turn smaller than a full turn, and name that turn.
4. Tell apart reflection symmetry (lines of symmetry) and rotational symmetry (turns) in letters, digits and numbers, and decide whether a shape has one, both or neither.
5. Make and change designs from squares, triangles and circles so that they look the same after a 1/2 turn or a 1/4 turn, colour a square for a quarter-turn design, and find the lines of symmetry of a design.
6. Read a wooden-block print: match a block to its half print through the line of symmetry, and say after which turn a print made by stamping a block 4 times looks the same.
7. Test shapes from the page border for reflection symmetry and for rotational symmetry, and make a symmetrical pattern with vegetable blocks (project).

### A5. Natural learning sequence
Fold a paper and cut a letter (reflection) -> which letters have horizontal, vertical or both lines -> turns and the windmill firki -> rotational symmetry, the table of letters -> telling reflection from rotation (digits and numbers) -> making designs that look the same after a turn (and their lines of symmetry) -> block printing (half print and line of symmetry, a block stamped many times) -> border shapes and project -> revision. This is the book's own order. Two small moves: (1) the border-shape question and the project, which close the book, go with block printing, as in the book; (2) the idea of "reflection only, turn only, or both" is taught before the digits, because the digits question needs it.

### A6. Points the textbook leaves unclear (to confirm; the plan does not guess)
1. **The cut-outs (resolved, nothing assumed).** The book says squares and equilateral triangles with the same side length "are provided at the end of the book". The sheet is supplied: "Tessellation and Shapes" (the 15th PDF page of the Class 5 Chapter 15 file) holds 6 squares (light blue, side 38 on the sheet) and 6 equilateral triangles (pink, base 38, height 33, the same side as the squares), and also 6 five-sided shapes, 6 six-sided shapes, 6 circles and two cut-out puzzles that this chapter does not use. The plan uses only the squares and the triangles, at most 6 of each; the sample designs use 1 square and 4 triangles at most.
2. **The triangles in the chapter's pictures are not equilateral.** In the pictures on pp. 139 and 140 each triangle is as tall as it is wide (height 48 on a base of 48, so the other two sides are 54), while an equilateral triangle on that base would be 42 tall; the triangles on the sheet are equilateral (base 38, height 33). The plan follows the words and the sheet; the symmetry answers do not change either way.
3. **The digit shapes.** The book draws the digit 1 as a plain straight bar, and draws 3 and 8 with a slightly smaller top. The plan reads 1 as the bar (so it has both a vertical and a horizontal line and looks the same after a 1/2 turn) and reads 3 and 8 as having two equal halves, as a hand-drawn digit does. Answers adopted: reflection symmetry 0, 1, 3, 8; rotational symmetry 0, 1, 8; both 0, 1, 8. 6 and 9 turn into each other but neither looks the same by itself after a 1/2 turn. Measured on the printed digits (a fold of the picture on itself, 100% is a perfect match): 0 matches on both lines (99%); 8 matches on the vertical line (97%) and on the horizontal line (87%, its top loop is smaller); 3 matches on a horizontal line only (67%, its top bowl is smaller). So the plan's reading of 3 and 8 is the usual hand-drawn reading, not an exact match of the typeface. In another typeface (a 1 with a flag, a small-top 8) the answers would change.
4. **"How many times does this shape look the same during a full turn?"** (p. 139). The plan counts the quarter turn, the half turn, the three-quarter turn and the full turn: 4 times. If the book means only the turns before the full turn, the answer is 3.
5. **Which colourings work** (Let Us Think, p. 139). Using two colours on the 8 triangles, a colouring that looks the same after every 1/4 turn must alternate the colours round the centre (only 2 such colourings, one is the other with the colours swapped). Such a design has NO line of symmetry (every dashed line would put one colour on the other). The plan answers the book's follow-up "Do these designs have reflection symmetry also?" for both designs of that part: the green and pink square has 2 lines (its two diagonals), the alternating square has none.
6. **Making Designs (b), (c) and Let Us Do on squares and triangles are open.** The book prints no answer and no wording of the target design; the plan gives one sample design for each and says the student's may differ.
7. **The turn direction is not stated.** The plan turns the shape to the right, the same way each time, and never writes "clockwise".
8. **The border shapes (p. 141) are not numbered.** The plan numbers them 1 to 19, going round the border clockwise from the top-left corner (top row left to right 1 to 5, right side top to bottom 6 to 10, bottom right to left 11 to 14, left side bottom to top 15 to 19). Each was measured on the page's drawing. Some shapes have 3, 5, 6 or 7 matching turns, so their turns are 1/3, 1/5, 1/6 and 1/7 of a full turn, which the chapter has not named (it names only 1/4, 1/2, 3/4); the plan uses these turn names only in the answer to this one question. Two shapes are drawn a little unevenly: shape 4 (three circles joined by a Y: one leg is about 20% shorter) and shape 9 (a ring of ten small triangles that is meant to be two twisted pentagons: its corners are not exactly even). The plan reads both as the even design they are drawn to be (both symmetries). Shape 3 (the yellow six-sided shape) is a little stretched (its sides are 22 and 25 long, not equal), so it has only a vertical and a horizontal line and a half turn; the plan says only that, which is true for an even six-sided shape too. Please confirm.
9. **Which shapes are symmetrical on the border** (answers the book does not print). The plan's reading: reflection symmetry in all shapes except 13 (the slanted four-sided shape, a parallelogram) and 15 (the thin right-angled triangle); rotational symmetry in all shapes except 15. So only shape 13 has rotational symmetry alone, and only shape 15 has neither.
10. **The matching of blocks to prints (p. 140)** prints only the line (iv) to (e). The plan works out the rest from the pictures: (i) to (c), (ii) to (d), (iii) to (a), (v) to (b). Each print shows half of its block along the block's line of symmetry.
11. **Design B (p. 141)** has dashed lines drawn for a vertical and a horizontal line of symmetry. The plan answers "1/4 turn" and "both rotational and reflection symmetry". The book's design A statement is kept as a printed fact (text), not as a question.
12. **Letter answers depend on the typeface.** The plan reads the letters as plain sans-serif capitals as the book prints them: E and K have a horizontal line, X and O have both lines, T and V have a vertical line, N has no line (it looks the same after a 1/2 turn). The seven letters look chosen to cover every case (two horizontal, two vertical, two both, one neither). Measured on the printed letters, K matches itself on a horizontal fold only roughly (60%, its lower leg is longer than its upper arm); the plan reads it as symmetric, as the book's choice of letters suggests. The book prints no answer for the opener questions.
13. **The QR code on the opener page** is not readable from the supplied file; nothing is taken from it.
14. **Printed words.** The book writes "Put a (✓) mark"; the plan writes "Put a tick mark" (a symbol the platform text may not show). In the question cards the book's "digit(s)", "shape(s)" and "line(s)" are written "digits", "shapes" and "lines", "2-, 3-, and 4-digit" is written "2-digit, 3-digit and 4-digit", and the printed options (a), (b), (c) of the question on the numbers 11 and 1001 are written in the sentence (the LaTeX converter turns the printed forms into nonsense). The wording is otherwise the book's. The word firki is kept as the book spells it.

## SECTION C: Interactive Tool Specifications

Common to every tool (details in sections D and E): one activity at a time; Back and Next always in the same place; progress dots; Hint, Retry, Reset, and (where moving objects) Undo; specific feedback ("The halves do not match: fold along the other line"), never only right or wrong; the mathematical object is the largest thing on screen. Practice values the plan adds are labelled "practice values"; every other number, letter and picture is the book's.

### Tool 1. Fold and Cut
- **Connected topic:** Happy Birthday Cutouts (the opening topic, free first try right after the hook; the concept video follows it). Page 136.
- **Learning objective:** Students find that a letter with a line of symmetry can be cut from folded paper by drawing only half (one fold) or a quarter (two folds) of it, and that a fold on a line that is not a line of symmetry gives a different cutout.
- **Why a tool:** the idea only convinces when students choose the fold themselves and see the unfolded cutout match or fail to match the letter.
- **Environment:** Lali's craft table with the birthday wall banner behind it; coloured paper, scissors, a pencil.
- **Objects:** a large square sheet of paper with a vertical and a horizontal dashed fold line; a shelf of letter cards; a part-to-draw outline that the tool draws on the folded paper; scissors; an "Open" button; a results sheet of ticks.
- **Controls:** tap a letter card; tap a fold (Vertical, Horizontal, or Both for one-fourth); tap Draw to see the part along the fold; tap Cut; tap Open to unfold.
- **What the student does:** The hook's picture, Prem and Manu's folded paper with half of the letter A drawn along the fold, and one line: "Tap the line where the paper is folded." (1) cut the letter A with the vertical fold (half); (2) cut the letter H with both folds (a quarter); (3) cut M with the horizontal fold first (wrong, the opened cutout does not match) and then the vertical fold (practice values); (4) cut B with the horizontal fold (practice values); (5) try F with every fold: none works (practice values). The book's questions unchanged: "Which of the following alphabet cutouts can be made by just drawing half (1/2) or quarter (1/4) of the letter? ... E N X T K V O" and "Which of the letters have a horizontal line of symmetry? Which of the letters have a vertical line of symmetry? Which letters have both vertical and horizontal lines of symmetry?" Each answer is built by cutting the letters and ticking the results sheet.
- **Feedback and hints:** wrong fold: "The two halves do not match when you open the paper, so this fold is not a line of symmetry." Hints: does the folded half land on the other half; try the vertical fold, then the horizontal; for this letter, which side matches; bottom-out shows the correct fold and the part to draw.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry unfolds the paper for this card; Reset clears the results sheet.

### Tool 2. Turn and Check
- **Connected topic:** Turns and the Windmill Firki (rotational symmetry). Pages 137 to 138.
- **Learning objective:** Students turn a shape by 1/4, 1/2, 3/4 and a full turn, say whether it looks the same after each turn, and name the turn at which a shape first looks the same.
- **Why a tool:** the idea is a movement; turning the shape on a pin and comparing it with its starting outline is the idea.
- **Environment:** Lali's craft table: a windmill firki on a stick and a round turntable with a pin at the centre, like the nail or tracing-paper method of the book.
- **Objects:** a firki with four blades and a dot on one blade; a turntable disc that holds a letter card; a dashed outline of the starting position; a turn dial with four stops (1/4, 1/2, 3/4, Full); a table row "looks the same after" for each stop with Yes and No cards, and a "Rotational symmetry" Yes or No box with the turn.
- **Controls:** tap a stop on the dial (the shape turns smoothly the same way each time); tap Looks the same or Looks different for each turn; choose the rotational symmetry answer.
- **What the student does:** The firki of the story with its dot, "Turn the firki a quarter of the way round. Where is the dot now?" (1) a four-blade pinwheel with a dot (practice values): turn it through the four stops and see where the dot goes; (2) the letter Z (practice values): same after a 1/2 turn only; (3) the letter T (practice values): never the same; (4) the plus sign (practice values): same after every quarter turn. The book's items unchanged: "Observe the dot in the firki. Does the firki look the same after 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, and a full turn?" and the table of letters (H solved as the example; then I, X, Y) with "Rotational symmetry (Yes/No)".
- **Feedback and hints:** "The dot moved, but the firki looks the same, because its four blades are equal." Hints: put the shape on its starting outline; turn a quarter and compare; check the half turn next; bottom-out turns the shape to the stop that works and shows it on the outline.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry turns the shape back to the start; Reset clears the table.

### Tool 3. Symmetry Sorter
- **Connected topic:** Reflection Only, Turn Only or Both (letters, digits and numbers). Page 138.
- **Learning objective:** Students test a letter, digit or number with a fold line and with a turn, and sort it into reflection only, turn only, both, or neither.
- **Why a tool:** the point is that two different tests give two different answers; running both on many cards and sorting makes the difference stick.
- **Environment:** Lali's craft table: number and letter cards on a wooden board; four trays.
- **Objects:** a test board with a card; a vertical and a horizontal dashed fold line that can be switched on and that fold one half over the other; the same turn dial as Tool 2; four trays: Reflection only, Turn only, Both, Neither; a results strip under each card.
- **Controls:** tap a card to bring it to the test board; tap Fold on the vertical line, Fold on the horizontal line (the halves are shown to match or not); tap a turn stop; drag the card into a tray.
- **What the student does:** A card with the letter M from the video: "Does M pass the fold test? Does it pass the turn test?" Sort the letters C, Z, H and F (practice values), one for each tray; then sort U, S, the plus sign and P (practice values) together. The book's items unchanged: "Find symmetry in the digits" 1 to 0 (which have reflection symmetry, rotational symmetry, both); the numbers 11 and 1001; then make 2-, 3- and 4-digit numbers with rotational symmetry, reflection symmetry or both.
- **Feedback and hints:** "6 turns into 9, so 6 does not look the same after a half turn." Hints: run the fold test; run the turn test; compare what each test says; bottom-out places the card in its tray with the two test results.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry returns the card to the board; Reset empties the trays.

### Tool 4. Design Studio
- **Connected topic:** Making Designs that Turn. Pages 139 to 140.
- **Learning objective:** Students add and change shapes so that a design looks the same after a 1/2 turn and after a 1/4 turn, colour a square for a quarter-turn design, and find the lines of symmetry of the result.
- **Why a tool:** a design is made by placing and copying parts; seeing where each part lands after a turn shows which part is missing.
- **Environment:** Lali's craft table: a round design board with a centre pin and four faint guide marks (top, right, bottom, left).
- **Objects:** light blue squares and pink equilateral triangles with the same side, 6 of each as on the book's sheet, and circles for the book's design, in a tray; colour pots; a turn dial (1/4, 1/2, 3/4); ghost copies that show where each part lands after the chosen turn (a red outline marks a part with no partner); a fold line tool (vertical, horizontal, two diagonals) that shows whether the halves match, colours included.
- **Controls:** drag a shape onto the board; tap a shape to turn it a quarter; tap a colour pot then a part to colour it; pick a turn on the dial to see the ghosts; pick a fold line; Undo.
- **What the student does:** A circle with one triangle above it (practice values): "Where will the triangle land after a half turn?" (1) add shapes so the circle and triangle look the same after a 1/2 turn; (2) add more for a 1/4 turn; (3) colour a two-square strip orange and blue and see that the colours decide the answer (practice values); (4) test a square cut by its diagonals, top and bottom orange, left and right blue, with turns and fold lines (practice values). The book's items unchanged: Making Designs (a) to (d); Let Us Think (the green and pink square, the square to colour, the reflection question); the squares and triangles (make designs, the four-square shape, sort in 3 categories).
- **Feedback and hints:** "After a 1/2 turn the circle on the right lands on the left, where nothing is. Add a circle there." Hints: where does each part land; which place has no partner; add a copy of the part in that place; bottom-out adds the missing part.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the last design of this card; Reset empties the board.

## SECTION D: Shared Visual Language

- **Overall illustration style:** warm, colourful, polished educational-game illustrations; 3D or isometric where the scene holds objects, soft shadows, clear shapes, clean uncluttered compositions; gentle animation. The shape being tested is always the largest, clearest thing on screen.
- **Chapter theme and environment:** "Lali's Craft Table". The book's own objects sit on one friendly Indian home craft table: coloured paper and scissors for the cutouts (the Happy Birthday wall banner behind), the windmill firki on its stick, wooden printing blocks and their prints, and the letters and digits as cards. Each tool is a station on the table: fold-and-cut paper, turntable, sorting trays, design board. Nothing is added that does not help the mathematics.
- **Characters:** only the book's: Prem, Manu and Lali. They appear to set a situation, never to teach.
- **Colour and visual direction:** warm cream and wood tones for the table; the book's own colours for the shapes (blue squares, sand triangles, the coloured letters). One colour per role: the part being asked about is orange, the matching part is pale cream, the line of symmetry is a dashed dark line, a turn is a curved arrow, correct is green, "look again" is soft amber (never red).
- **Main visual objects:** a folded sheet with its dashed fold line; letters and digits large and plain; a firki with a dot; a turn dial with 1/4, 1/2, 3/4 and Full marked; designs made of squares, triangles and circles; wooden blocks beside their prints.
- **Animation style:** only meaningful movement: the paper folds and opens, the shape turns on its pin to the chosen stop, the halves slide over each other to compare. No decoration that moves.
- **UI layout and navigation:** one activity per screen; instruction strip at the top (one sentence, large); the working area in the middle; Hint, Retry, Reset (and Undo) in a fixed row at the bottom; Back and Next fixed at the bottom corners; progress dots under the instruction. Same placement in all four tools.
- **Common interaction patterns:** tap to select, drag to place or sort, a dial to turn, a fold-line switch to test, a Check button that gives specific feedback, a three-step hint ladder with a worked step at the end, Retry for the step, Reset for the tool.

## SECTION E: Learning and Platform Rules

1. Videos explain; tools let the student explore, apply and get feedback. No classroom teacher is given a teaching role anywhere.
2. Every tool has one distinct mathematical purpose: Tool 1 reflection by folding; Tool 2 rotation by turning; Tool 3 telling the two apart and sorting; Tool 4 making designs for a turn.
3. A tool is made from its concept description: the idea, the screen, what the student does, the practice cards, feedback and hints. It is not scripted in phases.
4. A tool card that quotes the book uses the book's own questions, with their original wording, shapes and conditions. The plan does not change them and does not invent a challenge where the book has none.
5. Preserve the book's mathematics: its letters, its digits, its designs, its turns (1/4, 1/2, 3/4, full). Any shape or number the plan adds is labelled "practice values".
6. Do not introduce ideas outside the chapter: no degrees, no angles, no order of rotation, no tessellation, no centre of rotation, no congruence. The words "line of symmetry", "reflection symmetry", "rotational symmetry", "turn" and "design" are the book's.
7. One video per book question, on its answer, plus a question card and a solution card; one video per teaching idea; the hook comes first and does not explain the chapter.
8. Answers the book does not print are worked out from the chapter's own ideas and shown as the plan's reading (section A6).
9. Text hygiene: no em or en dash; minus is the true minus sign; students never read "NCERT", "textbook", page numbers or exercise numbers; say "your maths book".
10. Instructions are short, age-appropriate, one at a time; feedback is specific; hint, retry and reset are always present.
11. One visual identity (the craft table) and one navigation layout across all tools.
12. Verify the figures against the book's page images before any build.

### F2. Concepts and activities with no separate tool, and why
- **Block printing (Let Us Explore, designs A and B, p. 140 to 141):** matching a block to its half print and reading the turn of a print are recognition tasks, taught by two concept videos with pictures of real blocks; stamping a block round a centre is the same operation as Tool 4.
- **Border shapes (p. 141):** the same two tests as Tool 3 on 19 drawn shapes; a tool would repeat Tool 3.
- **Project Work (p. 141) and the diya and boat cutouts (p. 136):** hands-on paper and vegetable activities done away from the screen; each has a question card with a sample plan.
- **Chapter Revision:** a text summary with short checks (pipeline rule, see section B note).
- **The idea box and the Alphabet Cutout steps:** stated in videos and shown in Tools 1 and 2.

### F3. Count check
