## 1. Hard rules (gates enforce most; fix content, never a gate)
- Class 5 students, 10 to 11 years old: short plain sentences. Characters and settings are the book's: Shikha, Sonu (the village weaving centre), Ritika (rods), Sheena and Jennifer (the race), Saji (the walk), Rani (ribbons), Anita (embroidery). The plan's own WE use these characters with ropes, cloth, tapes, roads, rulers, doors and walls. Do not invent facts or datasets: every number that is not a plain WE number comes from the book or from section 4. Where the plan adds a number, say "practice values" in the question text of the WE or in the sample answer.
- **No em dash and no en dash** in any string or comment (the book's dashes become commas, colons or "to"). Minus is U+2212 `−`. Use `×` and `÷`. Hyphen only inside words. Write "3 km race", never "3-km race"; write "10 year old", not "10-year old".
- **Lengths are written as the book writes them:** `3 km 450 m`, `6 m 5 cm`, `4 cm 5 mm`, `2 m 150 cm`; units `km`, `m`, `cm`, `mm` only (never `metres` or `centimetres` spelled out inside a number phrase; in sentences you may say "metres", "centimetres", "kilometre", "millimetre"). Thousands use a comma as in the book: `1,000 m`, `4,187 km`, `10,552 m`, `2,340 mm` (but the book also prints `1600 cm` and `4625 cm`, copy those as printed only in `textbook=[...]`). Money is `₹100`, a blank amount is `₹___`. Heights in feet and inches are written in words: `4 feet 6 inches`, `1 foot`, `1 inch` (never `ft` or `in`, they convert badly). **No decimals anywhere** (2.5 cm is `2 cm 5 mm`) and no fractions of a unit, except the single phrase "half a metre" in the note on the 50 cm blank.
- **Part labels and blanks:** write each part as `(a): ...` with a colon, and separate parts with a semicolon (`(a): 45 mm; (b): ...`). Never put units in brackets (`(cm)`), the converter reads it as a part label. A blank is `___` (three underscores), always followed by its unit or a comma or semicolon (`___ mm`, `₹___,`). A blank comparison is `456 cm ___ 5 m`. The list of signs is written `(+, −, ×, ÷)` in the plain text.
- Never write "NCERT", "textbook", "page N", "Fig.", "Figure N" or exercise numbers in anything the student reads (names, teach lines, steps, questions, tool text, image text). Say "your maths book". Exception: the `textbook=[...]` argument of `A()` quotes the book verbatim (stored as a `textbook_text` block), still no dashes.
- **Arithmetic must be true in every string.** The equation gate `eqcheck_plain` checks only whole-number chains `a op b = c` with a literal `=` (commas in numbers are read) and SKIPS any chain that touches a unit word between the numbers (`20 × 80 cm = 1,600 cm` is read as `20 × 80` and not judged). So every length fact, conversion, comparison and sum is NOT machine checked: re-compute all of them with Python before you finish (write a small script; keep it in the scratchpad). Keep whole-number chains on one line, never write a chain that is only approximately true.
- 100% coverage: every id in `content.NCERT_ITEMS` (section 3) is attached to a concept: a `text` or `talk` id through `A(..., ncert=[...])`, a question id as an `FIO` item of the DOER of its topic.
- **One video per teaching concept.** A teaching concept `A()` has exactly ONE `WE` in `examples` (that WE is the concept video) with the PLAN's own numbers (practice values): never the number set of a book question. The hook concept has `video_focus=["HOOK"]`, `enrichment=True`, no WE. DOER concepts carry a recap, their question items and at most one tool, no WE.
- **Questions.** Every book question is a `FIO(cov, label, question, steps, answer, kind=..., q_figure=..., s_figure=...)` in the DOER of its topic, in the book's order; `core/qt.py` moves each into its own concept of the "Questions: ..." topic that follows. `question` is the wording given in section 3 (the book's, with the changes listed there). `steps` = the numbered working, one operation per line, values before combining, a check as the last step (at most 8 lines per solution card; longer working is cut into two cards by the pipeline, which is fine). `answer` = short final answer (see the length rule below). Open-ended items get a labelled "Sample answer" built by reasoning and say "Your answer may differ". `q_figure` is ONE plain sentence of ONLY what the question gives (never the answer or a hint), `s_figure` one sentence for the solution card (it may show the answer).
- **The answer is cut at 220 characters of its LaTeX form** (`answer_tex`). Units cost about 12 characters each (`\text{ cm}`), so a multi-part answer with units can pass 220. Put the full working in `steps` and keep `answer` to the final values; if it is too long, drop the units from the answer only where every value shares one unit that the question names, or shorten the wording; do not drop the "Sample answer" and "Your answer may differ" labels. Run `measure_answers.py` before you finish: no item may print TOO LONG.
- **Labels (`book`) decide the Questions topic** (`core/question_naming.py`): a label starting `Let Us Do` goes to "Questions: Let us do"; a label starting `Example N` goes to "Questions: Example" (use exactly the numbers in section 3); any other label goes to a topic named by the text before its FIRST colon, so a label like "Let Us Compare: Q2 (a) difference" gives the topic "Questions: Let Us Compare". Use exactly the labels in section 3.
- Names: DOER name starts with `Practice: ` or `Pull it together and do it: `.
- An idea topic has 2 to 4 primary concepts INCLUDING its DOER (last). The intro T1 is `topic_type="framing"` with `""` objective, all concepts `enrichment=True`: C1 hook (`video_focus=["HOOK"]`) and C3 a DOER with `role="try_it"` and the free-try tool (the framing topic holds exactly ONE video, the hook, and no book question). Say "number lines side by side" or "the two linked lines" instead of "double number line" in an objective. Topic objective on each idea topic (also without a tool title), none on framing and summary. Bloom words: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze.
- Misconception ids come from `content.MISCONCEPTIONS` (section 6); attach the relevant ones to teach concepts, DOERs and tools.
- `teach` lines: the first line is a prediction question ("Predict first: ..."), then the reveal, then the rule in one line. `minutes` 4 to 8.
- Tool specs (`T(...)`): section 5. Exactly 5 to 7 challenges, exactly 4 hints.
- Do not use words outside the chapter (`book_rules.json` banned_words): no decimals, perimeter, area, volume, weight, capacity, speed, litre, gram, mile, yard, fraction words, percent. Do not introduce concepts the chapter does not teach.

### Teaching concept seeds (the WE is the plan's own, with practice values; check each against section 3 so no WE repeats a book question's numbers)
- **T1.C1 hook (Shikha and Sonu at the weaving centre).** No WE, no explanation. Shikha and Sonu measure saris and stoles. Shikha's tape has centimetres, Sonu's tape has metres; they call out different numbers for the same sari. Ask: which tape for a handkerchief, a sari, the height of India Gate? Ends on an open question that leads to the tool. `ncert=["open.intro","sari.story"]`. Say nothing of 100 cm = 1 m.
- **T1B.C1 choosing a unit:** Shikha measures a pencil and Sonu a classroom wall (practice values: the pencil is 17 long, the wall is 8 long). Pencil: fits in a hand, so cm: 17 cm. Wall: longer than you, so m: 8 m. Check: 17 m would be longer than a classroom, 8 cm shorter than the pencil. Rule: small things in cm, long things in m, so the number sounds sensible. Misconception M_UNIT_ANY.
- **T1B.C2 one length, two names:** Sonu measures a stole as 3 m: in cm that is 3 × 100 = 300 cm. Shikha measures another stole as 450 cm: 450 cm = 400 cm + 50 cm = 4 m 50 cm (400 ÷ 100 = 4). Rules: 1 m = 100 cm; m to cm multiply by 100; cm to m and cm take out the hundreds; a part larger than 100 cm is allowed, 3 m 130 cm = 300 cm + 130 cm = 430 cm. Show the double number line (top m, bottom cm, arrow ×100). Misconceptions M_M_TEN_CM, M_JOIN_NUMBERS.
- **T2.C1 comparing in different units:** compare 7 m 9 cm and 790 cm: 7 m 9 cm = 7 × 100 + 9 = 709 cm; 709 < 790, so 7 m 9 cm < 790 cm. 9 cm is not 90 cm. Rule: change both lengths to the same unit (the smaller one), then compare the numbers. Misconceptions M_BIGGER_NUMBER, M_JOIN_NUMBERS.
- **T2.C2 the difference:** Sonu's stole is 285 cm and Shikha's is 2 m 60 cm. 2 m 60 cm = 260 cm; 285 − 260 = 25 cm: Sonu's stole is longer by 25 cm. Doubling: a rope of 90 cm doubled is 90 × 2 = 180 cm; to find the length that doubles to 180 cm, halve it: 180 ÷ 2 = 90 cm. Rule: difference = longer − shorter in the same unit; doubled means × 2 and the piece is half.
- **T3.C1 the kilometre:** a path is 7 km long: 7 × 1,000 = 7,000 m. A road is 5,400 m long: 5,400 m = 5,000 m + 400 m = 5 km 400 m. Rule: kilo stands for thousand, 1 km = 1,000 m, used for long distances. Name the four tools the book shows: long tape, rodometer (measuring wheel), rangefinder, laser distance meter (names only). `ncert=["km.tools","km.def"]`. Misconception M_KM_HUNDRED.
- **T3.C2 equal pieces along a distance:** a cycle track is 2 km long and a flag stands after every 400 m. 2 km = 2,000 m; 2,000 ÷ 400 = 5 flags, at 400 m, 800 m, 1,200 m, 1,600 m and 2,000 m (the last at the end). Rule: the number of equal pieces = the distance ÷ the length of one piece; they stand at 1, 2, 3 ... times the piece length. Misconception M_STOP_COUNT.
- **T4.C1 distance between two stations:** Station P is 125 km, Station Q 340 km and Station R 615 km from the first station of the route (practice values). Q to R: 615 − 340 = 275 km. The whole route up to R is 615 km. Rule: distances are measured from the same start, so the distance between two stations is the difference, not the sum. Misconception M_DIST_SUM.
- **T5.C1 the millimetre:** Sonu's pin is 3 cm long: 3 × 10 = 30 mm. A bead is 8 mm wide: 8 < 10, so it is less than 1 cm. Rule: 1 cm is cut into 10 equal parts, each 1 mm, with 9 small marks between two cm marks; 1 cm = 10 mm. `ncert=["mm.def","small.how"]` (the talk: how to measure small things like a sprout, small screws, a nail: with the small marks). Misconception M_CM_HUNDRED_MM.
- **T5.C2 reading and drawing:** a line ends on the 6th small mark after 4 cm: 4 cm 6 mm = 4 × 10 + 6 = 46 mm. To draw 72 mm: 72 = 70 + 2 = 7 cm 2 mm, go to 7 cm then 2 small marks. Explain the book's "how did you draw it?" with fresh numbers (52 mm is 5 cm 2 mm): split the mm into tens (cm) and ones (mm). `ncert=["draw.how"]`.
- **T6.C1 changing units:** 6 cm = 6 × 10 = 60 mm; 9 m = 9 × 100 = 900 cm; 8 km = 8 × 1,000 = 8,000 m. The other way, divide: 4,000 m = 4,000 ÷ 1,000 = 4 km. On a double number line the bigger unit is on the top line, the smaller on the bottom; going down multiply, going up divide. Rule: 10 mm = 1 cm, 100 cm = 1 m, 1,000 m = 1 km. `ncert=["rel.units"]`. Misconceptions M_M_TEN_CM, M_KM_HUNDRED, M_CM_HUNDRED_MM.
- **T6.C2 splitting a length:** 7,305 cm = 7,300 cm + 5 cm = 73 m 5 cm (7,300 ÷ 100 = 73); 3 m 7 cm = 300 cm + 7 cm = 307 cm. Rule: take out the part that is a whole number of the bigger unit, what is left stays in the smaller unit. `ncert=["conv.ex_mm","conv.ex_cm"]` (the book's own printed cases 89 mm = 80 mm + 9 mm = 8 cm 9 mm and 5,792 cm = 5,700 cm + 92 cm = 57 m 92 cm are quoted in `textbook=[...]` and used in the teach lines, the WE keeps its own numbers). Misconception M_JOIN_NUMBERS.
- **T7.C1 adding lengths:** Sonu walked 2 km 700 m to the market and 1 km 800 m back by another road. Like units: 2 km + 1 km = 3 km; 700 m + 800 m = 1,500 m = 1 km 500 m; 3 km + 1 km 500 m = 4 km 500 m. Or change to metres: 2,700 + 1,800 = 4,500 m = 4 km 500 m. The plan's own line (from the book's note, no teacher role): when the numbers are easy, like 200, 250, 400, 500 or 750, work in your head; otherwise use a column and regroup; lengths add like numbers. `ncert=["addsub.note"]`. Misconception M_ADD_ALL.
- **T7.C2 subtracting lengths:** Shikha has a ribbon of 9 m and cuts off 3 m 40 cm. 9 m = 900 cm; 3 m 40 cm = 340 cm; 900 − 340 = 560 cm = 5 m 60 cm. Or regroup: 9 m = 8 m 100 cm, then 8 m 100 cm − 3 m 40 cm = 5 m 60 cm. Check: 340 + 560 = 900. Misconception M_BORROW_TEN.
- **T8.C1 multiplying a length:** Rani sews curtains and each needs 2 m 40 cm of cloth. For 12 curtains: 12 × 2 m = 24 m; 12 × 40 cm = 480 cm = 4 m 80 cm; 24 m + 4 m 80 cm = 28 m 80 cm. Check: 12 × 240 = 2,880 cm = 28 m 80 cm. Rule (the book's margin note, `ncert=["md.method"]`): break the length into m and cm and multiply each, or change it to cm and multiply. Misconception M_MULT_PART.
- **T8.C2 dividing a length:** a pipe line of 18 km 900 m is laid in 3 equal days (practice values): 18 km ÷ 3 = 6 km; 900 m ÷ 3 = 300 m; each day 6 km 300 m. Or 18,900 m ÷ 3 = 6,300 m. Check: 3 × 6 km 300 m = 18 km 900 m.
- **T8.C3 price of a length:** a shop sells ribbon at ₹90 for 3 m. 1 m costs 90 ÷ 3 = ₹30. On a double number line, 3 m is over ₹90 and 9 m over ₹270 (multiply both lines by 3). ₹300 buys 300 ÷ 30 = 10 m. Check: 10 × 30 = 300. Rule: price of 1 m = the price ÷ the number of metres; then multiply for any length.
- **T9.C1 estimating with a reference:** Sonu wants the height of a wall; a door beside it is about 2 m high (practice value) and the wall is as high as 2 doors: 2 × 2 m = 4 m, about 4 m. Use the book's two pictures in the teach lines: children standing on each other's shoulders to reach a roof, a ladder against a pole: each person or ladder is a reference to count. Rule: pick a reference length you know, count how many times it fits, multiply; say "about". `ncert=["est.pictures"]`. Misconception M_GUESS.
- **T10.C1 feet and inches:** Shikha's height is 4 feet 6 inches (practice value): 4 × 12 = 48; 48 + 6 = 54 inches. 30 inches = 24 + 6 = 2 feet 6 inches. Rule: 1 foot = 12 inches = 30 cm; 1 inch = 2 cm 5 mm. `ncert=["inch.def"]`. Misconception M_FOOT_TEN.
- **T11.C1 sign puzzle (a puzzle video):** a 3 by 3 grid with the signs +, − and ×, no sign repeated in a row or a column. Given: + in the top left box, × in the middle box, − in the bottom right box. Top middle: its row has + and its column has ×, so it is −. Then the top right is ×. Left of the middle row: its column has +, its row has ×, so −; right of the middle row is +. Bottom row: × then + then −. Result: row 1 + − ×; row 2 − × +; row 3 × + −. Rule: fill the box with only one choice left; use the row and the column. Misconception M_SIGN_REPEAT.
- **T12.C1 summary:** one idea from each stop with a short check (unit that fits; m and cm; compare and difference; kilometre; equal pieces; stations; millimetre; changing units; adding and subtracting; multiplying and dividing; price; estimating; feet and inches; the sign puzzle), numbers chosen from the plan's own values, each check true.
