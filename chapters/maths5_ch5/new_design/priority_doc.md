## SECTION A: Chapter Understanding

### A1. Chapter name
Far and Near (Class 5, Maths Mela, Chapter 5).

### A2. Main learning objectives
Students will be able to:
1. Choose a sensible unit, centimetre or metre, for a length, and know that one length can be written in two units (1 m = 100 cm).
2. Compare lengths given in different units by changing them to one unit, and find how much longer one is than another.
3. Use the kilometre (1 km = 1,000 m) for long distances, and find how many equal pieces or stops fit along a distance.
4. Find the distance between two stations from their distances from the start of a route.
5. Use the millimetre (1 cm = 10 mm) to read, measure and draw small lengths on a ruler.
6. Change a length between mm, cm, m and km (the multipliers 10, 100 and 1,000, shown on a double number line) and split a length into two units.
7. Add and subtract lengths given in two units (regrouping 1 km = 1,000 m and 1 m = 100 cm).
8. Multiply and divide a length given in two units, and find the cost of a length at a given price.
9. Estimate a length with a reference, and read heights in feet and inches (1 inch = 2 cm 5 mm, 1 foot = 12 inches = 30 cm).
10. Reason through a sign puzzle one clue at a time.

### A5. Natural learning sequence
Choose a unit and see one length in two units (m and cm) -> compare lengths and find differences -> the kilometre and equal stops along a long distance -> distance between two stations -> the millimetre and the small marks of a ruler -> changing units with the multipliers 10, 100, 1,000 and splitting a length into two units -> adding and subtracting lengths -> multiplying and dividing lengths and the cost of a length -> estimating -> feet and inches -> the sign puzzle -> revision. This is the book's own order. The only change: the book's "Relationships between Different Units" box and the double number lines (pp. 62 to 64) come after the millimetre and before adding, exactly as printed.

### A6. Points the textbook leaves unclear (to confirm; the plan does not guess)
1. **Page numbers.** The opener page has no printed number: it is page 57 by sequence, and the 13 PDF pages are printed pages 57 to 69.
2. **Let Us Find (p. 57).** The table's second column is headed "Unit of measurement, Metre (m) or centimetre (cm)" but holds numbers (42, 40, 50, 13, 2, 5). The plan reads each number as the measure and the student chooses the unit: India Gate 42 m, handkerchief 40 cm, well 50 m, mobile phone 13 cm, elephant's trunk 2 m, buttons 5 cm. The book prints no answers. The well is the only doubtful one: a well that is 50 cm deep is not possible, so metres.
3. **The match table (top of p. 58).** The table has no heading and no instruction of its own. The plan reads it as the task "Find which measures represent the same sari or stole": each measure in the first column matches one in the second column (not the one in its own row): 204 cm = 2 m 4 cm, 540 cm = 5 m 40 cm, 750 cm = 6 m 150 cm, 240 cm = 2 m 40 cm, 404 cm = 2 m 204 cm. Some measures use more than 100 cm after the metres (2 m 204 cm, 6 m 150 cm); the plan keeps them as printed. The double number line (blanks 200 cm, 4 m, 5 m, 7 m) is not drawn to scale.
4. **Let Us Do on measuring long distances (p. 59)** needs the student's own school, steps and home. The book gives no numbers. The plan gives a sample answer built on practice values (125 steps for 100 m, 250 steps for 200 m, a step of 80 cm; a tea shop 120 m, a school gate 400 m, a temple 1,600 m away) and says "Your answer may differ".
5. **Kilometre Race (p. 60).** The book does not say whether a stop stands at the start line or the finish line. The plan reads positions from the starting point, the first stop at the first interval, and the last at the finish line (3 km): 6 water stations (500 m, 1,000 m, 1,500 m, 2,000 m, 2,500 m, 3,000 m), 10 children (every 300 m up to 3,000 m), 60 flags, 30 red and 30 blue (the colour of the first flag is not given; red first, and the counts are the same either way). A stop at the start line would add one to each count.
6. **Longest Train Journey (pp. 60 to 61).** The route starts at Dibrugarh (0 km). The distance between two stations is the difference of their distances from Dibrugarh; the book does not say this and the plan teaches it in the concept before the questions. Q1 "total length of the route" is the distance of the last station, Kanniyakumari, 4,187 km.
7. **Let Us Measure (p. 61).** The book prints no measurements. The design has eight lines, each with an empty box beside it. The plan measured the lines on the PDF drawing at the page's printed size (1 pt = 0.3528 mm, the page being 211.7 mm wide): 48 mm, 35 mm, 60 mm, 27 mm, 22 mm, 19 mm, 44 mm and 61 mm (each within 0.3 mm of a whole millimetre). A printed copy may be scaled slightly, so a student's ruler may differ by 1 mm; the answer says so. The plan numbers the eight lines 1 to 8 from the top of the picture to the bottom (see DESIGN.md, item measure.design).
8. **Sprout diary (p. 62)** is a real four-day activity with the student's own seeds. The plan gives a sample row of practice values and says "Your answer may differ".
9. **Let Us Do on double number lines, line (a) (p. 63).** The last blank under the 50 cm tick is printed with the unit m, while every other blank on that line is in mm. 50 cm is 500 mm and also half a metre. The plan answers "500 mm (which is also half a metre)" so that either reading is covered. Line (b) of the same question is drawn with its ticks evenly spaced although the values are not.
10. **Let Us Do on conversions, Q2 (pp. 63 to 64)** prints two solved cases (b) and (h) as margin notes. The plan keeps them as the book's worked method and still answers all fourteen parts. The fourteen parts are split into three question sets by unit pair (a to d, e to i, j to n) so that each answer fits the display limit.
11. **Multiplying and dividing, Q3 (Anita, p. 67).** "She needs a 1 m long thread to embroider a 50 cm sari" means 1 m of thread for every 50 cm of border. The plan reads the 5 m border as 500 cm = 10 x 50 cm, so 10 m of thread, costing 10 x Rs 50 = Rs 500.
12. **Let Us Estimate (p. 68)** is about the student's own neighbourhood. The plan gives a sample answer with a reference and practice values for each and says "Your answer may differ".
13. **The 4 by 4 sign puzzle (p. 69).** The book says "each 4 x 4 box should not repeat the sign", but the grid is itself 4 by 4. The plan reads the boxes as the four 2 x 2 boxes of the grid. The puzzle has exactly one solution from the rows and columns alone, and that solution also has no sign repeated in any 2 x 2 box, so the reading is safe. This is a printed-book error to be confirmed.
14. **Note for the Teachers (p. 65).** The plan keeps its idea (work in your head when the numbers are easy, like 200, 250, 400, 500 or 750; use a column method otherwise; see that lengths behave like numbers) as the plan's own teaching line, with no teaching role given to a teacher.
15. **Measure Your Height (p. 69).** The scale in the picture shows the labels 4 ft, 1 in and 2 in near the top; the book does not say what height the child has. Nothing is read from it.
16. **The pictures of the ruler (p. 61) and the inch ruler (p. 68)** are drawn larger than a real ruler; the plan takes no length from them except the count of small marks (9 between 1 cm and 2 cm).
17. The four long-distance tools (long tape, rodometer, rangefinder, laser distance meter) are only named in the book; the plan adds no facts about how they work.
18. The QR code on the opener page is not readable from the supplied file; nothing is taken from it.

## SECTION C: Interactive Tool Specifications

Common to every tool (details in sections D and E): one activity at a time; Back and Next always in the same place; progress dots; Hint, Retry, Reset, and (where objects move) Undo; specific feedback ("1,100 m is 1 km and 100 m, so carry 1 km"), never only right or wrong; the length being worked on is the largest thing on screen. Practice values the plan adds are labelled "practice values"; every other number is the book's.

### Tool 1. Unit Picker
- **Connected topic:** Which Unit Fits? (the opening topic, free first try right after the hook; the concept videos follow it). Page 57.
- **Learning objective:** Students choose centimetres or metres for a length by comparing the number with the size of the thing measured.
- **Why a tool:** the idea only convinces when students try both units and see how the length looks against a tape.
- **Environment:** the long cloth table of the village weaving centre, with a wall tape above it.
- **Objects:** a picture card of a thing (a pencil, a classroom door, a school bus, a postage stamp, a handkerchief) with a number tag beside it; two unit tags, cm and m, to drop on the number; a tape strip along the wall that draws the length as a bar for the chosen unit (a 1 m mark and a 1 cm mark are always shown); a "fits the picture?" read-out.
- **Controls:** drag the cm or the m tag onto the number tag; the bar appears; Check; choose answer cards for the book's questions.
- **What the student does:** The hook's picture of Shikha with a cm tape and Sonu with a m tape; one line: "Which tape fits a handkerchief? Which fits a sari?" (1) a pencil with the number 17: try m, the bar runs far past the picture, then try cm, the bar fits; (2) a classroom door with 2; (3) a school bus with 9; (4) a postage stamp with 3 (practice values); the tool says why the other unit does not fit. The book's Let Us Find unchanged: choose m or cm for the height of India Gate (42), the length of a handkerchief (40), the depth of a well (50), the length of a mobile phone (13), the length of an elephant's trunk (2), the distance between two buttons on a shirt (5). The concept videos that follow name the rule.
- **Feedback and hints:** "17 m is longer than a classroom wall, but a pencil is small, so 17 cm." Hints: how big is the thing; could it be as long as a classroom; which unit makes the bar fit the picture; bottom-out drops the right tag.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry takes the tag off this card; Reset returns all cards.

### Tool 2. Length Bars
- **Connected topic:** Comparing Lengths (Let Us Compare). Page 58.
- **Learning objective:** Students compare two lengths given in different units by changing both to one unit, and find the difference as the gap between the bars.
- **Why a tool:** seeing a 6 m 5 cm bar and a 6 m 50 cm bar side by side on one tape makes "605 cm is less than 650 cm" visible.
- **Environment:** the weaving centre's wall tape in cm, with rods and cloth lengths hung along it.
- **Objects:** two bars on a tape (drawn to scale for the chosen lengths); a length card for each bar in its own units (for example 6 m 5 cm); a "change to cm" button that shows the working (6 x 100 + 5 = 605); a sign selector <, =, >; a gap marker that shows the difference; a "double" button that stretches a bar to twice its length; the six statues of the book as bars on one base line, tap two to compare.
- **Controls:** tap "change to cm" on each card; drag the sign into the middle; tap two statues; tap "double".
- **What the student does:** The picture of two bars with different units; instruction "Change both to centimetres, then compare." (1) compare 4 m 3 cm with 430 cm after changing both to cm (practice values); (2) compare 3 m 20 cm with 300 cm + 20 cm; (3) find the gap between 3 m 45 cm and 290 cm; (4) double a bar of 85 cm and find the bar of the same length; (5) put three lengths in order after changing them to cm. The book's items unchanged: Ritika's five comparisons (a) 456 cm and 5 m, (b) 55 cm + 200 cm and 200 cm + 54 cm, (c) 6 m 5 cm and 6 m 50 cm, (d) 2 m 150 cm and 3 m 50 cm, (e) 238 cm and 138 cm + 1 m; and the statue questions (difference between the tallest statue and the Statue of Liberty; the statues with the least difference; the statues with the largest difference; the statue that doubled equals the Statue of Unity).
- **Feedback and hints:** "6 m 5 cm is 605 cm and 6 m 50 cm is 650 cm, so 605 is less than 650." Hints: are the units the same; which unit to change to; what is 6 m in cm; bottom-out shows both numbers in cm.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the sign and the conversion of this card; Reset restores all bars.

### Tool 3. Kilometre Track
- **Connected topic:** Measuring Long Distances (the kilometre, ropes and the Kilometre Race). Pages 59 to 60.
- **Learning objective:** Students see 1 km as 1,000 m and find how many equal ropes or stops fit along a distance and where they stand.
- **Why a tool:** laying ropes end to end along a long strip and counting them makes "1,000 divided by the length of one rope" something students do.
- **Environment:** the road outside the weaving centre, drawn as a long straight strip with a kilometre scale along it.
- **Objects:** a track strip for 1 km (and one for 3 km) marked in 100 m steps; rope pieces of chosen length (1,000 m, 100 m, 10 m, 200 m, 500 m, 250 m) that snap end to end; stop markers (a water cup, a child, a red flag and a blue flag) that the student places at a chosen interval; a counter "pieces placed" and a list of positions from the start.
- **Controls:** pick a rope length, drag ropes onto the track; pick an interval and a marker, tap "place stops"; remove with Undo; Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of a 2 km path with a flag after every 400 m (practice values); instruction "Fill the track with ropes of the same length." Fill 1 km with ropes of 125 m and of 50 m and count them (practice values); place a marker every 250 m on a 2 km track and read the positions; compare an interval of 400 m with 200 m to see that a smaller interval gives more stops; drag a marker to 1 km 500 m and type it in m. The book's items unchanged: the rope table (1,000 m, 100 m, 10 m, 200 m, 500 m, 250 m: how many ropes make 1 km) and the Kilometre Race: water stations after every 500 m, children at an interval of 300 m, red and blue flags alternately at every 50 m on a 3 km race.
- **Feedback and hints:** "4 ropes of 250 m make 1,000 m, which is 1 km." Hints: how many metres in 1 km; how long is one rope; how many of them fill 1,000 m; bottom-out drops the ropes.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the track being worked; Reset restores both tracks.

### Tool 4. Millimetre Ruler
- **Connected topic:** The World of Small Things. Pages 61 to 62.
- **Learning objective:** Students read and draw lengths in cm and mm using the small marks of a ruler.
- **Why a tool:** the marks are tiny on paper; a zoom lens and a draggable end make "4 cm and 6 small marks" something students see and set.
- **Environment:** the ruler desk of the weaving centre with a big yellow ruler and a magnifying lens.
- **Objects:** a long ruler in cm with 10 small marks in every cm (the 5 mm mark taller); a zoom lens that magnifies one cm; a draggable end point on a line; objects to measure (a sprout, a small screw, a nail, with practice lengths); a read-out "4 cm 6 mm = 46 mm"; the book's design with eight lines (lengths 48, 35, 60, 27, 22, 19, 44, 61 mm).
- **Controls:** drag the line end along the ruler (it snaps to a mm mark); tap the lens to zoom; choose the cm and mm boxes to type a reading; Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of a ruler with a lens on 1 cm: 10 equal parts, each 1 mm. Instruction "Count the small marks between two centimetre marks." Read the length of a sprout and a screw placed on the ruler (practice values), then drag the line end to draw 4 cm 6 mm and 72 mm (practice values), reading each in cm and mm and in mm only. The book's items unchanged: count the small marks between 1 cm and 2 cm; measure the eight lines of the design in cm and mm; draw lines of 5 cm 5 mm, 3 cm 6 mm, 8 cm 3 mm, 36 mm and 67 mm. (The sprout diary needs real seeds and is done at home.)
- **Feedback and hints:** "Your line ends on the 6th small mark after 4 cm: 4 cm 6 mm, which is 46 mm." Hints: which centimetre mark is just before the end; how many small marks after it; each small mark is 1 mm; bottom-out places the end point.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the line of this card; Reset restores the ruler and the objects.

### Tool 5. Double Number Line
- **Connected topic:** Changing Units (Let Us Do on double number lines and conversions). Pages 62 to 64.
- **Learning objective:** Students change a length from one unit to another with the multipliers 10, 100 and 1,000, and split a length into two units.
- **Why a tool:** two linked lines with a pointer that moves on both show the same length in two units, and the multiplier arrow is the idea itself.
- **Environment:** a pair of tapes on the weaving centre wall, one in the bigger unit and one in the smaller unit, ticks lined up.
- **Objects:** two parallel number lines with ticks; a draggable pointer that moves on both lines together; a unit switch (cm over mm, m over cm, km over m); multiplier arrow boxes (x10, x100, x1,000) that the student can type into; tick labels with blanks to fill; a split tray that cuts a length into a part in the bigger unit and a leftover part in the smaller unit (5,792 cm into 5,700 cm and 92 cm, then 57 m 92 cm).
- **Controls:** drag the pointer; tap a blank label and type; tap the multiplier boxes; drag the split mark in the tray; Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of 1 cm over 10 mm with the arrow x10. Instruction "Slide the pointer and read both lines." Slide the pointer to 6 cm and read 60 mm (practice values); switch to m over cm and read 4 m over 400 cm; switch to km over m and read 5 km over 5,000 m; type the multiplier for 8 cm over 80 mm; then split a length (practice values: 4,205 cm into 42 m 5 cm). The book's items unchanged: the three double number lines (cm over mm with x7 and x10; m over cm with x4 and x100; km over m with x9 and x1,000) with their blanks, and the conversions (a) to (n).
- **Feedback and hints:** "7 cm is 7 x 10 = 70 mm, so the blank is 70." Hints: which unit is on top; what is the multiplier from the top line to the bottom line; multiply the top number by it; bottom-out fills the blank.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the blanks of this card; Reset restores the lines.

### Tool 6. Length Columns
- **Connected topic:** Adding and Subtracting Lengths. Pages 64 to 66.
- **Learning objective:** Students add and subtract lengths given in two units by working in a column for each unit and regrouping (1 km = 1,000 m, 1 m = 100 cm).
- **Why a tool:** carrying and borrowing between unit columns is the idea, and exchange chips make "1,100 m becomes 1 km and 100 m" something students do.
- **Environment:** the cutting table of the weaving centre with a chart of two columns on the wall.
- **Objects:** a table with two columns (km and m, or m and cm) and rows for the two lengths and the answer; number tiles; exchange chips: one chip "1 km" turns into "1,000 m" and back, one chip "1 m" turns into "100 cm" and back; a "check by changing to the smaller unit" strip.
- **Controls:** tap a cell and type a number; drag a chip to exchange; tap Add or Subtract; Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of two lengths in a table. Instruction "Put each length in its columns." Add 2 km 900 m and 1 km 800 m and add 5 m 60 cm and 3 m 70 cm (practice values), carrying a full bigger unit with a chip; subtract 3 m 40 cm from 9 m and 2 km 600 m from 5 km 200 m by exchanging one bigger unit for the smaller unit; check each answer by changing to the smaller unit. The book's items unchanged: Saji's walk (3 km 450 m and 4 km 650 m), the electricians' cable (63 m minus 16 m 75 cm), Rani's ribbons (3 m 75 cm and 2 m 25 cm), Bhopal to Sanchi (48 km 700 m minus 17 km 900 m), Gulmarg Gondola (2 km 300 m and 2 km 650 m), and the seven pairs (circle the bigger length and find the difference).
- **Feedback and hints:** "450 m + 650 m = 1,100 m, which is 1 km 100 m: write 100 m and carry 1 km." Hints: which units are in each column; do the smaller unit first; can the answer in the smaller unit be changed to the bigger one; bottom-out does the exchange.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the cells of this card; Reset restores the table.

### Tool 7. Cut and Share
- **Connected topic:** Multiplying and Dividing Lengths. Pages 66 to 67.
- **Learning objective:** Students multiply and divide a length given in two units by working on each unit and then regrouping.
- **Why a tool:** laying equal pieces end to end and sorting the metres from the centimetres shows why 20 x 80 cm becomes 1,600 cm, which is 16 m.
- **Environment:** the cutting table with a roll of cloth and a road strip beside it.
- **Objects:** a strip of cloth of a chosen length in metres and centimetres; a "make copies" stepper that lays the copies end to end on a tape; two piles, metres and centimetres, that collect the parts; an exchange chip (100 cm becomes 1 m); a road bar of a chosen length (in km and m) with a "share into n equal days" stepper that gives each day box its km and m.
- **Controls:** stepper for the number of copies or days; drag the parts into the m pile and the cm pile; tap the exchange chip; Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of curtains of 2 m 40 cm. Instruction "Break the length into metres and centimetres." Lay 5, 6 and 8 copies of strips of 3 m 20 cm, 1 m 50 cm and 2 m 25 cm (practice values), read the m pile and the cm pile and exchange 100 cm for 1 m; share a road of 24 km 800 m into 4 equal days and a road of 7 km 200 m into 4 equal days (practice values), changing the road to m when the km do not share equally. The book's items unchanged: shirts for 20 children from 1 m 80 cm each; Anita's thread for a 5 m border and its cost; the road of 12 km 600 m laid in 6 equal days.
- **Feedback and hints:** "20 x 80 cm = 1,600 cm, and 100 cm make 1 m, so 16 m." Hints: how many pieces; what are the metres and the centimetres separately; multiply each part; bottom-out lays the pieces.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the piles of this card; Reset restores the roll and the road.

### Tool 8. Height Chart
- **Connected topic:** Feet and Inches. Pages 68 to 69.
- **Learning objective:** Students read a height in feet and inches (12 inches = 1 foot) and find the tallest in a list.
- **Why a tool:** a wall chart with a movable mark shows feet and inches together, and ordering a class list makes the mixed units a comparison problem.
- **Environment:** the wall of the weaving centre with a tall wooden height chart.
- **Objects:** a wall chart with foot marks and inch marks and a cm scale on the other edge; a figure standing against it; a draggable mark for the top of the head; a read-out "4 feet 6 inches = 54 inches"; a class list with typed entries (feet and inches) sorted by height; a note "1 inch = 2 cm 5 mm, 1 foot = 12 inches = 30 cm".
- **Controls:** drag the mark; type feet and inches into a list row; tap "find the tallest"; Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of a height of 4 feet 6 inches (practice values). Instruction "Set the mark and read feet and inches." Set the mark to 5 feet 2 inches and read 62 inches (practice values); change 45 inches into feet and inches; read 2 feet in cm and 1 inch in mm; put three heights in the list and find the tallest. The book's Measure Your Height: stand against a wall and mark your height; measure the distance between the floor and the marked point in feet and inches; other students measure theirs; find the tallest student in your class and his or her height in feet and inches. The student types the real heights, or uses the practice row of the tool.
- **Feedback and hints:** "54 inches is 4 feet 6 inches, because 4 x 12 = 48 and 54 - 48 = 6." Hints: how many inches in a foot; how many full feet; how many inches are left; bottom-out sets the mark.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the row being worked; Reset restores the chart and the list.

## SECTION D: Shared Visual Language

- **Overall illustration style:** warm, colourful, polished educational-game illustrations; 3D or isometric where the scene holds objects, soft shadows, clear shapes, clean uncluttered compositions; gentle animation. The length being measured is always the largest, clearest thing on screen.
- **Chapter theme and environment:** "Shikha and Sonu's Measuring Table at the Village Weaving Centre". One friendly weaving centre with a long measuring tape running along the top of the wall. Each tool is a station that hangs from that tape: the cloth table (Unit Picker, Length Bars), the road outside (Kilometre Track), the ruler desk (Millimetre Ruler), the double tape wall (Double Number Line), the cutting table (Length Columns, Cut and Share) and the tall wooden chart (Height Chart). Nothing is added that does not help the mathematics.
- **Characters:** only the book's: Shikha, Sonu, Ritika, Sheena, Jennifer, Saji, Rani and Anita. They appear to set a situation, never to teach.
- **Colour and visual direction:** warm cream and wood tones for the background; indigo and marigold cloth for the lengths. One colour per role: the length being asked about is orange, the second length is teal, correct is green, "look again" is soft amber (never red). Tape and ruler marks are dark on pale yellow.
- **Main visual objects:** a length drawn as a bar on a tape with equal ticks, labelled in large clear type with its unit; two linked number lines; rulers with small marks; unit tags (mm, cm, m, km) as coloured chips.
- **Animation style:** only meaningful movement: bars slide onto the tape, a pointer moves along both lines together, an exchange chip turns 1 m into 100 cm, ropes snap end to end. No decoration that moves.
- **UI layout and navigation:** one activity per screen; instruction strip at the top (one sentence, large); the working area in the middle; Hint, Retry, Reset (and Undo) in a fixed row at the bottom; Back and Next fixed at the bottom corners; progress dots under the instruction. Same placement in all eight tools.
- **Common interaction patterns:** tap to select, drag to move or snap, a stepper for "how many", a Check button that gives specific feedback, a three-step hint ladder with a worked step at the end, Retry for the step, Reset for the tool.

## SECTION E: Learning and Platform Rules

1. Videos explain; tools let the student explore, apply and get feedback. No classroom teacher is given a teaching role anywhere.
2. Every tool has one distinct mathematical purpose; no interaction is repeated across tools without a learning reason. Tools 5 and 6 both regroup units but one changes a single length between units and the other adds and subtracts two lengths; Tool 7 multiplies and divides.
3. A tool is made from its concept description: the idea, the screen, what the student does, the practice cards, feedback and hints. It is not scripted in phases.
4. A tool card that quotes the book uses the book's own questions, with their original wording, numbers and conditions. The plan does not change them (the one printed error, the 4 x 4 box of the sign puzzle, is named in A6.13) and does not invent a challenge where the book has none.
5. Preserve the book's mathematics: its units, its tables, its heights of statues, its station distances. Any number the plan adds is labelled "practice values".
6. Do not introduce ideas outside the chapter: no decimals or decimal points for lengths (2.5 cm is written 2 cm 5 mm), no perimeter, area, weight, capacity or speed.
7. One video per book question, on its answer, plus a question card and a solution card; one video per teaching idea; the hook comes first and does not explain the chapter.
8. Answers the book does not print are worked out from the chapter's own ideas and shown as the plan's reading (section A6). Activities that need the student's own school, home or seeds get a sample answer with practice values and "Your answer may differ".
9. Text hygiene: no em or en dash; minus is the true minus sign; students never read "NCERT", "textbook", page numbers or exercise numbers; say "your maths book".
10. Instructions are short, age-appropriate, one at a time; feedback is specific; hint, retry and reset are always present.
11. One visual identity (the weaving centre) and one navigation layout across all tools.
12. Verify page numbers, figures and numbers against the book's page images before any build.
