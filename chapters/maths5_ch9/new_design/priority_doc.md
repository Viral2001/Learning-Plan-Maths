## SECTION A: Chapter Understanding

### A1. Chapter name
Coconut Farm (Class 5, Maths Mela, Chapter 9). The chapter is about division: the link between multiplication and division, dividing tens and hundreds, mental strategies, dividing by taking away groups (partial quotients), dividing by place value, the remainder rule N = D × Q + R, word problems, and mathematical statements.

### A2. Main learning objectives
Students will be able to:
1. Write two division facts from one multiplication fact (an array of coconuts), and name the dividend, divisor and quotient with Dividend = Divisor × Quotient.
2. Divide tens, hundreds and thousands using a table fact and the place value pattern (4000 ÷ 10 = 400).
3. Divide in the head by splitting the number into convenient parts, by rounding up and taking the extra away, and by halving again and again.
4. Divide by taking away groups of the divisor (Susie's way) and by taking away the biggest groups that fit (Sunitha's way), estimating the answer first.
5. Find the remainder and check every division with N = D × Q + R.
6. Divide by place value, regrouping hundreds into tens and tens into ones, and write the zero that holds an empty place.
7. Solve word problems: decide what the remainder means, use a bill (total = cost of 1 × quantity), solve in steps and find missing information.
8. Use a known division to find related divisions when the dividend or the divisor is doubled or halved.
9. Check that both sides of an equal sign have the same value, and decide whether a statement is always, sometimes or never true.

### A5. Natural learning sequence
Division facts from an array (hook, a free try, the two facts, the three names) -> dividing tens and hundreds and the place value pattern -> mental strategies (split, round up, halve) -> Susie's farm: taking away groups, the biggest group, the remainder rule -> bigger numbers (oil, husk, tender coconuts) -> division by place value (regroup, zero holder, thousands, two-digit divisors) -> word problems (the meaning of the remainder, bills, steps, missing information) -> how the quotient changes -> mathematical statements (equal sign; always, sometimes, never) -> revision. This is the book's own order, with three changes, all listed in section B notes: (1) the word problems of pages 126 and 132 to 134 are gathered after place value division, where all the methods are known; (2) Let Us Solve Q5 of page 132 (how the quotient changes) has its own topic; (3) the remainder questions of pages 126, 132 and the twelve divisions of page 134 sit with the idea they practise (the remainder rule and place value division).

## SECTION C: Interactive Tool Specifications

Common to every tool (details in sections D and E): one activity at a time; Back and Next always in the same place; progress dots; Hint, Retry, Reset, and (where moving objects) Undo; specific feedback ("700 is more than the 509 coconuts left, take away a smaller group"), never only right or wrong; the mathematical object is the largest thing on screen. Practice values the plan adds are labelled "practice values"; every other number is the book's.

### Tool 1. Coconut Array
- **Connected topic:** Susie's Array of Coconuts (the opening topic, free first try right after the hook; the concept videos follow it). Page 119.
- **Learning objective:** Students build an array of coconuts with a chosen number of rows and columns and read the multiplication fact and the two division facts that go with it.
- **Why a tool:** seeing the same coconuts split by columns and by rows is the whole idea of the two division facts; building the array makes it a discovery before the video names it.
- **Environment:** the packing shed table on Susie and Sunitha's farm; a wooden tray holds coconuts in rows.
- **Objects:** a tray that shows up to 9 rows and 9 columns of coconuts; two steppers (rows, columns); a fact board with three lines (rows × columns = total, total ÷ columns = rows, total ÷ rows = columns) and blank cards for the student to fill; a "groups by columns" and a "groups by rows" highlight.
- **Controls:** tap the steppers to set the rows and columns; tap a highlight to colour the groups; drag number cards onto the fact board; Check.
- **What the student does:** The hook's array of 5 rows of 7 coconuts; instruction: "Count the coconuts. How many rows? How many in each row?" Practice values: (1) build 4 rows of 6 and read 4 × 6 = 24; (2) highlight columns: 6 groups with 4 in each group, 24 ÷ 6 = 4; highlight rows: 4 groups with 6 in each group, 24 ÷ 4 = 6; (3) build 3 rows of 8: the same 24, new facts; (4) a given array of 6 rows of 5: fill the fact board; (5) build 1 row of 9 and see 9 ÷ 1 = 9. The book's items unchanged: "Observe the following array of coconuts. Write two division facts using the given multiplication fact." (5 × 7 = 35) and "Write the appropriate multiplication fact for the array shown below. Write two division facts that follow from the multiplication fact." (4 rows of 8).
- **Feedback and hints:** "35 split into 7 groups has 5 in each group: 35 ÷ 7 = 5." Hints: how many rows and columns; which number is the total; which two numbers are the groups and the size of a group; bottom-out fills one fact.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the fact board of this card; Reset returns an empty tray.

### Tool 2. Place Value Shifter
- **Connected topic:** Patterns in Division and Place Value. Page 121.
- **Learning objective:** Students divide tens, hundreds and thousands by 10, by 100 and by a one-digit number using a table fact, and describe how the digits and zeros move.
- **Why a tool:** the pattern only shows when the same digit is seen sliding one place right each time a zero is divided out.
- **Environment:** a chart board on the packing shed wall: columns Thousands, Hundreds, Tens, Ones, in the book's colours (green, orange, blue, pink).
- **Objects:** a place value chart with digit tiles; a "divide by" dial (10, 100, or a one-digit number); a "tens bundles" row that groups the number into bundles of ten or hundred; a read-out "28 tens ÷ 4 = 7 tens = 70"; a pattern strip that stacks the rows already done.
- **Controls:** drag digit tiles or press the dial; tap "Show bundles"; type the quotient into the chart; Check.
- **What the student does:** "150 is 15 tens. How many 3s in 15?" with the chart showing 15 tens (the video idea; the numbers 150 and 3 are the book's hint, shown only as a question). Practice values: (1) 280 ÷ 4: 28 tens ÷ 4 = 7 tens = 70; (2) 2400 ÷ 8: 24 hundreds ÷ 8 = 3 hundreds = 300; (3) 6000 ÷ 10 = 600 and 6000 ÷ 100 = 60, the digit 6 slides right; (4) 9100 ÷ 91 = 100 and 910 ÷ 91 = 10; (5) fill the chart for 3000 ÷ 10 and 5000 ÷ 100. The book's items unchanged: "Solve the following division problems. Notice the patterns and discuss in class." (nine divisions with the hints) and "Now fill the place value chart." (both tables).
- **Feedback and hints:** "40 ÷ 10 = 4: the 4 moved from tens to ones." Hints: how many tens or hundreds does the number have; what is the table fact; how many zeros does the answer need; bottom-out slides the digits once.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the chart of this card; Reset empties the chart and the pattern strip.

### Tool 3. Smart Splitter
- **Connected topic:** Mental Strategies for Division. Pages 123 to 124.
- **Learning objective:** Students split a dividend into convenient parts (a sum or a difference), divide each part and combine, and halve again and again for divisors 4 and 8.
- **Why a tool:** choosing where to cut the number, and seeing the two parts divided, is the strategy.
- **Environment:** the farm's tally board: number bars that can be cut.
- **Objects:** a long number bar for the dividend; a cutter with a handle that sets the cut at a chosen place; a "plus" and a "minus" mode (minus shows a longer round bar with a piece taken off); two small bars for the parts, each with a divisor dial; a halving chain of boxes; the answer slot.
- **Controls:** drag the cutter; choose plus or minus; type each part divided by the divisor; press Halve to fill the next box; Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's split of 2856 into 2800 and 56 (practice values); instruction "Cut the number where 7 divides both parts easily." Practice values: (1) 3,654 ÷ 6 as 3,600 + 54; (2) 1,484 ÷ 7 as 1,400 + 84; (3) 4,995 ÷ 5 as 5,000 − 5; (4) 392 ÷ 4 by halving twice; (5) 520 ÷ 8 by halving three times. The book's Try It! items unchanged: 64 ÷ 4, 265 ÷ 5, 1560 ÷ 8, 4824 ÷ 24 with the split boxes, and 168 ÷ 8, 144 ÷ 4 with the halving boxes.
- **Feedback and hints:** "40 and 24 are both easy to divide by 4." Hints: which round number is close; does the divisor divide each part; add or subtract the two answers; bottom-out places the cut.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the cut of this card; Reset returns the bar.

### Tool 4. Take Away Groups
- **Connected topic:** Susie's Farm in Kerala (taking away groups, the biggest group, the remainder rule). Pages 124 to 126.
- **Learning objective:** Students divide by taking away groups of the divisor from a pile, record the partial quotients, read the remainder, and check the result with N = D × Q + R.
- **Why a tool:** taking groups away from a pile and watching the number left fall makes partial quotients concrete, and the "too big" feedback shows why the biggest group must fit.
- **Environment:** a pile of coconuts at the packing shed and a row of bags (groups).
- **Objects:** the dividend as a pile and a number; a divisor; take-away buttons ("take 100 groups", "take 10 groups", "take 1 group" of the divisor); the book's layout recording each subtraction and the partial quotients (for example 20 + 20 + 10 + 7); a remainder counter; the N = D × Q + R board; an estimate bar ("less than 100? between 100 and 200?").
- **Controls:** press a take-away button; Undo; type the estimate; type D, Q and R into the board; Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of 378 coconuts and groups of 6 (practice values); instruction "Take away groups of 6. How many groups can you take?" Practice values: (1) 456 ÷ 6 by two takes (6 × 70, then 6 × 6); (2) 509 ÷ 7: the 100-group is too big, then 70 and 2, remainder 5; (3) estimate 845 ÷ 5 first (between 100 and 200), then 100 + 60 + 9; (4) 372 ÷ 12 with a two-digit divisor (30 + 1); (5) write 509 = 7 × 72 + 5 and check that the remainder is less than the divisor. The book's items unchanged: Let Us Learn to Divide, 726 ÷ 4 and 902 ÷ 16 with the blanks, and "Is 726 = 4 × 181? Yes/No. So, 726 = 4 × 181 + ___" with "Is 902 = 16 × 56? Yes/No. So, 902 = 16 × 56 + ___".
- **Feedback and hints:** "700 is more than the 509 coconuts left. Take away a smaller group." Hints: how many groups fit; take the biggest tens you can; what is left; bottom-out takes one group for you.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry restores the pile of this card; Reset returns a new pile.

### Tool 5. Regroup and Share
- **Connected topic:** Division Using Place Value. Pages 128 to 131.
- **Learning objective:** Students divide by place value: share each place equally, regroup what is left into the next place, and write the quotient with a zero in any empty place.
- **Why a tool:** regrouping a ten into 10 ones or a hundred into 10 tens is a physical move; the zero in the quotient makes sense when a place cannot be shared.
- **Environment:** the packing shed table with trays for the equal parts.
- **Objects:** base ten blocks (flats for hundreds, rods for tens, small cubes for ones, in the book's colours orange, blue, pink); 2 to 9 plates (the divisor); a remainder tray; a regroup button (1 hundred into 10 tens, 1 ten into 10 ones); a place value strip H T O (and Th) for the quotient digits; the long-division record that fills in beside it.
- **Controls:** press Share for a place; press Regroup; drag a block to a plate; type the digit into the strip; Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of 83 shared among 6 plates (practice values); instruction "Share the tens first. Are any tens left over?" Practice values: (1) 94 ÷ 4 (regroup 1 ten, quotient 23, remainder 2); (2) 57 ÷ 9 (no tens shared, 6 remainder 3); (3) 245 ÷ 5 (no hundreds shared, 24 tens, quotient 49); (4) 618 ÷ 6 (the empty tens place, quotient 103); (5) predict the digits of 856 ÷ 4 and 356 ÷ 4 before sharing. The book's items unchanged: "Sunitha's mother has 62 candies to be distributed equally among 5 children. How many candies would each child get?", 75 ÷ 8, 324 ÷ 3, 136 ÷ 6, and "Can you tell just by looking at the divisor and dividend, how many digits the quotient would have?"
- **Feedback and hints:** "2 tens cannot be shared among 3 plates, so each plate gets 0 tens: write 0." Hints: can every plate get at least one block of this place; what do you do with the blocks left; how many ones now; bottom-out regroups once.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry restores the blocks of this card; Reset clears the table.

### Tool 6. Quotient Change
- **Connected topic:** How the Quotient Changes. Page 132.
- **Learning objective:** Students predict how the quotient changes when the dividend or the divisor is doubled or halved, and use a known division to write related divisions.
- **Why a tool:** a rectangle whose area is the dividend and whose sides are the divisor and the quotient shows at once why doubling one side doubles the area and halving the other keeps it.
- **Environment:** a farm plot drawn as a rectangle of coconut trees on a notice board.
- **Objects:** a rectangle with side D, side Q and area N; buttons "double the dividend", "halve the dividend", "double the divisor", "halve the divisor"; a "related facts" list that stores each new division; a prediction card (more, less or the same).
- **Controls:** press a change button; choose the predicted change; type the new quotient; Check.
- **What the student does:** The rectangle for a known division, 240 ÷ 12 = 20 (practice values); instruction "What is the area? What are the sides?" Practice values: (1) double the dividend: 480 ÷ 12 = 40; (2) halve the dividend: 120 ÷ 12 = 10; (3) double the divisor: 240 ÷ 24 = 10; (4) halve the divisor: 240 ÷ 6 = 40; (5) double both: 480 ÷ 24 = 20, the same quotient. The book's items unchanged: "Find the solutions for part A. Observe the relations between the quotient, divisor and dividend and use it to answer parts B and C." with parts A, B and C.
- **Feedback and hints:** "The divisor doubled, so the quotient is halved: 240 ÷ 24 = 10." Hints: which number changed; did it get bigger or smaller; what happens to the other side; bottom-out shows the rectangle changing.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry restores the start rectangle; Reset clears the related list.

### Tool 7. Equal Balance
- **Connected topic:** True or False Statements. Page 135.
- **Learning objective:** Students decide if a statement is true by finding the value of both sides, and complete a statement so that both sides have the same value.
- **Why a tool:** a balance that tips shows what "the same value on both sides" means, and a missing number is found by making it level.
- **Environment:** the farm's market scale with two pans.
- **Objects:** a balance with a left pan and a right pan; number and operation cards that make a side (for example 3 × 8 and 20 + 4); a value read-out above each pan; a blank slot for a missing number; a True and a False tag.
- **Controls:** drag cards to the pans; type a number into the blank; tap True or False; Check.
- **What the student does:** A balance with 6 × 5 on the left and 20 + 10 on the right (practice values), level; instruction "Find the value of each side." Practice values: (1) 3 × 8 and 20 + 4: level, true; (2) 40 − 9 and 5 × 7: the scale tips, false; (3) 56 ÷ 7 and 4 × 2: level; (4) complete 9 × 4 = ___ + 16 (20); (5) complete ___ ÷ 6 = 18 ÷ 3 (36). The book's items unchanged: Q1 "Find out whether the following statements are True (T) or False (F). A true sentence is one where both sides of the '=' sign have the same value." (a) to (e), and Q2 "Complete the following statements such that they are true." (a) to (d).
- **Feedback and hints:** "The left side is 31 and the right side is 35, so the statement is false." Hints: find the value of the left side; find the right side; compare them; bottom-out shows both values.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the pans; Reset restores the starting card.

### Tool 8. Always, Sometimes, Never
- **Connected topic:** Always, Sometimes or Never True. Page 135.
- **Learning objective:** Students test a statement with number examples and decide if it is always true, sometimes true or never true, knowing that one example that fails shows it is not always true.
- **Why a tool:** testing many numbers quickly, and watching the pile of examples and counter-examples grow, is how a statement is judged.
- **Environment:** three baskets on a market stall labelled Always true, Sometimes true and Never true.
- **Objects:** a statement card; a number-card tray (the student picks numbers to test); a test result strip (works or does not work); three baskets.
- **Controls:** tap a number card to test it; drag the statement into a basket; Check.
- **What the student does:** A statement for the video's idea, "Adding 4 to an even number gives an even number" (practice values); instruction "Test it with three numbers." Practice values: (1) "Adding 2 to an even number gives an even number": always; (2) "Adding 1 to an even number gives an even number": never; (3) "Multiplying a number by 3 gives an even number": sometimes (3 × 4 = 12, 3 × 5 = 15); (4) "Doubling a number gives an even number": always. The book's items unchanged: Q3 (a) "When two odd numbers are added, the sum is even.", (b) "Multiplying a number by 2 can give an odd number.", (c) "Halving a number always leads to an even number." and Q4, the five statements to tick as always, sometimes or never true.
- **Feedback and hints:** "6 is even and 6 + 2 = 8 is even, it works. One example that fails is enough to show it is not always true." Hints: try a small number; try an odd number; can you find a number that fails; bottom-out shows one example that fails or works.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the tested numbers; Reset restores the statement.

## SECTION D: Shared Visual Language

- **Overall illustration style:** warm, colourful, polished educational-game illustrations; 3D or isometric where the scene holds objects, soft shadows, clear shapes, clean uncluttered compositions; gentle animation. The numbers and the groups are always the largest, clearest thing on screen.
- **Chapter theme and environment:** "Susie and Sunitha's Coconut Farm in Kerala". Every scene of the chapter sits on one friendly farm: the coconut grove, the packing shed with its crates, bags and trays, the oil press, and a market stall with a scale and a record book. Each tool is a station there: packing tray, chart board, tally board, pile and bags, block table, plot notice board, market scale, stall baskets. Nothing is added that does not help the mathematics.
- **Characters:** only the book's: Susie, Sunitha and her mother, Ibrahim, Munshi Lal and his grandson, Sabina, Seema, Rani, Sofia, Naina, Megha and Rahul. They appear to set a situation, never to teach.
- **Colour and visual direction:** coconut brown and palm green on warm sand; the book's place value colours (Thousands green, Hundreds orange, Tens blue, Ones pink); the number being asked about is orange, correct is green, "look again" is soft amber (never red).
- **Main visual objects:** arrays and piles of coconuts, bags and crates, base ten blocks, number bars that can be cut, a place value chart, the long-division layout of the book, balances and baskets.
- **Animation style:** only meaningful movement: coconuts slide into rows, groups are taken away from a pile, a ten breaks into ten ones, digits slide one place right. No decoration that moves.
- **UI layout and navigation:** one activity per screen; instruction strip at the top (one sentence, large); the working area in the middle; Hint, Retry, Reset (and Undo) in a fixed row at the bottom; Back and Next fixed at the bottom corners; progress dots under the instruction. Same placement in all eight tools.
- **Common interaction patterns:** tap to select, drag to move, a stepper or dial for a number, a Check button that gives specific feedback, a three-step hint ladder with a worked step at the end, Retry for the step, Reset for the tool.

## SECTION E: Learning and Platform Rules

1. Videos explain; tools let the student explore, apply and get feedback. No classroom teacher is given a teaching role anywhere.
2. Every tool has one distinct mathematical purpose; no interaction is repeated across tools without a learning reason. Tool 4 takes groups from a pile (partial quotients) and Tool 5 regroups blocks by place value; Tool 3 works on the number, not on a pile.
3. Each tool has three phases, in order: Recall and get ready, Play and apply, NCERT challenge.
4. A tool card that quotes the book uses the book's own questions, with their original wording, numbers and conditions. The plan does not change them and does not invent a challenge where the book has none.
5. Preserve the book's mathematics: its arrays (5 by 7 and 4 by 8), its numbers in every question, its two methods for 582 ÷ 6 and 4376 ÷ 8. Any number the plan adds is labelled "practice values".
6. Do not introduce ideas outside the chapter: no decimals, fractions or percent; no divisors beyond the book's methods; only whole numbers.
7. One video per book question, on its answer, plus a question card and a solution card; one video per teaching idea; the hook comes first and does not explain the chapter.
8. Answers the book does not print are worked out from the chapter's own ideas and shown as the plan's reading (section A6).
9. Text hygiene: no em or en dash; minus is the true minus sign; students never read "NCERT", "textbook", page numbers or exercise numbers; say "your maths book". A division with a remainder is written "quotient q, remainder r" and "n = d × q + r", never as one false chain.
10. Instructions are short, age-appropriate, one at a time; feedback is specific; hint, retry and reset are always present.
11. One visual identity (the coconut farm) and one navigation layout across all tools.
12. Verify page numbers, figures and numbers against the book's page images before any build.

### F2. Concepts and activities with no separate tool, and why
- **Dividend, divisor and quotient (naming) and dividing by 1:** short ideas shown in the video and used in every tool; Tool 1 already shows the three numbers.
- **Hundreds, tens and ones groups with bigger numbers (the oil, husk and tender coconut problems):** the same interaction as Tool 4 with bigger numbers; a second tool would repeat it.
