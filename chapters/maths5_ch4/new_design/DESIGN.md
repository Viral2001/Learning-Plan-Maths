## 1. Hard rules (gates enforce most; fix content, never a gate)
- Class 5 students, 10 to 11 years old: short plain sentences. Characters and settings are the book's: Nazrana and her friends, Mahesh and his family, Mary, Sukanta, Piku, Namita, Nitin, Jincy, Raghu, Ambrish; fuel, lorries, a minibus, a ship, trains, a truck, maps, a factory. The plan's own WE may name no new person: use "a traveller", "a family", "a shop", or a book character with the plan's own numbers. Do not invent facts or datasets: every number that is not a plain WE number comes from the book or from section 4. A WE number is a practice value and is said to be one only where the text would otherwise look like a fact (a table, a distance, a height).
- **No em dash and no en dash** in any string or comment (the book's dashes become commas, colons, hyphens inside words or "to"). Minus is U+2212 `−`. Use `×` and `÷`. Hyphen only inside words ("North-South", "5-digit").
- **Numbers:** four or more digits take commas as the book does (`1,234`, `12,345`, `3,717`); years do not (`2013`). The one six-digit number is written `1,05,050` (section A6.4). Amounts: `₹21,880` (the symbol glued to the number), litres, kilometres and kilograms written `litres`, `km`, `kg` after a space. Never the unit letter `l`. In a chain that the equation gate reads, write the numbers alone: `12,540 − 3,275 = 9,265`, then say "₹9,265" in words after it (the gate skips a chain that has ₹, km or kg inside it, so unit chains are NOT checked: you check them).
- Never write "NCERT", "textbook", "page N", "Fig.", "Figure N" or exercise numbers in anything the student reads (names, teach lines, steps, questions, tool text, image text). Say "your maths book". Exception: the `textbook=[...]` argument of `A()` quotes the book verbatim (stored as a `textbook_text` block), still no dashes.
- **Text shapes that convert to LaTeX cleanly** (`core/solutions/latex_convert.py` plus `ch_latex_house.py`): a part label gets a colon and a semicolon between parts, `(a): 15 + 79 = 94; (b): 46 + 99 = 145` (never `(a) 15 + 79 (b) ...`); place names are words, never the letters Th, H, T, O or TTh ("hundreds", "tens", "ones", "thousands", "ten thousands"); the words ON and OFF are fine (the house module puts them back); a blank is `___`; avoid a bare capital letter next to a number except "Group 1", "Group 2" and "Row 1"; write "3 digit" or "3-digit" as one phrase; do not start a sentence with a lone digit.
- **Arithmetic must be true in every string.** The equation gate `eqcheck_plain` checks whole-number chains `a op b op c = d` (comma-grouped numbers are read as one value) and SKIPS any chain that touches a letter, a slash, a bracket, ₹ or a unit. Every other fact (units, comparisons, years, counts, pattern claims) you re-compute with Python before you finish. Do not write an equation that is only approximately true. Keep whole-number chains on one line. Do not write a chain whose sides are different numbers of the same thing (`10 ones = 1 ten` is words, not digits, so it is safe).
- 100% coverage: every id in `content.NCERT_ITEMS` (section 3) is attached to a concept: a `text` or `talk` id through `A(..., ncert=[...])` or a DOER's `ncert=`, a question id as an `FIO` item of the DOER named. Use ONLY ids that exist in `content.py`; ask nothing, invent no id.
- **One video per concept.** A teaching concept `A()` has exactly ONE `WE` in `examples` (that WE is the concept video) with the PLAN's own numbers (section 2 gives them): never the exact numbers of a book question. The hook concept has `video_focus=["HOOK"]`, `enrichment=True`, no WE. DOER concepts carry a recap, their question items and at most one tool, no WE.
- **Questions.** Every book question is a `FIO(cov, label, question, steps, answer, kind=...)` in the DOER of its topic, in the book's order; `core/qt.py` moves each into its own concept of the "Questions: ..." topic that follows. `question` is the book's wording (copy it; blanks `___`; "textbook" becomes "maths book"; litres in full). `steps` = the numbered working, one operation per line, values before combining, a check as the last step; `answer` = short final answer. Open-ended items get a labelled "Sample answer" built by reasoning and say "Your answer may differ". Add `q_figure` (one plain sentence: ONLY what the question gives, never the answer or a hint) when the question card needs a drawing or a table, `s_figure` when the solution card needs one (it may show the answer).
- **Answers stay short: the displayed answer is cut at 220 characters of its LaTeX form** (a longer answer fails `solution_content`; every number in `$...$` costs 2 extra characters). Multi-part answers are value-only: `(a): 94; (b): 145; ...`. The full working goes in `steps`. The check script measures this.
- **Labels (`book`) decide the Questions topic** (`core/question_naming.py`): a label starting `Let Us Do` goes to "Questions: Let us do"; `Example N` to "Questions: Example" (N is the number given in section 3); any other label to a topic named by the text before its first colon. Use exactly the labels given in section 3.
- Names: DOER name starts with `Practice: ` or `Pull it together and do it: `.
- An idea topic has 2 to 4 primary concepts INCLUDING its DOER (last). The intro T1 is `topic_type="framing"` with `""` objective, all concepts `enrichment=True`: C1 hook (`video_focus=["HOOK"]`) and C3 a DOER with `role="try_it"` and the free-try tool (the framing topic holds exactly ONE video, the hook, and no book question). The summary topic is `topic_type="summary"`, one concept, no tool, no video_focus (there is no recap video).
- Objectives: one per non-enrichment concept, starts "Students ...", checkable (a verb you could test: add, subtract, find, list, explain, compare, check, make, fill), shares a key term with its concept name or text, and NEVER names a tool. Topic objective on each idea topic. Bloom words: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze.
- Misconception ids come from `content.MISCONCEPTIONS` (section 6); attach the relevant ones to teach concepts, DOERs and tools.
- `teach` lines: the first line is a prediction question ("Predict first: ..."), then the reveal, then the rule in one line. `minutes` 4 to 8. Do not use the word "NCERT". Do not teach multiplication tables, division, fractions, decimals, negative numbers, averages, place names beyond ten thousands.
- Tool specs (`T(...)`): section 5. Exactly 5 to 7 challenges (the first ones are Phase 2 play cards with answers, the LAST one or two start with "Phase 3" and quote the book's own question unchanged), exactly 4 hints, `student_does` has THREE labelled entries.
- Because the free-try tool follows the hook directly (before the concept video), its Phase 1 recalls the hook's story, and its Phase 3 asks the opener's question as an open challenge.

- **T1.C1 hook:** no WE. A short story: Nazrana and her friends are on their road trip (the book's own trip, Delhi onwards); to pass the time they write numbers on small pieces of paper and make two groups, 1, 2, 7, 9 and 3, 4, 5, 9, whose sums are 19 and 21. "Can one pair swap places so both groups add to the same number?" Do not solve it, do not teach. `textbook=[...]` quotes the book's opening paragraph; `ncert=["open.intro"]`.
- **T1.C3 try-it DOER:** recap lines (the hook), `tool="balance_sums"`, `role="try_it"`, `enrichment=True`, objective `""`, `misconceptions=["M_SWAP_ONE_SUM"]`.
- **T1B.C1 Swapping a Pair Changes Both Sums** (`ncert=["open.hint"]`): WE: group 1 is 2, 6, 9 (sum 17), group 2 is 4, 7, 10 (sum 21), gap 4. Swap a number from group 1 with a bigger number from group 2: group 1 goes up by the difference and group 2 goes down by the same difference, so the gap closes by twice the difference. To close a gap of 4 the difference must be 2: swap 2 and 4: 4 + 6 + 9 = 19 and 2 + 7 + 10 = 19. Also show a swap that does not work (6 and 7: sums 18 and 20, gap 2). Rule: to make two sums equal, swap two numbers whose difference is half the gap. Misconceptions M_SWAP_ONE_SUM, M_SWAP_BIGGEST.
- **T2.C1 Adding with Place Value** (`ncert=["fuel.facts","fuel.remember"]`, `textbook` quotes the fuel-quantities paragraph): WE 67 + 58 = 125: ones 7 + 8 = 15, write 5 and regroup 10 ones as 1 ten; tens 6 + 5 + 1 = 12, write 2 and regroup 10 tens as 1 hundred; hundreds 1: 125. Misconception M_NO_CARRY.
- **T2.C2 Easier Ways to Add:** WE 54 + 98 = 152 (98 is 2 less than 100: 54 + 100 = 154, take 2 back) and 63 + 30 = 93 (add the tens: 6 tens + 3 tens = 9 tens, then the 3 ones). Misconception M_FORGET_ONE. Say the plan's reading in `teach`: the book says "wherever possible, find easier ways" (section A6 of the priority doc).
- **T3.C1 Addition and Subtraction Are Linked:** WE: 35 + 28 = 63, so 63 − 28 = 35 and 63 − 35 = 28; and from 74 − 29 = 45 write 29 + 45 = 74 and 74 − 45 = 29. Rule: three numbers, one whole and two parts; any one sentence gives the others; use it to check. Misconception M_UNRELATED.
- **T3.C2 Subtracting with Place Value** (no `ncert` text id): WE 72 − 38 = 34: ones 2 are fewer than 8, regroup 1 ten as 10 ones, 12 − 8 = 4; tens 6 − 3 = 3; check 38 + 34 = 72. Misconception M_SMALL_FROM_BIG.
- **T4.C1 Consecutive Numbers and Their Sums** (`ncert=["cons.def","cons.boxes"]`): WE: sums of 3 consecutive numbers starting at 10, 11, 12 are 33, 36, 39. The difference between successive sums is 3 (each of the 3 numbers goes up by 1). The sums are odd, even, odd: they alternate. Misconceptions M_CONSEC_PARITY, M_SUCC_DIFF_ONE.
- **T4.C2 Pair the Ends** (`ncert=["cons.patterns"]`): WE: 41 + 42 + 43 + 44 + 45: 41 + 45 = 86, 42 + 44 = 86, the middle 43 stands alone: 86 + 86 + 43 = 215 = 5 × 43; and 52 + 53 + 54 + 55: 52 + 55 = 107, 53 + 54 = 107, so 214 = 2 × 107.
