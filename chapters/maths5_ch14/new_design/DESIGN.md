## 1. Hard rules (gates enforce most; fix content, never a gate)
- Class 5 students, 10 to 11 years old: short plain sentences. Characters and settings are the book's: Manu, Lali, Rohan, Ravi, Prem, Raju, Tinku, Jaideep, Golu (houses on the street map), the girl in the room, the Siberian Crane and the four little chicks, the ant, the zoo animals. The plan's own WE use the same kind of things. Do not invent facts or datasets: every number that is not a plain WE number comes from the book or from section 4.
- **No em dash and no en dash** in any string or comment (the book's dashes become commas, colons or "to"). Minus is U+2212 `−` (this chapter has none). Use `×` and `÷` (hardly needed). Hyphen only inside words.
- **Directions** are written in lower case in sentences: north, east, south, west. A compass letter is a capital: N, E, S, W. "Stop 1 (S1)" and "Stop 2 (S2)" as in the book. **Positions** are written `(column,row)` with no space, for example `(4,1)`, `(11,11)`. Never write "x" and "y" for them, never "coordinates" (the book does not use the word; say "position", "column", "row"). The word "grid" is the book's. A distance is `3 cm`.
- Only the four directions: never north-east, south-west and the like, never "clockwise", never "degrees", never "scale", never "kilometre". Say "turn right" and "turn left".
- Never write "NCERT", "textbook", "page N", "Fig.", "Figure N" or exercise numbers in anything the student reads (names, teach lines, steps, questions, tool text, image text). Say "your maths book". Exception: the `textbook=[...]` argument of `A()` quotes the book verbatim (stored as a `textbook_text` block), still no dashes.
- **Arithmetic must be true in every string.** The equation gate `eqcheck_plain` checks only whole-number chains (`3 + 2 = 5`, `5 + 3 + 2 = 10`). Re-compute every number you write (cm totals, station counts, steps east and north) with Python. Do not write an equation that is only approximately true. Keep whole-number chains on one line.
- 100% coverage: every id in `content.NCERT_ITEMS` (section 3) is attached to a concept: a `text` or `talk` id through `A(..., ncert=[...])` (or a WE's `ncert=`) or a DOER's `ncert=`, a question id as an `FIO` item of the DOER of its topic.
- **One video per concept.** A teaching concept `A()` has exactly ONE `WE` in `examples` (that WE is the concept video) with the PLAN's own numbers and objects (section 2): never the exact numbers of a book question (the book's questions are in section 3). The hook concept has `video_focus=["HOOK"]`, `enrichment=True`, no WE. DOER concepts carry a recap, their question items and at most one tool, no WE.
- **Questions.** Every book question is a `FIO(cov, label, question, steps, answer, kind=..., q_figure=..., s_figure=...)` in the DOER of its topic, in the book's order; `core/qt.py` moves each into its own concept of the "Questions: ..." topic that follows. `question` is the book's wording (copy it; blanks `___`; "textbook" becomes "maths book"). `steps` = the numbered working, one operation per line, a check as the last step; `answer` = short final answer (**at most 220 characters of its LaTeX form: keep it under about 170 plain characters**). Open-ended items get a labelled "Sample answer" built by reasoning and say "Your answer may differ" (in the answer if short, else in the last step). Add `q_figure` (one plain sentence: ONLY what the question gives, never the answer or a hint) when the question card needs the picture (almost every question here does: the street map, the metro map, the grid, the room), and `s_figure` (one plain sentence, may show the answer) when the solution card needs a drawing. Where the book shows a picture, say what the picture holds in words a drawer can follow.
- **Labels (`book`) decide the Questions topic** (`core/question_naming.py`): a label starting `Let Us Do` goes to "Questions: Let us do"; a label starting `Example N` goes to "Questions: Example" (the number is the label's own: Example 1 and Example 2 as given in section 3); any other label goes to a topic named by the text before its first colon, so labels that share a heading share a Questions topic and are numbered Q1, Q2 ... in book order inside it. Use exactly the labels given in section 3. A label must not contain a second colon.
- Names: DOER name starts with `Practice: ` or `Pull it together and do it: `.
- An idea topic has 2 to 4 primary concepts INCLUDING its DOER (last). The intro T1 is `topic_type="framing"` with `""` objective, all concepts `enrichment=True`: C1 hook (`video_focus=["HOOK"]`) and C3 a DOER with `role="try_it"` and the free-try tool (the framing topic holds exactly ONE video, the hook, and no book question). The concept video and the book questions of the opener live in the idea topic T1B. The summary topic is `topic_type="summary"`, one concept, no tool, no video_focus (there is no recap video).
- **Objectives** (`gates/objectives.py`): one per non-enrichment concept, the DOER concepts included; starts "Students ...", 15 to 40 words, a checkable verb (find, name, say, colour, place, trace, count, compare, write, mark, explain, steer, list). NEVER the words learn, know, understand, familiar. Each objective must share a key word with its own concept (use a word of the concept's name or of its `key_terms`). Never name a tool in an objective (no "Direction Clues", "Ant Path", "Bus Route Driver", "Metro Route Planner", "Zoo Grid Locator", "Face the Rising Sun"). Topic objective on each idea topic (at most 55 words), covering the vocabulary of at least half of its teaching concepts. Bloom words: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze.
- Misconception ids come from `content.MISCONCEPTIONS` (section 6); attach the relevant ones to teach concepts, DOERs and tools.
- `teach` lines: the first line is a prediction question ("Predict first: ..."), then the reveal, then the rule in one line ("Rule: ..."). `minutes` 4 to 8.
- Tool specs (`T(...)`): section 5. Exactly 5 to 7 challenges, exactly 4 hints.
- A kit, sheet or cut-out is not used in this chapter.
- Do not use words outside the chapter (`book_rules.json` banned_words).

Teaching concept seeds (the WE is the plan's own; these are the intended WEs, all checked in section 4):
- T1B.C1 finding directions by the Sun: WE: "Lali faces the rising Sun on the school ground. She then turns to face the direction her left hand was pointing to. Which direction is she facing now, and which direction is behind her?" Facing the rising Sun is facing east; left hand points north; she turns to face north; behind her is south (opposite of north). Rule: face the rising Sun and you face east; left hand north, right hand south, west behind. The compass rose has N at the top, E (in red) on the right, S at the bottom, W on the left. Textbook lines: "One of the ways we figure out directions is by facing the rising Sun." The talk item note.dir (naming the four directions, other ways such as a compass) goes into the teach lines as an invitation: say the four names aloud, a compass is another way.
- T2.C1 reading directions on a picture: on a picture with a compass, N is the top, S the bottom, E the right, W the left. WE (the plan's own playground, practice values): a swing at the top left, a slide at the top right, a sandpit at the bottom left, a seesaw at the bottom right, compass N at the top. Which is east of the swing (the slide), south of the slide (the seesaw), west of the seesaw (the sandpit), north of the sandpit (the swing). Ncert: bird.setup.
- T2.C2 directions from where you stand: the direction of a thing is said from a person or place: "in relation to". WE (the plan's own): a girl stands at the centre of a room plan, compass N at the top: a table above her, a bed to her right, a door below her, a cupboard to her left: the table is north of her, the bed east, the door south, the cupboard west. Then she asks: from the bed, in which direction is she? West (she is to the left of the bed). Rule: say what is in each direction while standing at the centre; if A is east of B then B is west of A. Misconception M_SAME_DIRECTION.
- T3.C1 reading a street map: a street map is a picture of roads and places seen from above; the compass says which way is north; a dotted line shows a route; roads have names; stops are marked. WE on the book's street map (the plan's own question, not a book question): "What is just south of the Basketball Court across Market Road, and what is just north of it?" South across Market Road: the Shopping Centre. North across the little road: the block with the pond and Jaideep's house. Ncert: trip.story, note.street. Textbook lines: "Observe the map carefully and help the children board the bus." (the bus picks up children from Stop 1 and Stop 2).
- T3.C2 turning right and left on a route: WE (plan's own): "A cycle drives north along a road and turns left at a junction. Which direction is it driving after the turn? At the next junction it turns left again. Which direction now?" North, left turn: west; west, left turn: south. Rule: a right turn changes north to east, east to south, south to west, west to north; a left turn does it the other way round: north to west, west to south, south to east, east to north. Misconception M_RIGHT_EAST.
- T4.C1 reading a metro map: WE: "On the metro map, look at Hauz Khas. Which two lines meet there, and how do you know you can change lines there?" The symbol at Hauz Khas is a capsule with a yellow dot and a magenta dot; the key says that symbol is an Interchange Line; the plain circle is a Metro Station. Yellow and Magenta meet. Ncert: metro.intro, metro.key, note.metro.
- T4.C2 changing lines and counting stations: WE: "Ride the Magenta Line from Hauz Khas to Kalkaji Mandir, change to the Violet Line and ride on to Badarpur. How many stations are between Hauz Khas and Kalkaji Mandir, and between Kalkaji Mandir and Badarpur?" 4 circles between Hauz Khas and Kalkaji Mandir; Kalkaji Mandir is a capsule (Magenta and Violet), so change there; 6 circles between Kalkaji Mandir and Badarpur; in all 4 + 1 + 6 = 11 stations between Hauz Khas and Badarpur (Kalkaji Mandir counts as one). Rule: count the circles between the two ends, a capsule counts as one station, do not count the two ends. Misconceptions M_COUNT_ENDS, M_CROSS_CHANGE.
- T5.C1 making a map with a key: WE (plan's own): "Make a map of a library corner seen from above: 2 shelves on the west wall, 1 table with 4 chairs in the middle, the door on the south wall. Make a key, and hide a bookmark with a red dot." The key: a long rectangle for a shelf, a square with 4 small squares for the table and chairs, a gap in the wall for the door, a red dot for the hidden bookmark. The hidden bookmark is under the table: from the door walk north to the table. Rule: every symbol on a map is explained in the key; mark the hidden thing with a red dot. Ncert: school.sample. Textbook lines: the book's key words (Gate, Road, Building, Tank, Hidden Objects, Wall, Fence, Seat/Bench, Path, Garden, Tree).
- T6.C1 distance and direction on a grid: WE (the plan's own, practice values): an ant at the anthill on a grid of 1 cm squares; a leaf 4 cm towards the west; a seed 6 cm towards the south; a berry 2 cm towards the east and then 4 cm towards the north (6 cm in all). Rule: each square is 1 cm; count the squares in a straight line for the distance and say the direction; a path with a turn is two or more legs. Ncert: ant.setup.
- T6.C2 finding the shortest route: WE (plan's own, practice values): a berry 4 cm east and 3 cm north of the ant. Route 1: 4 cm east then 3 cm north = 7 cm; Route 2: 3 cm north then 4 cm east = 7 cm; Route 3: 5 cm east, 3 cm north, 1 cm west = 9 cm. The shortest routes are 7 cm: they never go back. Rule: the shortest route goes only towards the food; a step the wrong way adds to the length. Misconception M_FEW_TURNS (the route with the fewest turns is the shortest: Routes 1 and 2 each have one turn, Route 3 has two turns but also the extra step; do not claim fewer turns is always shorter).
- T7.C1 reading a position: column and row: WE (on the book's zoo map; the giraffe is the plan's own example, not asked in the book): "Find the position of the giraffe. Start from 0, move east to the 8th column, then move north to the 9th row." The giraffe is at (8,9): 8 steps east, then 9 steps north. Rule: start at 0, first move horizontally east to the column, then vertically north to the row; write (column,row). The meeting point of column 1 and row 1 is (1,1). Ncert: zoo.setup, zoo.rule, zoo.hint. Say that the numbers are on the grid lines and the animal sits where two lines meet.
- T7.C2 the order of the two numbers: WE (the plan's own): "A child says the giraffe is at (9,8). Is that right?" The giraffe is at (8,9). (9,8) means 9 steps east and then 8 steps north: a different place, a spot on the path with no animal. The first number is always the column (east), the second the row (north). Misconception M_ORDER_SWAP. (Do NOT use (4,1) and (1,4) in the WE: they are in the book's questions.)
- T8.C1 revision: one line per idea, each with a short check using chapter content, all numbers from section 4 or the WEs. Ncert: summary.
