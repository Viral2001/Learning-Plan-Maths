## SECTION A: Chapter Understanding

### A1. Chapter name
Maps and Locations (Class 5, Maths Mela, Chapter 14).

### A2. Main learning objectives
Students will be able to:
1. Find east by facing the rising Sun, and name the direction on the left hand, the right hand and behind (north, south, west), using the compass rose.
2. Read directions on a picture that has a compass: say which object is to the north, south, east or west of another object or of a person.
3. Read a street map: roads and places seen from above, the compass, a route drawn as a dotted line, and the direction the bus drives after each right or left turn.
4. Read a metro map: coloured lines are routes, circles are stations, a capsule is an interchange where one can change lines; trace routes and count the stations between two stations.
5. Make a map of a place with a key that tells what each symbol means, and use it to find hidden things.
6. Describe a path on a grid as distances (cm) and directions, and find the shortest route to a point.
7. Locate a place on a grid by its column and row, write the position as (column, row), and mark a position.

### A5. Natural learning sequence
Directions from the rising Sun (a free try right after the hook, then the rule) -> directions on a picture and from where you stand -> a street map and turning right or left -> a metro map, changing lines and counting stations -> making a map with a key -> distance and direction on a grid (the ant) -> position by column and row (the zoo) -> revision. This is the book's own order. Two small moves: the free-try tool comes straight after the hook and the concept video follows it (pipeline rule); the Let Us Do school map, which the book prints after the metro questions, stays after the metro topic, with the idea of a key.

### A6. Points the textbook leaves unclear (to confirm; the plan does not guess)
1. **Room picture (p. 172) is drawn in perspective.** The compass beside it has N at the top. The plan reads "up the picture" as north (towards the back wall with the window), "right" as east and "left" as west, and answers with the nearest of the four directions: window north (on the back wall behind the girl), bed east (on her right), cupboard west (on her left, a little behind), bedside table south (in front of her, a little to the right). The cupboard and the bedside table sit between two directions, so the plan labels this a "Sample answer" and says "Your answer may differ". The chapter teaches only the four directions, so no other direction names are used.
2. **Which table is the "bedside table".** The picture has a dressing table with a mirror on the left and a small round table with a glass and a bottle beside the bed. The plan takes the round table beside the bed.
3. **Bird Watching (p. 172):** there are 4 tents. The plan reads: west of the road is the tent on the left of the road (blue); closest to the lake is the tent just above the lake (green); north of the green tent is the tent above it, near the top (purple); south of the lake is the tent below the lake (yellow). The new tent to the east of the lake can be drawn anywhere on the ground to the right of the lake.
4. **"Make a drawing of your room" and "Write the names of the things placed in the following directions" (p. 173)** depend on the student's own room. The plan gives a labelled sample answer from a sample room of its own (a room with a window on the north wall, a bed on the east side, a cupboard on the west side and a door on the south wall) and says the student's answer will differ. To find north in a real room the plan uses the book's own method: face the rising Sun (east); north is on the left hand.
5. **Street map route (p. 174).** The red dotted route on the map has a few extra dashes near the parking area (a short piece at the left edge and an arrow below the parking) that the text does not describe, and the road the bus drives north on has no name. The plan uses only the route the text describes: parking area, north, right turn onto Hospital Road (east) to Stop 1, then right turn onto Park Road (south) to Stop 2. The bus stops S1 (at the hospital gate on Hospital Road) and S2 (beside the shopping centre, near the south end of Park Road) are read from the picture.
6. **(a) "east of Jaideep's house".** The houses to the east, across Park Road, are Prem's and Raju's. Prem's house sits a little higher and Raju's a little lower than Jaideep's, so neither is exactly level. The plan answers Prem's house and Raju's house.
7. **(b) and (d) ask the student to trace a route.** Many routes along the roads are right. The plan gives one sample route in words (and a drawing on the solution card) and says other routes may differ. For (d) the plan reads the nearest bus stop as Stop 1 (it is a short way from Golu's house; Stop 2 is at the far end of the map). No distances are printed, so "closer" is judged from the picture.
8. **(c) "closer to Lali's house":** Stop 1, read from the picture (Lali's house is at the top left, Stop 1 at the top of the map, Stop 2 at the bottom right).
9. **(e) "In which direction would Prem have to move to reach Stop 2":** Stop 2 is below Prem's house and a little to the left, so the plan answers south (a little to the west). The chapter teaches only the four directions.
10. **Metro map (p. 175) is a drawing, not a printed table.** The plan counts the circles drawn on each line and counts an interchange capsule as one station. "Stations between" means the stations strictly between the two ends, not counting Qutb Minar or Supreme Court. Not every station is named on the map; the plan uses only the names printed and calls the others "the unnamed stations". Counts read from the picture: Route A (Yellow Line from Qutb Minar to Rajiv Chowk, then Blue Line to Supreme Court) has 14 stations between; Route B (Yellow Line to INA, Pink Line to Mayur Vihar, Blue Line) 15; Route C (Yellow Line to Hauz Khas, Magenta Line east to Botanical Garden, Blue Line) 25; Route D (Yellow Line to Hauz Khas, Magenta Line west to Janakpuri West, Blue Line) 31; Route E (Yellow Line to Central Secretariat, Violet Line to the Pink Line interchange, Pink Line to Mayur Vihar, Blue Line) 21. Please confirm the counting rule.
11. **(b) Lali's suggestion.** The Magenta Line runs both east and west from Hauz Khas. East it meets the Blue Line at Botanical Garden; west at Janakpuri West (a much longer way: 31 stations between instead of 25). The plan answers Botanical Garden and mentions the other. Kalkaji Mandir is also an interchange (with the Violet Line) on the way, but no change is needed there.
12. **(c) "least number of stations" and (d) "best way".** (c) is the shortest of the routes the plan lists (Route A, 14 stations between; no route on the map is shorter). (d) is an opinion: the plan's sample answer is Route A (fewest stations and only one change) and it says the answer may differ.
13. **The key on p. 175** numbers 10 symbols (8 coloured lines, 9 Metro Station, 10 Interchange Line). Where two lines cross on the map without a capsule (for example the Airport Express Line and the Magenta Line) there is no interchange, so one cannot change lines there. The book does not say this; the plan reads it from the key.
14. **LUD school map (p. 176) is the student's own drawing.** The plan gives a labelled sample answer. The sample map in the book also shows a small square near the lower right corner that has no symbol in its key; the plan does not describe it.
15. **Ant grid (p. 177): "crawl along the dotted lines on the grid".** The grid is drawn with solid grey lines and the routes with red dashed lines. The plan reads the grid lines as the lines the ant may crawl on (otherwise there could be no "other routes"). Part (a), "2 cm towards the east", is printed already answered; the plan makes it Example 1. The grid is 11 by 11 squares of 1 cm. The apple is 3 cm east and 3 cm south of the anthill; the drawn route (5 cm south, 3 cm east, 2 cm north) is 10 cm, and the shortest routes are 6 cm (many of them, in any order of 3 cm east and 3 cm south).
16. **Zoo grid (p. 178): the numbers belong to the grid LINES** (the panda is where line 1 across and line 1 up cross), not to the squares. Positions are written (column, row), the east number first. The grid has 12 columns and 12 rows plus 0.
17. **Animal names.** The book names only the lion, elephant, tortoise, deer, panda and crocodile (and gives the tortoise at (4,1) and the panda at (1,1)). The plan names the other animals from the picture: toucan (11,11), tiger (2,6), flamingo (3,10), monkey (11,3), zebra (10,9), snake (1,4). "Deer" is read as the slim antelope-like animal at (10,6), the only deer-like animal in the picture. The animals at (8,9) (giraffe), (8,7) (a big cat), (1,11) (a parrot) and the hippo in the pond (6,8) are not asked in the book; the plan's own examples use the giraffe and the parrot.
18. **Q3 dots (p. 178).** The book prints "(3,8 (Orange)" with a missing bracket and "(6, 6)" with a space; the plan prints (3,8) and (6,6). It does not say what is at these six places; the picture shows open ground or paths there, and the plan does not name anything at them.
19. **"which way is west?" (p. 171)** has no blank. The plan answers it as the direction behind Manu, opposite to the rising Sun (east).
20. **"Notes for Teachers" (pp. 171, 174, 175)** speak to a teacher. The plan does not give a teacher a role; the discussions they suggest (finding directions with the rising Sun and a compass, reading the street map and asking questions about distances and directions, reading the metro map and what the coloured lines mean) are kept as talk items inside the teaching lines. The plan does not explain how birds find their way, because the book does not.

## SECTION C: Interactive Tool Specifications

Common to every tool (details in sections D and E): one activity at a time; Back and Next always in the same place; progress dots; Hint, Retry, Reset, and (where moving objects) Undo; specific feedback ("Turning right from north makes the bus drive east"), never only right or wrong; the map, grid or compass is the largest thing on screen; a compass rose card (N at the top, E in red on the right, S at the bottom, W on the left, as in the book) on every board. Practice values the plan adds are labelled "practice values"; every other number is the book's.

### Tool 1. Face the Rising Sun
- **Connected topic:** Finding Directions (the opening topic, free first try right after the hook; the concept video follows it). Page 171.
- **Learning objective:** Students find east by facing the rising Sun and name the directions of the left hand, the right hand and behind.
- **Why a tool:** the idea is a turn of the body. Turning Manu on the screen until the Sun is in front, then reading his hands, lets every student test it without being outdoors.
- **Environment:** the green hill field of the opener at sunrise: soft hills, a large bright Sun low in the sky, Manu standing with both arms stretched out, a compass rose card in the corner.
- **Objects:** Manu seen from behind in the middle of a circular field; the rising Sun fixed on the horizon; his left and right hands as two arrows; four empty direction tags on the field ring (North, East, South, West) that the student fills; a compass rose card that stays hidden until the practice cards are done.
- **Controls:** Turn left and Turn right buttons (a quarter turn each); tap a tag word (N, E, S, W) and then tap the end of an arrow to put the tag there; Check; Hint, Retry, Reset.
- **What the student does:** The hook's picture, the crane and the children, and the line "Human beings also keep track of directions. One way is to face the rising Sun." Instruction: "Turn Manu until he faces the rising Sun." (1) turn Manu to face the Sun and tag the direction in front of him; (2) tag the directions of his left and right hands; (3) turn Manu to face north and say which direction his right hand points to (practice); (4) turn him back to face the Sun and tag the direction behind him; (5) open the compass card and compare. The book's lines unchanged: "Manu is facing the rising Sun. That direction is ___. His left hand is pointing in the ___ direction. His right hand is pointing in the ___ direction." and "which way is west?" The student fills the tags; the concept video that follows names the rule.
- **Feedback and hints:** "The Sun is not in front of Manu yet. Turn him again." "Manu faces the rising Sun, so the tag in front of him is East." Hints: where is the Sun; which way does Manu face; which hand is on which side; bottom-out turns Manu to the Sun and puts the first tag.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the tags of this step; Reset returns Manu to his starting turn.

### Tool 2. Direction Clues
- **Connected topic:** Directions on a Picture (Reading Directions on a Picture, Directions from Where You Stand). Pages 172 to 173.
- **Learning objective:** Students find the direction of an object from another object or from a person on a picture that has a compass, and place an object in a given direction.
- **Why a tool:** the direction of a thing depends on what it is measured from. Moving a compass cross onto the lake, then onto the girl, shows this at once, and colouring or placing tents turns the clues into actions.
- **Environment:** three boards on the trip desk: the bird-watching camp seen from above (a curved road, a lake, four white tents), the girl's room, and a small playground (practice values).
- **Objects:** the camp board with the 4 tents, the road and the lake; a blue, a green, a purple and a yellow pen; a movable compass cross that splits the picture into four zones (north above, south below, east right, west left, the diagonals as borders); a clue strip; for the room board, the girl, the window, the bed, the cupboard and the bedside table, with N, E, S, W tags; for the playground board (practice values), a swing, a slide, a sandpit and a seesaw in a 2 by 2 pattern.
- **Controls:** drag the compass cross onto an object; tap a tent and then a pen to colour it; drag a spare tent onto the board; drag N, E, S, W tags next to a name; Check; Hint, Retry, Reset.
- **What the student does:** The compass card and the line "Facing the rising Sun is facing east." Instruction: "Put the compass cross on the lake. Which tent is to its north?" (1) cross on the lake: name the tent to its north and the tent to its south; (2) playground (practice values): cross on the swing, which thing is east of it; (3) cross on the sandpit, which thing is north of it. The book's items unchanged: the four clues of Bird Watching (colour the tents), "Now, draw a tent to the east of the lake."; Map of a Room: "Write the directions of the following in relation to the girl." Window, Bed, Cupboard, Bedside table.
- **Feedback and hints:** "The tent closest to the lake is the one just above it. That tent is green." "The window is on the back wall, above the girl: north." Hints: where is the compass cross; which zone is the object in; which clue is it; bottom-out colours the first tent.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry removes the colours of this step; Reset restores the board.

### Tool 3. Bus Route Driver
- **Connected topic:** Reading a Street Map and Turning Right and Left on a Route. Pages 173 to 174.
- **Learning objective:** Students steer a bus along a street map, say the direction it drives after each right or left turn, and name the roads and stops on its route.
- **Why a tool:** a turn is relative (right or left), the direction is not (north, east). Steering the bus and watching a read-out of its direction change after each turn makes the link.
- **Environment:** the street map of the book from above: parking area at the bottom, Hospital Road along the top, Park Road down the right, Market Road, the hospital, the children's park, the basketball court, the shopping centre, the houses of Lali, Rohan, Ravi, Prem, Raju, Tinku, Jaideep and Golu; Stop 1 at the hospital and Stop 2 at the shopping centre.
- **Objects:** the street map; a yellow school bus on the parking area facing north; a heading read-out "The bus is driving: north" with a small compass; road name tags to tap; a dotted red trail drawn behind the bus.
- **Controls:** Forward (one block), Turn right, Turn left (the bus turns on the spot at a junction); tap a road name tag; tap a house or stop; Undo; Hint, Retry, Reset.
- **What the student does:** The video's small map: a bus that goes north and turns left. Instruction: "Drive the bus north to the Hospital Road corner." (1) drive north, then Turn right: read the new direction; (2) drive to the Park Road corner and Turn right again; (3) a Turn left from north (practice values on the map's side roads); (4) name the road after each turn. The book's items unchanged: "The bus will start from the parking area. It will go north and then it will take a right turn onto ___ Road driving in the ___ direction to reach Stop 1 (S1)." "To reach Stop 2 (S2), it will turn ___ (right/left) onto ___ Road driving in the ___ direction."; then (a) to (e).
- **Feedback and hints:** "The bus was driving north. A right turn makes it drive east." Hints: which way is the bus facing now; is the turn right or left; which direction comes after a right turn; bottom-out turns the bus and shows the new direction.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry returns the bus to its last stop; Reset returns it to the parking area.

### Tool 4. Metro Route Planner
- **Connected topic:** Reading a Metro Map and Changing Lines and Counting Stations. Pages 175 to 176.
- **Learning objective:** Students read the key of a metro map, follow a coloured line, change lines only at an interchange capsule, and count the stations between two stations.
- **Why a tool:** the metro map is a network. Choosing the line to ride, changing at a capsule and watching a counter of stations makes routes comparable, which the book's questions ask for.
- **Environment:** a station information board with the Delhi metro map of the book, the key panel and a route card.
- **Objects:** the map with 8 coloured lines (Red, Yellow, Blue, Airport Express, Green, Violet, Pink, Magenta), the circles that are stations and the capsules that are interchanges; the key panel; a route card that lists the lines used and the changes; a counter "stations between"; a highlight that colours the chosen part of a line.
- **Controls:** tap a start station and an end station; tap a line colour in the key to ride along that line; tap a capsule to change line; Undo; Clear route; Hint, Retry, Reset.
- **What the student does:** The video's strip: Hauz Khas with the Yellow and Magenta lines. Instruction: "Find the capsule. Which two lines meet there?" (1) match the key: the circle is a station, the capsule is an interchange; (2) ride the Blue Line from Mayur Vihar to Botanical Garden and read the count of stations between (practice values: 5); (3) ride the Pink Line from INA to Mayur Vihar (practice values: 5 stations between); (4) tap the crossing of the Airport Express Line and the Magenta Line: there is no capsule, so no change. The book's items unchanged: "What do the different coloured lines represent?"; "Mark the Qutb Minar station on the Yellow Line and the Supreme Court station on the Blue Line."; (a) trace different routes from the Qutb Minar metro station to the Supreme Court metro station; (b) Lali's suggestion; (c) which route has the least number of stations; (d) the best way to reach the zoo.
- **Feedback and hints:** "You can change lines only where the capsule shows both colours." "Between Mayur Vihar and Botanical Garden there are 5 stations, not counting the two ends." Hints: which line are you on; where does it meet the next line; count the circles between, not the ends; bottom-out highlights the route.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the route of this question; Reset clears the board.

### Tool 5. Ant Path
- **Connected topic:** Distance and Direction on a Grid, Finding the Shortest Route. Page 177.
- **Learning objective:** Students describe a path on a grid as distances in cm and directions, and find the shortest route to a point.
- **Why a tool:** moving the ant one square at a time turns a path into a list of distances and directions, and lets the student compare two routes to the same food by length.
- **Environment:** the grid of the book: 11 by 11 squares, each 1 cm, with the compass; the ant at the anthill in the middle; laddoos, sugar, bread and an apple.
- **Objects:** the grid; the ant on a grid point; the four foods at the book's places (laddoos 2 cm east, sugar 3 cm north, bread 3 cm west and 2 cm south, apple 3 cm east and 3 cm south of the ant); N, E, S, W arrow buttons; a legs list ("3 cm east, then 2 cm south"); a length counter in cm; a dashed trail.
- **Controls:** tap an arrow to move the ant 1 cm; Undo; Clear trail; tap two routes to compare lengths; Hint, Retry, Reset.
- **What the student does:** The video's small grid. Instruction: "Move the ant 2 cm towards the east." (the book's first answer). (1) practice values: a leaf 4 cm to the west; (2) practice values: a berry 2 cm east then 4 cm north, read the legs and the length 6 cm; (3) practice values: two routes to a berry 4 cm east and 3 cm north, compare their lengths. The book's items unchanged: the ant to the sugar, to the bread, to the apple (fill the distances and directions), and "Identify other routes to reach the point where apple is located. Which one is the shortest?"
- **Feedback and hints:** "You moved 3 cm east and 3 cm south: 6 cm in all." Hints: which way is the food; how many squares; which turn comes first; bottom-out shows the first leg.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the trail of this question; Reset puts the ant back.

### Tool 6. Zoo Grid Locator
- **Connected topic:** Reading a Position: Column and Row, The Order of the Two Numbers. Page 178.
- **Learning objective:** Students read the position of a place on a grid as (column, row), find what is at a position, and mark a position.
- **Why a tool:** walking a marker east first and then north from 0 is what a position means; the read-out (column, row) changes as the marker moves, and swapping the order shows a different place.
- **Environment:** the zoo map of the book on a 12 by 12 grid with numbered lines, the zoo sign at the bottom, the compass.
- **Objects:** the zoo grid with the animals at the book's positions; a marker that starts at 0; a read-out "(column, row)"; East and North step buttons; a swap button that shows what is at the swapped position; six coloured dots (red, green, blue, orange, black, pink).
- **Controls:** tap East to move the marker one step along the bottom, tap North to move it up; tap an animal to read its position; tap a position box to type a pair; Place dot; Hint, Retry, Reset.
- **What the student does:** The video's giraffe walk: east then north. Instruction: "Start at 0. Move east, then north." (1) practice values: walk to the giraffe, reading the pair as it grows; (2) tap the tiger and read its position; (3) practice values: type (9,8) and see that it is not the giraffe; (4) compare (4,1) and (1,4) with the swap button. The book's items unchanged: the way to the panda and to the tortoise; "What is at (1,4)?"; Q1 locate the animal at (11,11), (2,6), (6,4), (3,10), (11,3), (10,9); Q2 write the position of the lion, elephant, tortoise, deer, panda, crocodile; Q3 place dots at (8,3) red, (2,9) green, (7,3) blue, (3,8) orange, (8,6) black, (6,6) pink.
- **Feedback and hints:** "You went 1 step east and 4 steps north: that is (1,4), the snake." Hints: which number is the column; move east first; then move north; bottom-out moves the marker to the column.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry returns the marker to 0; Reset clears the dots.

## SECTION D: Shared Visual Language

- **Overall illustration style:** warm, colourful, polished educational-game illustrations; 3D or isometric where the scene holds objects, soft shadows, clear shapes, clean uncluttered compositions; gentle animation. The map, the grid or the compass is always the largest, clearest thing on screen.
- **Chapter theme and environment:** "The Trip Desk". Every tool is a board on one friendly wooden desk used to plan the school trip: a sunrise field board, the camp board, the street map board, the metro information board, the ant grid board and the zoo grid board. The trip is the book's own (the bus, the Qutb Minar metro station, the zoo). Nothing is added that does not help the mathematics.
- **Characters:** only the book's: Manu, Lali, Rohan, Ravi, Prem, Raju, Tinku, Jaideep, Golu, the girl in the room, the teacher at Stop 1 and the children at the bus stop. They set a situation, they never teach.
- **Colour and visual direction:** warm cream and wood tones for the desk; the book's own colours for the maps (grey roads, green parks, a red dotted route). The compass rose is the same on every board: N at the top, E in red on the right, S at the bottom, W on the left. Metro lines keep the key's colours. One colour per role: the thing being asked about is orange, correct is green, "look again" is soft amber (never red). The route line is red dashed, as in the book.
- **Main visual objects:** a compass rose, roads and places seen from above, a bus, coloured metro lines with circles and capsules, a grid with clearly numbered lines, a small ant, animals at grid points.
- **Animation style:** only meaningful movement: Manu turns in quarter turns, the bus turns on the spot and leaves a dotted trail, a marker walks east then north, the ant moves one square at a time. No decoration that moves.
- **UI layout and navigation:** one activity per screen; instruction strip at the top (one sentence, large); the working area in the middle; Hint, Retry, Reset (and Undo) in a fixed row at the bottom; Back and Next fixed at the bottom corners; progress dots under the instruction; the compass card in the same top right corner. Same placement in all six tools.
- **Common interaction patterns:** tap to select, drag to move or place, arrow buttons for turns and steps, a Check button that gives specific feedback, a three-step hint ladder with a worked step at the end, Retry for the step, Reset for the tool.

## SECTION E: Learning and Platform Rules

1. Videos explain; tools let the student explore, apply and get feedback. No classroom teacher is given a teaching role anywhere.
2. Every tool has one distinct mathematical purpose; no interaction is repeated across tools without a learning reason. Tool 1 turns a person and fixes the directions to the Sun; Tool 2 moves a compass cross between objects; Tool 3 steers a vehicle and links turns to directions; Tool 4 reads a network and counts stations; Tool 5 builds a path from distances and directions on a grid; Tool 6 reads and writes a position by column and row.
3. Each tool has three phases, in order: Recall and get ready, Play and apply, NCERT challenge.
4. A tool card that quotes the book uses the book's own questions, with their original wording, numbers and conditions. The plan does not change them and does not invent a challenge where the book has none.
5. Preserve the book's mathematics: its pictures, the 11 by 11 ant grid, the 12 by 12 zoo grid, the positions of the animals and foods, the roads and stops of the street map, the lines and stations of the metro map. Any number the plan adds is labelled "practice values".
6. Do not introduce ideas outside the chapter: only the four directions (no north-east and the like), no scale, no distance calculation on maps beyond adding the cm of the legs, no positions with negative numbers, no x and y axes.
7. One video per book question, on its answer, plus a question card and a solution card; one video per teaching idea; the hook comes first and does not explain the chapter.
8. Answers the book does not print are worked out from the chapter's own ideas and shown as the plan's reading (section A6). Open answers are labelled "Sample answer" and say "Your answer may differ".
9. Text hygiene: no em or en dash; minus is the true minus sign; students never read "NCERT", "textbook", page numbers or exercise numbers; say "your maths book".
10. Instructions are short, age-appropriate, one at a time; feedback is specific; hint, retry and reset are always present.
11. One visual identity (the trip desk) and one navigation layout across all tools.
12. Verify page numbers, figures and numbers against the book's page images before any build.
