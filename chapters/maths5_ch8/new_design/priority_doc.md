## SECTION A: Chapter Understanding

### A1. Chapter name
Weight and Capacity (Class 5, Maths Mela, Chapter 8).

### A2. Main learning objectives
Students will be able to:
1. Judge whether a recorded weight looks right for an object by deciding if it is a light thing (grams) or a heavy thing (kilograms).
2. Read weighing scales that have a needle: find the weight at each label, work out what one small mark stands for, and read a weight such as 1 kg 800 g or 660 g.
3. Say that 1 kg = 1,000 g, change kg and g to grams and back (3 kg 500 g = 3,500 g, 5 kg 50 g = 5,050 g), and use a double number line for it.
4. Compare weights given in different units (1 kg 600 g and 1,700 g) with <, = and >, and order weights.
5. Use the milligram (1 g = 1,000 mg), the quintal (100 kg) and the tonne (10 quintals = 1,000 kg), and change between them.
6. Add and subtract weights in kg and g (regrouping 1,000 g as 1 kg) in the three ways the book shows, multiply and share a weight by a whole number, and find the cost of a weight.
7. Use a double number line to scale two linked quantities together (people and rice, kilograms and rupees).
8. Choose between litre and millilitre, change between them (1 l = 1,000 ml, 1 l 400 ml = 1,400 ml), read container scales, and compare, add, subtract, multiply and divide capacities.
9. Solve everyday problems about weight and capacity in more than one step.

## SECTION C: Interactive Tool Specifications

Common to every tool (details in sections D and E): one activity at a time; Back and Next always in the same place; progress dots; Hint, Retry, Reset, and (where moving objects) Undo; specific feedback ("500 g and 750 g make 1,250 g, which is 1 kg and 250 g more"), never only right or wrong; the mathematical object is the largest thing on screen. Practice values the plan adds are labelled "practice values"; every other number is the book's.

### Tool 1. Weight Check
- **Connected topic:** Does This Weight Look Right? (the opening topic, free first try right after the hook; the concept video follows it). Page 104.
- **Learning objective:** Students decide whether a recorded weight looks right for an object by comparing the object with a 1 kg block and reading the unit (g or kg).
- **Why a tool:** "does it look right" is learnt by trying: putting the thing on one pan, the 1 kg block on the other, and seeing the tag disagree with the balance.
- **Environment:** Anu's room, from the book's picture: the bed, almirah, sofa, fridge, a rice bag, a bucket and a water bottle, with a two pan balance on the floor in the middle.
- **Objects:** picture cards of the seven things on Anu's list, each with her recorded weight tag (40 g, 60 kg, 5 kg, 30 g, 1 kg 800 g, 650 g, 50 g); a two pan balance whose right pan always holds a 1 kg block labelled "1 kg = 1,000 g"; two bins "Looks right" and "Does not look right"; for play, three practice cards (a pencil, a school bag, a brick) with practice tags.
- **Controls:** drag a card onto the left pan (the pan tips: heavier, lighter, or about the same as 1 kg, shown by a plain sentence); drag the card's tag to a bin; Check, Hint, Retry, Reset, Back, Next.
- **What the student does:** The hook's picture of Anu's room and her list; the line "Anu's list may have a mistake. Put each thing on the balance against the 1 kg block." Practice values: (1) the pencil, the school bag and the brick against the 1 kg block: heavier or lighter? (2) pick the tag that fits: 6 g or 6 kg for the pencil, 4 g or 4 kg for the school bag; (3) sort three more practice cards into "grams" and "kilograms". The book's question unchanged and open: "Anu has recorded the weights of the items in her house. Check if she has recorded them correctly by putting a tick against them if they look correct." The student puts each of the seven things against the 1 kg block and drops its tag in a bin; the tool shows only whether the tag is more or less than 1 kg (1,000 g), never "right" for the borderline ones (bucket, bottle, bed). The concept video that follows names the idea.
- **Feedback and hints:** "The almirah is much heavier than the 1 kg block, but the tag says 40 g. 40 g is less than 1 kg, so it cannot be right." Hints: which pan is lower; is the tag more or less than 1 kg (1,000 g); is g or kg the unit for something this heavy; bottom-out puts the card in its bin.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the balance and bins for this card; Reset returns all cards.

### Tool 2. Scale Reader
- **Connected topic:** Reading Weighing Scales. Page 104.
- **Learning objective:** Students read a needle on four kinds of scale by finding the weight of one small mark and counting marks from 0.
- **Why a tool:** the needle has to be moved and the marks counted one by one to see why the same position reads 600 g on one scale and 1 kg 800 g on another.
- **Environment:** the weighing counter of the market lane: a round dial scale on a stand, with a plate above it.
- **Objects:** a round scale face, 0 at the top in red, labels round the face, small marks between labels, a draggable needle; a scale chooser with four faces: a 4 kg full turn with 40 marks (100 g a mark), a 2 kg full turn with 20 marks (100 g), a 6 kg full turn with 60 marks (100 g), a 1 kg full turn with 100 marks (10 g); a "count marks" button that lights the marks one at a time; a read-out with two boxes (kg and g).
- **Controls:** drag the needle round the face (it snaps to the nearest small mark); tap two labels to see the gap between them; tap "count marks"; type the reading in the kg and g boxes; Check, Hint, Retry, Reset, Back, Next.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of a dial with a needle past a label; the line "Find out what one small mark stands for, then read the weight." (1) tap two labels and say what weight lies between them and how many gaps (the tool shows 1,000 ÷ 10 = 100); (2) set the needle to a weight the card gives (practice values: 300 g, 1 kg 200 g, 2 kg 900 g on the 4 kg face); (3) read a needle the tool sets and type kg and g; (4) the same needle position, the 6th mark after 0, on two faces: on the 4 kg face (100 g a mark) it reads 600 g, on the 1 kg face (10 g a mark) it reads 60 g, and the student says why the readings differ (practice values). The book's six scales (a) to (f) unchanged: "Read the scales. Write the correct weight in the space given below." The student reads each of the six faces and types the weight.
- **Feedback and hints:** "The needle is on the 6th mark after 0, and one mark is 100 g, so 6 x 100 g = 600 g." Hints: where does the needle start (0); what is the weight between two labels and how many gaps; how many marks has the needle passed; bottom-out lights the marks and counts them.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry resets the needle for this face; Reset restores all faces.

### Tool 3. Kilogram and Gram Line
- **Connected topic:** Same Weight, Different Units (with the mixed weight conversions). Pages 105 to 106.
- **Learning objective:** Students change kg and g to g and g to kg and g by putting numbers on a double number line (x1,000 and divide by 1,000) and by splitting a mixed weight into its kg and g parts.
- **Why a tool:** the book's double number line has blanks to fill; moving number cards and the x1,000 arrow makes the link between the two lines something students do.
- **Environment:** the market weighing counter again: two display balances (one in kg, one in g) with the same bag on both; under them a double number line painted on the counter edge.
- **Objects:** the double number line (kg above, g below, equal ticks); number cards; an arrow card x1,000 from the kg line to the g line; a mixed weight card split into a kg box and a g box (7 kg 67 g); a "parts to grams" strip with the g box padded to three places; two bags on two balances.
- **Controls:** drag cards onto ticks (they snap); drag the x1,000 arrow; type the missing number; switch between "kg to g" and "g to kg and g"; Check, Hint, Retry, Reset, Back, Next.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of one bag showing 2 kg on one balance and 2,000 g on the other; the line "The same bag, two numbers. Complete the line." (1) fill a line with 1 kg to 1,000 g and an x3 arrow, then 4 kg, 9 kg and 16 kg (practice values); (2) go back: 9,000 g, 16,000 g to kg; (3) split a mixed weight: 6 kg 40 g, the g part needs three places, 6,040 g (practice values); (4) 11,205 g to kg and g. The book's items unchanged: "Match the bags that have the same weights" (six bags in kg and six in g), the double number line on page 105; "Shamim and Rehan observed someone buying sugar weighing 5 kg 50 g... Who do you think is right and why?"; "Complete the conversions by filling in the blanks" (a) 7 kg 67 g to (f) 12,042 g with the double number line.
- **Feedback and hints:** "5 kg 50 g: 5 kg is 5,000 g, and 50 g is 50 g, so 5,050 g. 5,500 g would be 5 kg 500 g." Hints: how many g make 1 kg; multiply the kg by 1,000; add the g part in its own three places; bottom-out fills the line.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears this line; Reset restores all lines.

### Tool 4. Weight Regrouper
- **Connected topic:** Adding and Subtracting Weights. Pages 110 to 111.
- **Learning objective:** Students add and subtract weights in kg and g by exchanging 1,000 g for 1 kg (add) and 1 kg for 1,000 g (subtract).
- **Why a tool:** the carry and the borrow are an exchange of something real; students see 1,250 g become 1 kg 250 g and then do it themselves.
- **Environment:** the grocery counter: two trays on the counter, a kg tray and a g tray, in front of Rathna's store shelves.
- **Objects:** two weights written as a kg part and a g part; a kg column and a g column; weight chips (1 kg bag, 500 g, 250 g and 100 g packets); an exchange box that shows "1,000 g = 1 kg"; a result strip.
- **Controls:** drag chips into the columns; when the g column reaches 1,000 g or more the exchange box lights up and the student taps "exchange 1,000 g for 1 kg"; for subtracting, tap "break 1 kg into 1,000 g" on the top weight; a mode switch add, subtract; Check, Hint, Retry, Reset, Undo, Back, Next.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of a kg|g column sum with a carry; the line "Put each weight in its column. Exchange when the g column is full." (1) add two weights where the g column makes less than 1,000 g (practice values: 2 kg 100 g + 1 kg 300 g); (2) add two weights where it makes 1,000 g or more (3 kg 600 g + 2 kg 700 g); (3) subtract where no break is needed (6 kg 800 g minus 2 kg 300 g); (4) subtract where a break is needed (5 kg 200 g minus 2 kg 800 g). The book's items unchanged: "Rathna went to the local grocery store... She bought 2 kg 500 g rice for daily use and 1 kg 750 g additional rice... How much total rice did she buy?" and "How much extra rice did she buy for household use than for the Pongal festival?" and the Let Us Do word problem on the 4 kg 500 g of sand taken from a sack of 10 kg.
- **Feedback and hints:** "500 g + 750 g makes 1,250 g. Exchange 1,000 g for 1 kg: 1 kg and 250 g." Hints: which column are you adding; is the g column 1,000 g or more; exchange 1,000 g for 1 kg and carry it; bottom-out shows the exchange.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the columns for this card; Reset restores the tray.

### Tool 5. Scaling Line
- **Connected topic:** Cost and Scaling. Pages 112 to 113.
- **Learning objective:** Students scale two linked quantities by the same number on a double number line, and find a cost from the cost of 1 kg.
- **Why a tool:** setting the multiplier and seeing both lines change together is the idea; adding the same number to both lines is the slip, and the tool shows why it fails.
- **Environment:** the grocery stall price board: a double number line painted on the board, with a price tag and a weighing pan.
- **Objects:** a double number line with two rows (for example people above, grams below; kg above, rupees below); a multiplier arrow x2, x3, x10 and a divide arrow (divide by 2 for 500 g of 1 kg, divide by 10 for 100 g of 1 kg); a given pair at the left and a blank at the right; number keys.
- **Controls:** drag the multiplier arrow and choose the number; type the missing value; a "try adding instead" button that shows the lines no longer match; Check, Hint, Retry, Reset, Back, Next.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of a double number line with a pair and an x3 arrow; the line "Whatever you do to the top line, do to the bottom line." Practice values: (1) 3 plates need 240 g of lentils, 9 plates; (2) 1 kg of rice costs 36 rupees, 4 kg 500 g (4 kg, then half of 1 kg); (3) divide: 6 kg cost 90 rupees, so 1 kg costs; (4) the "try adding instead" check. The book's items unchanged: "4 people need 500 g rice for a meal. How much rice will be needed for 8 people if they eat similar quantity of rice?"; "5 kg of tomatoes cost 73 rupees. How much will 10 kg of tomatoes cost?"; "Nitesh is a scrap dealer... (b) 20 kg iron, if he paid 200 rupees for every 10 kg of iron? (c) 10 kg plastic, if he paid 30 rupees for 5 kg of plastic? Make double number lines for answering (b) and (c)."
- **Feedback and hints:** "4 people to 8 people: x2. So 500 g x 2 = 1,000 g." Hints: what happened to the top line; do the same to the bottom line; is it times or plus; bottom-out puts the arrow and the answer.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears this line; Reset restores all lines.

### Tool 6. Measuring Jug
- **Connected topic:** Litres and Millilitres (with Big to Small, Small to Big). Pages 113 to 114.
- **Learning objective:** Students read the scale of a container, add capacities written in l and ml, and change between l and ml by pouring into a vessel marked only in ml.
- **Why a tool:** pouring and reading a scale is the real act of measuring capacity, and the vessel marked only in ml shows 2 l as 2,000 ml.
- **Environment:** the milkman's cart corner of the market lane: cans, jugs and a vessel on a table, with a tap.
- **Objects:** three measuring jugs (1 l with a 500 ml mark and small marks, 500 ml with marks every 100 ml, 100 ml with a 50 ml mark); a ml-only vessel with marks every 1,000 ml; a milk can; a pouring handle; the book's container groups; a unit chooser l or ml.
- **Controls:** drag the pouring handle to fill a jug up to a mark; tap a mark to read it; pour a jug into the ml-only vessel and read the mark; type the capacity in l and ml; Check, Hint, Retry, Reset, Back, Next.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of a jug with a scale and a number line of l and ml; the line "Fill, read, and say it in l and ml." Practice values: (1) fill the 500 ml jug to the 300 ml mark and read it; (2) read the 1 l jug at its 500 ml mark; (3) pour a 2 l jug and a 250 ml cup into the ml-only vessel (2,250 ml); (4) type 3 l 40 ml in ml and 5,030 ml in l and ml. The book's items unchanged: "Write the total capacity of the following containers in each blank" (the four groups of containers) and "Bhalerao's family orders 2 l of milk everyday. This family has a vessel marked in ml only. What mark will you see in the vessel corresponding to 2 l?" with the double number line.
- **Feedback and hints:** "The 1 l jug holds 1,000 ml, the 500 ml jug holds 500 ml: together 1 l 500 ml." Hints: which unit is on the jug; what is the top mark of each jug; add the l and the ml separately; bottom-out fills the jugs.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry empties the jugs for this card; Reset restores all jugs.

## SECTION D: Shared Visual Language

- **Overall illustration style:** warm, colourful, polished educational-game illustrations; 3D or isometric where the scene holds objects, soft shadows, clear shapes, clean uncluttered compositions; gentle animation. The scale, number line or jug is always the largest, clearest thing on screen.
- **Chapter theme and environment:** "Anu's Neighbourhood Market". One friendly lane in an Indian town: Anu's home at one end, then a grocery store (weights), a fruit stall, a milkman's cart and a petrol pump (capacity). Each tool is a counter or a corner of this lane: Anu's room (Weight Check), the weighing counter (Scale Reader, Kilogram and Gram Line), the grocery counter (Weight Regrouper), the price board (Scaling Line), the milkman's corner (Measuring Jug). Nothing is added that does not help the mathematics.
- **Characters:** only the book's: Anu, Shrenu, Shamim, Rehan, Harpreet and family, Rathna, Aarav, Ramiz, Muskaan, Khayal chacha, Bhalerao, Dev, Mary, Daisy, Kiran, Sam, Tina, Riya, Nitesh. They appear to set a situation, never to teach.
- **Colour and visual direction:** warm cream and wood tones for counters; the book's own object colours (rice cream, flour white, milk white in steel cans, petrol pump red and blue). One colour per quantity role: the quantity being asked about is orange, the rest is cream, kilogram and litre are teal, gram and millilitre are amber-gold, correct is green, "look again" is soft amber (never red).
- **Main visual objects:** a dial scale with 0 in red at the top, a double number line with equal ticks and the units on the left, kg|g and l|ml columns, jugs and beakers with scales, a 1 kg block.
- **Animation style:** only meaningful movement: the needle swings, the pan tips, 1,000 g bundle into 1 kg, liquid pours up to a mark, the multiplier arrow slides along both lines. No decoration that moves.
- **UI layout and navigation:** one activity per screen; instruction strip at the top (one sentence, large); the working area in the middle; Hint, Retry, Reset (and Undo) in a fixed row at the bottom; Back and Next fixed at the bottom corners; progress dots under the instruction. Same placement in all six tools.
- **Common interaction patterns:** tap to select, drag to move or fill, a stepper or arrow for the multiplier, a Check button that gives specific feedback, a three-step hint ladder with a worked step at the end, Retry for the step, Reset for the tool.

## SECTION E: Learning and Platform Rules

1. Videos explain; tools let the student explore, apply and get feedback. No classroom teacher is given a teaching role anywhere.
2. Every tool has one distinct mathematical purpose; no interaction is repeated across tools without a learning reason. Tool 3 changes units (a fixed x1,000 link), Tool 5 scales two linked quantities (any multiplier), Tool 4 exchanges 1,000 g and 1 kg, Tool 2 reads a scale, Tool 6 reads and pours capacity.
3. Each tool has three phases, in order: Recall and get ready, Play and apply, NCERT challenge.
4. A tool card that quotes the book uses the book's own questions, with their original wording, numbers and conditions. The plan does not change them and does not invent a challenge where the book has none.
5. Preserve the book's mathematics: its scales, its numbers, its tables, its units. Any number the plan adds is labelled "practice values".
6. Do not introduce ideas outside the chapter: no decimals (no 1.5 kg), no fractions of a kilogram written as numbers, no kilolitre, no metre, no ratio or proportion words, no percent. Words like "half" are used only to say that 500 g is half of 1,000 g.
7. One video per book question, on its answer, plus a question card and a solution card; one video per teaching idea; the hook comes first and does not explain the chapter.
8. Answers the book does not print are worked out from the chapter's own ideas and shown as the plan's reading (section A6).
9. Text hygiene: no em or en dash; minus is the true minus sign; students never read "NCERT", "textbook", page numbers or exercise numbers; say "your maths book".
10. Instructions are short, age-appropriate, one at a time; feedback is specific; hint, retry and reset are always present.
11. One visual identity (the market lane) and one navigation layout across all tools.
12. Verify page numbers, figures and numbers against the book's page images before any build.

### F2. Concepts and activities with no separate tool, and why
- **Comparing weights (p. 107) and comparing capacities (p. 116):** after both quantities are in one unit this is comparing whole numbers; the change of unit is Tool 3 and Tool 6, so a comparison tool would repeat them.
- **Milligram, quintal and tonne (pp. 108 to 110):** the book says converting g to mg "is similar to converting kg to g", and the quintal and tonne are two facts; a tool would repeat Tool 3.
- **Times as heavy and King's Weight (p. 109):** one multiplication or division; the video is enough.
- **Multiplying and sharing a weight (p. 112), multiplying and dividing capacities (p. 118):** the working is kg and g handled together, shown in the video; Tool 4 already has the exchange these steps use.
- **Which unit for capacity (p. 113):** the same judgement as Tool 1 (small thing or big thing, small unit or big unit); a second sorting tool would repeat it.
- **Adding and subtracting capacities (pp. 116 to 117):** the book says "explain ... as was done in the case of weight"; Tool 4's exchange is the same, so no second tool.
- **Let Us Do on the cost table, Let Us Solve (p. 118) and all drill sets:** each is a question card with a solution video and solution card; the multi-step problems use the double number line on paper, "whenever needed", as the book says.
- **Chapter Revision:** a text summary with short checks (pipeline rule, see section B note).
- **The word problems on pages 111, 115 to 118:** the Examples and Let Us Do items are question cards with videos, not tools.
