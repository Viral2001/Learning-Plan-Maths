## 1. Hard rules (gates enforce most; fix content, never a gate)
- Class 5 students, 10 to 11 years old: short plain sentences. Characters and settings are the book's: Anu, Shrenu, Shamim, Rehan, Harpreet, Rathna, Aarav, Ramiz, Muskaan, Khayal chacha, Bhalerao, Dev, Mary, Daisy, Kiran, Sam, Tina, Riya, Nitesh; the grocery store, fruit stall, milkman, petrol pump, scrap dealer, picnic. A WE or a tool practice card uses "practice values" (a sensible number the plan picks) and says so; never give a real-world weight that is not obviously true.
- **No em dash and no en dash** in any string or comment (the book's dashes become commas, colons or "to"). Minus is U+2212 `−`. Use `×` and `÷`. Hyphen only inside words. Rupees: `₹36`, the sign before the number, no space.
- **Units.** Write `kg`, `g`, `mg`, `l`, `ml` for the units after a number ("3 kg 500 g", "1 l 400 ml", "5 g", "250 mg") exactly as the book does; in a sentence say "litre", "kilogram", "gram", "millilitre", "milligram" when no number is next to the unit; `quintal(s)` and `tonne(s)` are always words. A mixed quantity is the big unit, a space, the small unit: `2 kg 500 g`, `3 l 40 ml`. Numbers of four digits or more have a comma: `1,000`, `12,500`, `200,000` (the one exception is the book's own `10035 g` in one question, see section 3). **No decimals, ever** (not 1.5 kg, not 2.5 l, not 12.5): write 1 kg 500 g or "1,500 g". **No fraction signs** and no ½ ¼: say "half of 1,000 g is 500 g" and "a quarter of 1,000 g is 250 g". Never glue a letter to a number except the unit letters above.
- Never write "NCERT", "textbook", "page N", "Fig.", "Figure N" or exercise numbers in anything the student reads (names, teach lines, steps, questions, tool text). Say "your maths book". Exception: the `textbook=[...]` argument of `A()` quotes the book verbatim (stored as a `textbook_text` block), still no dashes.
- **Arithmetic must be true in every string.** The equation gate `eqcheck_plain` checks only chains of whole numbers joined by `+ − × ÷ =` and (see below) can read a chain you did not mean. You must re-compute every fact in your files (every conversion, sum, difference, product, quotient, comparison, scale reading, cost) with Python before you finish. Do not write an equation that is only approximately true.
  **Trap, found by reading `gates/eqcheck_plain.py`:** the gate reads `N op M = R` where the numbers are NOT followed by a letter. A unit after an operand hides that operand from the chain but the TAIL of the chain is still read: `3 kg × 4 = 12 kg` is read as `4 = 12` and FAILS; `1,000 g ÷ 4 = 250 g` is read as `4 = 250` and FAILS; `2 l × 5 = 10 l` fails the same way. Write a chain either with no unit anywhere before its last `=` (`3 × 4 = 12, so 12 kg`; `1,000 ÷ 4 = 250 g`; `5,200 − 2,800 = 2,400 g`) or in words ("3 kg shared 4 times is 12 kg"). A chain whose sides carry units on the numbers themselves (`500 g + 750 g = 1,250 g`, `1 kg 800 g = 1,800 g`) is skipped by the gate, which is fine, but you still verify it. Test every string of your files with the gate's own function (see section 7).

Teaching concept seeds (the WE is the plan's own, practice values; all are free of the book's number sets and all verified):
- T1B.C1 gram or kilogram: a pencil is 6 g or 6 kg? A school bag is 4 g or 4 kg? 1 kg = 1,000 g, so 6 kg = 6 × 1,000 = 6,000 g (far too heavy for a pencil); 4 kg = 4 × 1,000 = 4,000 g. Rule: grams for light things, kilograms for heavy things; 1 kg = 1,000 g; the number AND the unit tell the weight (M_UNIT_SIZE).
- T2.C1 reading a needle (face: 0 at the top, a full turn is 5 kg, labels 1 kg to 5 kg, 5 equal gaps between labels): one gap = 1,000 ÷ 5 = 200 g; needle on the 3rd mark after the 3 kg label: 3 kg + 3 × 200 g = 3,000 g + 600 g = 3 kg 600 g. Rule: start at 0, find the last label the needle has passed, then count the marks after it.
- T2.C2 what one mark stands for (face: full turn 2 kg, labels at 0, 500 g, 1 kg, 1 kg 500 g, 10 equal gaps between labels): one mark = 500 ÷ 10 = 50 g; needle on the 7th mark after the 1 kg 500 g label: 1,500 + 7 × 50 = 1,850 g = 1 kg 850 g. Rule: one mark = the weight between two labels ÷ the number of gaps; the same needle spot reads differently on different faces (M_MARK_IS_ONE, M_NEAR_LABEL).
- T3.C1 kilograms and grams: double number line, 1 kg = 1,000 g, 4 kg = 4 × 1,000 = 4,000 g, 16 kg = 16,000 g, 9,000 g = 9,000 ÷ 1,000 = 9 kg. Rule: kg to g × 1,000, g to kg ÷ 1,000 (M_KG_100G).
- T3.C2 mixed weights: 6 kg 40 g = 6,000 g + 40 g = 6,040 g (the g part is 040, three places; not 6,400 g); 11,205 g = 11,000 g + 205 g = 11 kg 205 g. Rule: kg to g, then add the g part with three places; back: take out whole thousands (M_PART_PLACE).
- T4.C1 comparing: 4 kg 200 g = 4,200 g and 4,020 g: 4,200 > 4,020; 1 kg 250 g + 750 g = 1,250 + 750 = 2,000 g = 2 kg, so it equals 2 kg. Rule: change both to the same unit, then compare as numbers; add or subtract first if the weight is a sum (M_BIG_NUMBER). Ordering: put the weights in one unit and order the numbers.
- T5.C1 milligram: 1 g = 1,000 mg; 8 g = 8 × 1,000 = 8,000 mg; 2 g 40 mg = 2,000 + 40 = 2,040 mg; 6,300 mg = 6 g 300 mg. Rule: g to mg × 1,000, same as kg to g (M_QUINTAL, M_PART_PLACE).
- T5.C2 quintal and tonne: 100 kg = 1 quintal; 10 quintals = 1 tonne = 1,000 kg; 700 kg = 700 ÷ 100 = 7 quintals; 3 tonnes = 3 × 1,000 = 3,000 kg; 20 quintals = 20 ÷ 10 = 2 tonnes = 2,000 kg. Rule: kg to quintal ÷ 100, quintal to tonne ÷ 10, tonne to kg × 1,000 (M_QUINTAL).
- T5.C3 times as heavy: a load 6 times as heavy as 3,000 kg weighs 3,000 × 6 = 18,000 kg; a load 9 times as heavy as another weighs 450 kg, so the small one weighs 450 ÷ 9 = 50 kg. Rule: n times as heavy: multiply to go up, divide to come back.
- T6.C1 adding: 3 kg 600 g + 2 kg 700 g: kg and g separately 3 + 2 = 5 kg and 600 + 700 = 1,300 g = 1 kg 300 g, so 6 kg 300 g; in grams 3,600 + 2,700 = 6,300 g = 6 kg 300 g; column with carry 1 kg (M_NO_EXCHANGE). Rule: 1,000 g or more in the g column makes kg.
- T6.C2 subtracting: 5 kg 200 g − 2 kg 800 g: take away 200 g from 5 kg 200 g (5 kg), take away 2 kg (3 kg), take away the other 600 g (2 kg 400 g); column: break 1 kg into 1,000 g, 1,200 − 800 = 400 g and 4 − 2 = 2 kg; grams 5,200 − 2,800 = 2,400 g (M_SMALL_FROM_BIG).
- T7.C1 multiplying: 6 sacks of 2 kg 500 g: 6 × 2 = 12 kg and 6 × 500 = 3,000 g = 3 kg, so 15 kg; in grams 2,500 × 6 = 15,000 g = 15 kg (M_ONE_PART).
- T7.C2 sharing: 9 kg 600 g shared equally by 3: 9 ÷ 3 = 3 kg and 600 ÷ 3 = 200 g, so 3 kg 200 g; in grams 9,600 ÷ 3 = 3,200 g = 3 kg 200 g; check 3,200 × 3 = 9,600.
- T8.C1 cost of a weight: rice ₹36 for 1 kg (practice value): 4 kg 500 g costs 4 × 36 = ₹144 for the 4 kg, plus half of 36 = 36 ÷ 2 = ₹18 for 500 g, 144 + 18 = ₹162 (M_PART_FULL_PRICE). Rule: whole kg by the cost of 1 kg; a part of a kg from 1,000 g (500 g is half, 250 g a quarter, 100 g a tenth of the cost of 1 kg, when the price divides evenly).
- T8.C2 scaling: 3 plates need 240 g of lentils; 9 plates: 3 to 9 is × 3, so 240 × 3 = 720 g; the same arrow on both lines; adding 6 to the plates and 6 to the grams would be wrong (M_ADD_TO_SCALE).
- T9.C1 litre or millilitre: capacity is how much a container holds; a cup of juice holds 200 ml, a drum holds 200 l (200 l = 200 × 1,000 = 200,000 ml, far too much for a cup); 1 l = 1,000 ml. Rule: ml for small amounts, l for big ones (M_UNIT_SIZE).
- T10.C1 litres into millilitres: a jug holds 4 l = 4 × 1,000 = 4,000 ml; a tank of 6,000 ml is 6,000 ÷ 1,000 = 6 l; 4 l poured into 200 ml cups: 4,000 ÷ 200 = 20 cups (big to small, then divide) (M_KG_100G, M_MIXED_UNITS).
- T10.C2 mixed capacities and container scales: 3 l 40 ml = 3,000 + 40 = 3,040 ml; 5,030 ml = 5 l 30 ml; a 2 l jug and a 250 ml cup hold 2 l 250 ml = 2,250 ml together; a container's capacity is its top mark (M_PART_PLACE).
- T11.C1 comparing capacities: 2 l 50 ml = 2,050 ml and 2,500 ml: 2,050 < 2,500; 600 ml + 700 ml = 1,300 ml = 1 l 300 ml, so it equals 1 l 300 ml (M_BIG_NUMBER).
- T11.C2 adding and subtracting capacities: 4 l 600 ml + 3 l 700 ml = 7 l + 1,300 ml = 8 l 300 ml; 6 l 200 ml − 2 l 900 ml = 6,200 − 2,900 = 3,300 ml = 3 l 300 ml (the same as for weights; 1 l = 1,000 ml).
- T12.C1 multiplying and dividing capacities: 6 jugs of 1 l 250 ml: 6 × 1 = 6 l and 6 × 250 = 1,500 ml = 1 l 500 ml, so 7 l 500 ml; 9 l 600 ml shared equally into 8 cans: 9,600 ÷ 8 = 1,200 ml = 1 l 200 ml; how many 300 ml glasses in 3 l: 3,000 ÷ 300 = 10 (convert to one unit first, M_MIXED_UNITS).
