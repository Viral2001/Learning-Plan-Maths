# maths7_ch4 - tool descriptions (6 tools)

Tool Name: Words to Expression Assembler

Tool Description:

A story card runs along the top of the screen. The first one says that Jowar roti costs 30 rupees a plate and pulao costs 20 rupees a plate, and that some plates of each were ordered in a day. Under it is a tray of tiles: number tiles taken from the story, letter tiles such as x, y, d, h and n, operation tiles for plus, minus and times, and a pair of brackets. Below the tray is a long empty assembly line. On the right is a Test panel with one number box for every letter and two value displays, one for the story and one for the student's line.

The student first taps the two things that change, the plates of roti and the plates of pulao, and gives each a letter tile, here x and y. Then the student drags tiles onto the line to build the total, for example 30, times, x, plus, 20, times, y. The tool also shows each product in its short form, 30x, with the number first. Pressing Test asks for a number in each box, say 2 for x and 3 for y, and works the story in numbers, 30 times 2 plus 20 times 3, beside the value of the line. The line turns green only when the two values agree for two different pairs of numbers. One pair is not enough, because with x and y both equal to 1 a line with the prices swapped still gives 50.

A wrong line is shown, not just refused. With the prices swapped, 20x + 30y, the numbers 2 and 3 give 130 against the story's 120, side by side. A number tile dropped right beside a letter tile with no sign between them is read as a product, so a 20 placed next to k reads as 20 times k. If the story adds k to 20, the test with k equal to 3 shows 60 against the story's 23. A correct line written in another order, such as y times 20 plus x times 30, is accepted.

The six stories are the two kinds of plates (30x + 20y); small bags of 12 sweets and big bags of 20 sweets; a number that is 5 more than d and a number that is 4 less than d (d + 5 and d - 4); hours worked at 50 rupees an hour plus a bonus of 200 rupees (50h + 200); 15 pages read each day for d days plus 10 more (15d + 10); and the money left from 100 rupees after buying n pens at 12 rupees each (100 - 12n). On the pens story, brackets round 100 minus 12 give 440 instead of 40 for 5 pens, and the test shows it. Hints come in order. First: what changes in this story, and what stays the same? Then: say the value in words first, then put the letter-number where the changing quantity goes. Then: which operation joins the two parts, a sum, a product, or less than? Last, the tiles are placed one by one to match the story, the test confirms it, and a new story follows. Through doing, the student learns that a letter-number stands for a quantity that changes, that each part of a story becomes a term, and that an expression is right only when it gives the story's value for every number tried.

Tool Name: Letter Slot Loader

Tool Description:

An expression card sits in the middle of the screen with a small empty slot under every place where a letter-number stands, so 7k has one slot under the k and 5m + 3 has one slot under the m. Beside it is a number dial for each letter, a working strip underneath, and a value box on the right. The first card is 7k with the dial waiting at 0.

The student turns the dial to the number the card asks for, here 4 for k. The number slides into the slot and the working strip rewrites the expression with the number in place, keeping a times sign between the number and the one next to it, so the strip reads 7 times 4. The student works the strip in the usual order and types the value, 28. A student who reads the two digits side by side as 74 sees the product 7 times 4 shown with its value 28 beside the 74. When the dial goes below zero the slot shows an empty pair of brackets, and the student must switch them on before working the line. On 10 minus a with a equal to -4, leaving the brackets off gives 10 minus 4, which is 6; switching them on gives 10 minus (-4), which is 10 plus 4, or 14. The two lines are shown one above the other.

The other cards test the order of working. For 5m + 3 with m equal to 2, adding first gives 5 times 5, which is 25, and the tool highlights the right order: 5 times 2 first, then plus 3, which is 13. For 3(m + 1) with m equal to -6, the bracket is worked first, -6 plus 1 is -5, and then 3 times -5 is -15. For 3s - 2 with s equal to 7, 21 minus 2 is 19, not 3 times 5, which is 15. For 2r + 1 with r equal to 8, the value is 17, not 28 plus 1, which is 29. After each card the dial stays live. The student moves it and watches the same expression give different values: 7k gives 28, 35 and 70 for k equal to 4, 5 and 10.

Hints come in order. First: which letter-numbers are in the expression, and how many slots does each have? Then: put the number in every slot, with brackets round a negative number. Then: multiply before you add, and work inside the brackets first. Last, the number is loaded into each slot, the expression is worked step by step to its value, and a new card follows. Through doing, the student learns that a number written next to a letter means multiplication, that the same number goes into every slot of a letter-number, and that the value of an expression changes with the number put in.

Tool Name: Like Term Gatherer and Equality Tester

Tool Description:

Coloured term cards lie on a table at the top of the screen, for instance 5c, 3c and 10c. Under them is a row of bins, each labelled with a letter-number: a c bin, a d bin and so on, with one bin for plain numbers when the expression has one. Every card carries its own sign, so a card such as -4n keeps its minus with it. A button under each bin adds up what the bin holds. On the right is an equality tester with a number dial for each letter and two value displays, one for the original expression and one for the gathered one.

The student drags each card into the bin that has its letter-number. On 5c + 3c + 10c all three cards go into the c bin. Pressing Add shows the working, 5 times c plus 3 times c plus 10 times c is (5 + 3 + 10) times c, and the bin turns into one card, 18c. A card with no number in front, such as the d in 4d + 6d + d, is read as 1 times d, so that bin adds 4, 6 and 1 to make 11d.

Bins protect against wrong joins. If the student drops 3a and 2b into the same bin, the bin refuses and shows the two letters a and b. A student who still adds them into 5 is sent to the tester. With a equal to 2 and b equal to 4 the original gives 14 and the 5 gives 5, so they differ. The tester asks for three different numbers, because a equal to 1 and b equal to 1 give 5 for both and look equal by accident. After gathering, the student runs the tester on three numbers and sees both values match each time. For 3p + 2q + p - q with p equal to 2 and q equal to 5, both give 13.

The six expressions are 5c + 3c + 10c (18c); 4d + 6d + d (11d); 18c + 11d, which stays as it is because each bin holds one card; 12n - 4n (8n); 3p + 2q + p - q (4p + q); and 6a - 2b - a + 5b + 3 (5a + 3b + 3). The student also answers whether each result can be simplified further. Hints come in order. First: which terms have the same letter-numbers? Then: put like terms in one bin and add the numbers in front of them. Then: a term such as d has the number 1 in front, and terms with different letters stay in separate bins. Last, the terms are gathered into bins, each bin is added, the tester confirms the values match, and a new expression follows. Through doing, the student learns that only terms with the same letter-numbers can be added, that adding them means adding the numbers in front, and that the gathered form has the same value as the original.

Tool Name: Minus Bracket Sign Flipper

Tool Description:

The workbench holds one expression with a bracket, for example (40x + 75y) - (6x + 10y): the money paid for x chairs and y tables on rent, less what is paid back when they are returned. Each term inside a bracket is its own card with its sign on it, so the second bracket holds plus 6x and plus 10y. In front of the bracket stands a minus, a plus or a number. Under the bench are three buttons, Flip, Open and Multiply, then a result line and a number test with a dial for each letter.

For a minus sign the student presses Flip. The cards inside turn over one after another, plus 6x to minus 6x and plus 10y to minus 10y, then the bracket and its minus sign drop away and the terms join the result line as 40x + 75y - 6x - 10y. The student can instead tap one card to flip only that card. The student then gathers the like terms, typing 40 - 6 for the x terms and 75 - 10 for the y terms, to reach 34x + 65y. For a plus sign, as in 2 + (x + 3), Open leaves every sign as it is. For a number in front, as in 4(x + y) - y, Multiply multiplies every card in the bracket by 4 to give 4x and 4y. The minus y outside the bracket joins as a term of its own, and gathering 4y - y gives 4x + 3y.

At any point the student tests the result against the starting expression with a chosen number. Both values appear side by side and the card that breaks the match is outlined. If only the first card is flipped in the chairs and tables bill, giving 40x + 75y - 6x + 10y, then x equal to 1 and y equal to 2 give 164 for the original and 204 for the student's line, and the 10y card that was never flipped is outlined. If Flip is pressed on the plus sign in 2 + (x + 3), x equal to 4 gives 9 for the original and -5 for the line. If Multiply reaches only the first card of 4(x + y), the y card is highlighted as unmultiplied.

The six expressions are the bill above (34x + 65y); 23p - 7q - (21p - 9q), Krishita's total less Charu's (2p + 2q); 4(x + y) - y (4x + 3y); 5 - (2 - 6z) (3 + 6z); 2 + (x + 3) (x + 5); and (6a + 9b - 18) - (9a - 6b + 14) (-3a + 15b - 32). Hints come in order. First: what is in front of the bracket, a plus, a minus or a number? Then: a minus changes the sign of every term inside, a plus changes nothing, and a number multiplies every term. Then: write each term with its new sign, then gather the like terms. Last, every term inside is changed as the sign says, the like terms are gathered, the test confirms the values, and a new expression follows. Through doing, the student learns that a minus sign before a bracket acts on every term inside it, not just the first, and that a plus sign and a number outside each have their own effect.

Tool Name: Grid Shape General Prover

Tool Description:

A calendar fills the screen in rows of seven dates, laid out like November 2024 and carrying on past 30 without end, so the student can scroll down as far as they like. A movable frame in the shape of the card sits on it, starting as a 2 by 2 square on 12, 13, 19 and 20. Above the frame are a sum line and a badge, and at the side is a list of kept examples.

First the student slides the frame to a few places, presses Keep example each time, and watches the diagonal sums. At 12, 13, 19, 20 they are 32 and 32, and on the square 9, 10, 16, 17 they are 26 and 26. The badge reads Pattern seen, not yet proved, because the calendar has endless squares and no list can cover them all. Then the student taps the top left cell and calls it a. The other cells take expressions in a. To the right is a + 1, below is a + 7 and diagonal is a + 8. Writing a + 1 for the date below lights up the date to the right instead, so the student can see the slip. When the frame is slid, the value of a changes but the expressions stay.

Next the student adds the expressions, a + (a + 8) and (a + 1) + (a + 7), and gathers like terms until both read 2a + 8. The tool then puts a into 2a + 8 beside each kept example, 2 times 12 plus 8 is 32 and 2 times 9 plus 8 is 26, and the badge turns to True for every position.

The six shapes are the 2 by 2 square (2a + 8); the cross of five dates, with 8, 14, 15, 16, 22 as the first case (5a); a row of three dates (3a); a column of three dates (3a); a 2 by 3 grid with the bottom middle date called w, whose six dates add to 6w - 21 (69 when w is 15); and a shape of the student's own, tapped cell by cell, whose sum must be written in a and compared with the dates of the shape. Hints come in order. First: what changes when you slide the frame, and what stays the same? Then: call one date a, a date to the right is 1 more, a date below is 7 more. Then: write every date in the frame as an expression in a and add them. Last, the dates are labelled in a, the sum is gathered into one expression, and the examples confirm it; a new shape follows. Through doing, the student learns that examples only show a pattern, and that naming one date a and writing the others from it proves it for every position.

Tool Name: Pattern Formula Finder

Tool Description:

The first steps of a growing pattern are drawn across the top of the screen, and below them is a table of step numbers with empty count cells. At the bottom are a Start box, an Added each step box and a formula line with the step number y, where the student places a start, a plus, an added amount, a times and a bracket of y minus 1. The first card shows matchstick triangles in a row, Step 1 to Step 4.

The student counts the sticks in each picture and types 3, 5, 7 and 9 into the table. The tool circles the jump between steps, 2 each time, and the student enters 3 as the start and 2 as the amount added. Then the formula is built from tiles: 3 + 2 times (y - 1), since Step y has y minus 1 lots of 2 added to the 3. A formula using y instead of y - 1, such as 3 + 2 times y, gives 5 at Step 1 against 3 in the table, and that cell turns red. A formula left unsimplified is accepted, and its simplified form, 2y + 1, appears beside it. Pressing Check compares the formula with every step in the table, not just the first two, and a formula that breaks at a later step is marked as not yet proved. Then the student asks for a far step: Step 33 gives 67, Step 84 gives 169 and Step 108 gives 217, with no drawing.

Five more patterns follow. Squares in a row start at 4 and add 3 (3w + 1, so 31 for 10 squares). A rope folded r times and cut starts at 2 pieces with no fold and adds 1 for each fold, so the table counts folds from 0 and the added amount is used r times (r + 2). Growing squares start at 5 and add 4 (4n + 1, so 201 at Step 50). A 4 column grid of numbers 1, 2, 3, 4; 5, 6, 7, 8 and so on has the start c in row 1 and adds 4 for each row down, giving 4(r - 1) + c, for example 124 in row 31, column 4. The last card lets the student choose a start and an amount added, draws the first steps as rows of dots, and asks for the formula and a far step.

Hints come in order. First: what is the count at Step 1, and what is added to reach the next step? Then: at Step y, the amount is added one time fewer than y. Then: write the start plus the added amount times (y - 1), then simplify. Last, the formula is built from the start and the added amount, simplified and checked on every step, and a new pattern follows. Through doing, the student learns that a pattern which grows by the same amount has a formula in the step number: a start plus the added amount repeated one time fewer than the step.

(tool id M3.S30.T30.C121.2D1, concept M3.S30.T30.C121, topic: Matchstick Patterns)
