## 1. Hard rules (gates enforce most; fix content, never a gate)
- Class 5 students, 10 to 11 years old: short plain sentences. Characters and settings are the book's: Tamanna, Gurpreet, Sameer, Sevi, Shami, Raman, Radhika, Maa, Baba, Dadiji, Dadaji, Raman's father; chocolates, chikki, parathas, pizza, grids, the fraction kit. The plan's own WE use the same kitchen things. Do not invent facts or datasets: every number that is not a plain WE number comes from the book or from section 4.
- **No em dash and no en dash** in any string or comment (the book's dashes become commas, colons or "to"). Minus is U+2212 `−`. Use `×` and `÷`. Hyphen only inside words ("one-third" is fine; write 1/3 as `1/3`).
- **Fractions are written `a/b` with a plain slash and no spaces** (`1/3`, `13/20`). A mixed number is a whole number, a space and a fraction: `2 1/2`. Never the characters ½ ⅓ ¼. Never glue a letter to a fraction: write "pieces of 1/6", "1/6 pieces", "three 1/4 pieces", NEVER "1/6s" (it converts to nonsense). "of" between a fraction and a thing is fine ("1/3 of the bar"). A blank inside a fraction is `___/10` (blank top) or `8/___` (blank bottom); a blank standing alone is `___`; a comparison blank is `1/4 ___ 3/4`. A part label is `(a)` before the part, once per part.
- Never write "NCERT", "textbook", "page N", "Fig.", "Figure N" or exercise numbers in anything the student reads (names, teach lines, steps, questions, tool text). Say "your maths book". Exception: the `textbook=[...]` argument of `A()` quotes the book verbatim (stored as a `textbook_text` block), still no dashes.
- **Arithmetic must be true in every string.** The equation gate `eqcheck_signed` checks only whole-number chains (`4 ÷ 2 = 2`, `2 × 3 = 6`) and SKIPS any chain that touches a slash, so fraction facts are NOT machine checked. You must re-compute every fraction fact (`1/2 + 1/4 + 1/4 = 1`, `5/2 = 2 1/2`, comparisons) with Python `fractions.Fraction` before you finish. Do not write an equation that is only approximately true. Keep whole-number chains on one line, and do not write a whole-number chain whose sides are different numbers of the same thing.
- 100% coverage: every id in `content.NCERT_ITEMS` (section 3) is attached to a concept: a `text` or `talk` id through `A(..., ncert=[...])` (or a WE's `ncert=`) or a DOER's `ncert=`, a question id as an `FIO` item of the DOER of its topic.
- **One video per concept.** A teaching concept `A()` has exactly ONE `WE` in `examples` (that WE is the concept video) with the PLAN's own numbers: never the exact number set of a book question (the book's printed examples are questions). Free choices: fractions with denominators 7, 9, 10, 11, 15, 16, 20, 24 or pairs the book does not use (4/5 and 8/10, 5/6 and 10/12, 3/8 and 6/16, 3/10 and 7/10, 3/4 and 3/10, 11/4, 8/3, 8/5, 4/5 and 9/7). The hook concept has `video_focus=["HOOK"]`, `enrichment=True`, no WE. DOER concepts carry a recap, their question items and at most one tool, no WE.
- **Questions.** Every book question is a `FIO(cov, label, question, steps, answer, kind=...)` in the DOER of its topic, in the book's order; `core/qt.py` moves each into its own concept of the "Questions: ..." topic that follows. `question` is the book's wording (copy it; fractions as `a/b`; "textbook" becomes "maths book"; the book's "1/6s" becomes "pieces of 1/6"). `steps` = the numbered working, one operation per line, values before combining, a check as the last step; `answer` = short final answer. Open-ended items get a labelled "Sample answer" built by reasoning and say "Your answer may differ". Add `q_figure` (one plain sentence: ONLY what the question gives, never the answer or a hint) when the question card needs a drawing, `s_figure` when the solution card needs one (it may show the answer).
- **Labels (`book`) decide the Questions topic** (`core/question_naming.py`): a label starting `Let Us Do` goes to "Questions: Let us do"; a label starting `Example N` goes to "Questions: Example" (the number is the label's own, use exactly the numbers given in section 3); any other label goes to a topic named by the text before its first colon. Use exactly the labels given in section 3.
- Names: DOER name starts with `Practice: ` or `Pull it together and do it: `.
- An idea topic has 2 to 4 primary concepts INCLUDING its DOER (last). The intro T1 is `topic_type="framing"` with `""` objective, all concepts `enrichment=True`: C1 hook (`video_focus=["HOOK"]`) and C3 a DOER with `role="try_it"` and the free-try tool (the framing topic holds exactly ONE video, the hook, and no book question: core/qt.py never moves questions out of it). The concept video and the book questions of the opener live in the idea topic T1B. The summary topic is `topic_type="summary"`, one concept, no tool, no video_focus (there is no recap video).
- Objectives: one per non-enrichment concept, starts "Students ...", checkable (a verb you could test: shade, find, make, compare, show, explain, circle). Topic objective on each idea topic. Bloom words: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze.
- Misconception ids come from `content.MISCONCEPTIONS` (section 6); attach the relevant ones to teach concepts and tools.
- `teach` lines: the first line is a prediction question ("Predict first: ..."), then the reveal, then the rule in one line. `minutes` 4 to 8.

Teaching concept seeds (the WE is the plan's own; these are suggestions, check each against the free list in section 1):
- T1B.C1 same whole: two rotis, one big and one small: half of the big roti is more than half of the small one, so fractions of different wholes cannot be compared; cut two rotis of one size and then compare 1/2 and 1/4 of them. Rule line: to compare two fractions the wholes must be the same.
- T2.C1 a fraction of a grid: a grid of 20 squares (4 by 5): 1/4 of it is 20 ÷ 4 = 5 squares; 1/5 is 4 squares. Split the squares into equal groups, one group is the fraction.
- T2.C2 small parts that join: a grid of 24 squares: 1/8 is 3 squares, 1/4 is 6 squares; two 1/8 parts shade the same 6 squares as one 1/4, so 2/8 and 1/4 name the same shaded area.
- T3.C1 make a whole with one size: 4 pieces of 1/4 make a whole; n pieces of 1/n make a whole (4/4 = 1).
- T3.C2 different pieces: pieces of different sizes make a whole too, for example 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + 1/8 = 4/8 + 2/8 + 1/8 + 1/8 = 8/8 = 1; a piece can be replaced by smaller pieces that cover it: 1/4 is covered by two 1/8 pieces, so 1/4 = 2/8: two names for the same part are equivalent fractions.
- T3.C3 how many small pieces fit in a bigger piece: on a grid of 20, 1/4 is 5 squares and 1/20 is 1 square, so 5 pieces of 1/20 fit in 1/4 (5/20 = 1/4).
- T4.C1 cutting parts: 4/5 shaded, cut every fifth into 2 equal pieces: 8/10; shaded area unchanged, the names change: 4/5 = 8/10.
- T4.C2 the pattern: 3/8 = 6/16 = 9/24: top and bottom are multiplied by the same number (2, then 3); the whole shaded area stays.
- T5.C1 same denominator: 3/10 and 7/10: the pieces are the same size (tenths), 7 pieces are more than 3 pieces, so 7/10 > 3/10.
- T6.C1 same numerator: 3/4 and 3/10: both take 3 pieces, but a 1/4 piece is bigger than a 1/10 piece (the whole is cut into fewer pieces), so 3/4 > 3/10. Rule line: same top, the bigger bottom number means smaller pieces.
- T7.C1 halves: 9 pieces of 1/2 = 9/2: 4 whole parathas (8 pieces) and 1 half: 4 1/2.
- T7.C2 fourths and thirds: 11 pieces of 1/4 = 11/4 = 2 3/4; 8 slices of 1/3 pizza = 8/3 = 2 2/3.
- T7.C3 top and bottom: 8/5 is more than 1 because 8 pieces of 1/5 are more than 5 pieces (8 > 5): 1 3/5; 3/10 is less than 1 because 3 < 10; n/n is exactly 1.
- T8.C1 compare with 1: 3/5 is less than 1 and 10/9 is more than 1, so 3/5 < 10/9.
- T8.C2 half as a reference: a fraction equals 1/2 when its top is half of its bottom: 9/18 = 1/2 (9 is half of 18); 7/15 is less than 1/2 (half of 15 is 7 and a half, and 7 is less).
- T8.C3 less or more than half: 5/12 is less than 1/2 (half of 12 is 6, 5 < 6) and 7/10 is more than 1/2 (half of 10 is 5, 7 > 5), so 5/12 < 7/10.
- T9.C1 relations: read a relation one link at a time: your father's father is your grandfather; your father's brother is your uncle; the plan's own example, "your mother's only sister's daughter" is your cousin sister (one link at a time).
