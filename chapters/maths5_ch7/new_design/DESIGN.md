## 1. Hard rules (gates enforce most; fix content, never a gate)
- Class 5 students, 10 to 11 years old: short plain sentences. Characters and settings are the book's: Nisha ({{NAME_1}}, cube question), Tanu ({{NAME_2}}, puzzle). The hook has no character: it is about woven baskets and paper mats. Do not invent facts or datasets; every number that is not a plain WE number comes from the book or from section 4. In topic and tool text write the real names (Nisha, Tanu), not tokens.
- **No em dash and no en dash** in any string or comment (the book's dashes become commas, colons or "to"). Minus is U+2212. Use `×` and `÷`. Hyphen only inside words ("3-sided", "four-sided", "one-third").
- Never write "NCERT", "textbook", "page N", "Fig.", "Figure N" or exercise numbers in anything the student reads (names, teach lines, steps, questions, tool text, image text). Say "your maths book". Exception: the `textbook=[...]` argument of `A()` quotes the book verbatim (still no dashes).
- **No degrees and no angle measures** (the chapter says "right angle", "equal angles", "corner", never a number of degrees). **No words outside the chapter**: see `book_rules.json` banned_words (degree, protractor, perimeter, area, volume, symmetry, symmetric, polygon, trapezium, trapezoid, hypotenuse, acute, obtuse, congruent, prism, pyramid, parallel, ...). Do not use them anywhere, not even in a tool screen text. Say "the longest side", "wide angle", "angle smaller than a right angle" if you must; better, avoid. Shape words allowed: triangle, equilateral, isosceles, scalene, square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram, kite, quadrilateral (4-sided shape), pentagon, hexagon, octagon, circle, diameter, centre, cube, net, face, edge, vertex (point where faces meet), icosahedron, dodecahedron, tangram, regular shape, tessellate, adjacent sides, opposite sides, right angle.
- **Arithmetic must be true in every string.** The gate `eqcheck_plain` checks only whole-number chains (`2 × 5 = 10`, `8 + 24 + 24 + 8 = 64`); anything else you must verify yourself (counts, comparisons, lengths). Do not write a chain whose sides count different things. Keep a whole-number chain on one line.
- 100% coverage: every id in `content.NCERT_ITEMS` (section 3) is attached to a concept: a `text` or `talk` id through `A(..., ncert=[...])`, a question id as an `FIO` item of the DOER named in the table.
- **One video per teaching concept.** A teaching concept `A()` has exactly ONE `WE` in `examples` (that WE is the concept video) with the PLAN's own numbers: never the exact numbers or figure of a book question. The hook concept has `video_focus=["HOOK"]`, `enrichment=True`, no WE. DOER concepts carry a recap, their question items and at most one tool, no WE.
- **Questions.** Every book question is `FIO(cov, label, question, steps, answer, kind=..., q_figure=..., s_figure=...)` in the DOER of its topic, in the book's order. `core/qt.py` moves each into its own question+answer concept of a "Questions: <heading>" topic after the idea topic. `question` is the book's wording (section 3; "(a) ..." parts keep their letter; blanks `___`). `steps` = the numbered working, one idea per line, ending in a check; `answer` = the short final answer, at most 220 characters in LaTeX form (measure with the real converter, see section 8). A question whose card needs a drawing MUST have `q_figure`.
- **Labels (`book`) decide the Questions topic** (`core/question_naming.py`): the text before the first colon is the topic heading; `Let Us Do ...`, `Example N`, `Exercise ...`, `Figure it Out ...` are special (none are used here). Use exactly the labels of section 3 (all are "other" category: one Questions topic per heading, headings differ per topic on purpose). Never put a second colon in a label; no comma-Qn endings.

### Teaching concept seeds (the WE is the plan's own; numbers fixed here are verified)
- T1.C1 hook: woven baskets with different weaving patterns; we will weave a mat with paper strips; ends on the open question "how would you describe the pattern?". No explanation.
- T1B.C1 describing a weave: a row is written as steps: "3 over, 2 under, 3 over, 2 under ..." (repeat). WE: a mat of 10 slits: the strip goes over, over, over, under, under, over, over, over, under, under: "Row 1: 3 over, 2 under (repeat)". The next row swaps over and under: "Row 2: 3 under, 2 over (repeat)". Rule: describe a row by counting the over and under steps, then say "repeat".
- T1B.C2 rows that repeat: WE of 9 slits, each row starts one step later: Row 1 "1 over, 2 under (repeat)" = O U U O U U O U U; Row 2 "1 under, 1 over, 1 under (repeat)"... write it as the row string UOUUOUUOU: "1 under, 1 over, 2 under, 1 over, 2 under, 1 over, 1 under"; Row 3 = UUOUUOUUO; Row 4 = OUUOUUOUU = Row 1. So the rows repeat after 3 rows. Rule: write the rows until a row matches Row 1; then the pattern repeats.
- T2.C1 regular shapes: WE: a square with every side 6 cm, a rectangle 6 cm by 4 cm, a pentagon with every side 3 cm: the square and the pentagon are regular shapes (all sides equal), the rectangle is not. Book rule: "Shapes that have equal sides are called regular shapes." Use "regular" ONLY for the equilateral triangle, square, regular pentagon, hexagon and octagon.
- T2.C2 fitting shapes around a point: WE with rectangles of one size (right angle corners): 4 fit around a point with no gap and no overlap (4 right-angle corners fill the space), a 5th would overlap. Rule: shapes fit around a point when their corners fill the space with no gap and no overlap; if the corners leave a gap too small for another shape, or overlap, they do not.
- T3.C1 two shapes take turns: WE: a border of 12 tiles in a row: square, triangle, square, triangle, ...: 6 squares and 6 triangles (6 + 6 = 12); at every join the same two kinds of shape meet. Rule: tiles of two shapes can cover a region when they repeat in the same order; at each meeting point list the shapes and compare.
- T4.C1 isosceles: WE: a triangle with sides 5 cm, 5 cm, 3 cm: two equal sides: isosceles; folded in half the two sides match and the two angles at the ends of the 3 cm side are equal.
- T4.C2 equilateral and scalene: WE: three triangles A (6, 6, 6), B (7, 5, 4), C (8, 8, 3): A equilateral (3 equal sides, 3 equal angles), B scalene (no equal sides), C isosceles.
- T5.C1 kites: WE: a four-sided shape with sides 5, 5, 3, 3 cm in that order is a kite (adjacent sides equal, side 1 = side 2, side 3 = side 4); sides 5, 3, 5, 3 in order are opposite equal: a parallelogram, not a kite.
- T5.C2 parallelograms and rectangles: WE: opposite sides 8, 5, 8, 5 cm: a parallelogram; if all four angles are right angles it is a rectangle (special parallelogram); a slanted one with the same sides is a parallelogram that is not a rectangle.
- T6.C1 seven tangram pieces: WE: sort the 7 pieces by number of sides: 5 triangles (2 small, 1 medium, 2 large) and 2 four-sided shapes (square, parallelogram): 5 + 2 = 7. All five triangles have a right angle and two equal angles; the square has 4 right angles and 4 equal sides; the parallelogram has no right angle.
- T7.C1 four shapes: WE: shape X all sides 6 cm and all right angles: square; Y sides 9, 4, 9, 4 and all right angles: rectangle; Z all sides 7 cm, angles not right: rhombus; W sides 8, 5, 8, 5, angles not right: parallelogram; the statement "opposite sides are equal" fits all four.
- T8.C1 two diameters: WE: a circle of diameter 10 cm: radius 10 ÷ 2 = 5 cm; any two diameters are 10 cm each; join their four ends: a 4-sided shape with opposite sides equal and four right angles: a rectangle; two diameters at right angles give four equal sides: a square (a special rectangle).
- T8.C2 joining points: WE: a circle with 12 points: join 1 to 5, 5 to 2, 2 to 6, 6 to 3 and so on until you are back at 1: the path is 1, 5, 2, 6, 3, 7, 4, 8, 5, 9, 6, 10, 7, 11, 8, 12, 9, 1: that is 17 lines. Rule: the points move on by 4, then back by 3, until the path closes.
- T9.C1 faces and a net: WE: a cube with faces sun, moon, heart, leaf, star and a plain face. View 1: top sun, left heart, right leaf. View 2: top sun, left leaf, right moon. The heart and the moon both touch the sun and the leaf, so the heart is opposite the moon. Rule: two faces that touch the same two neighbouring faces are opposite.
- T9.C2 counting small cubes: WE: a big cube of 4 × 4 × 4 = 64 small cubes painted on the outside: three faces painted: the 8 corners; two faces: 12 edges × 2 = 24; one face: 6 faces × 4 = 24; none: 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 inside; 8 + 24 + 24 + 8 = 64. Rule: corners, edges, faces, inside.
- T10.C1 puzzle: WE: Nisha puts 5 shapes in a line: a circle, a square, a triangle, a hexagon and a rectangle. Clues: (a) the circle is at the left end; (b) the square is next to the circle; (c) the triangle is between the square and the hexagon; (d) the rectangle is to the right of the hexagon. Answer: circle, square, triangle, hexagon, rectangle (unique, checked). Rule: place what is surest first, then use each clue to fill the next place.
- T11.C1 faces and corners: WE: a box (matchbox): 6 faces, all rectangles, opposite faces the same; 3 faces meet at every corner; 8 corners. Rule: count faces face by face and tick each one; a vertex is a point where faces meet.
- T11.C2 counting edges: WE: the same box: 6 faces × 4 sides = 24 sides; every edge is the side of 2 faces, so edges = 24 ÷ 2 = 12. Rule: count the sides of all faces, then halve.
