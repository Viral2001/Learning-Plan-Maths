## SECTION A: Chapter Understanding

### A1. Chapter name
Shapes and Patterns (Class 5, Maths Mela, Chapter 7).

### A2. Main learning objectives
Students will be able to:
1. Describe a weave row in words (over and under steps, "repeat") and weave or draw a pattern from such rules, and see when the rows start to repeat.
2. Say what a regular shape is and tell which regular shapes (triangle, square, hexagon, pentagon, octagon) fit round a point with no gap and no overlap, that is, tessellate.
3. Read tessellating patterns made of two shapes: name the shapes, list what meets at a point, and continue the pattern.
4. Make shapes by joining triangular pieces of a rhombus; tell isosceles, equilateral and scalene triangles by their sides and angles (paper folding).
5. Name kites, parallelograms and rectangles by their sides and angles; draw kites and parallelograms on a grid; count the sides of shapes made from 3 and 4 pieces.
6. Name the seven tangram pieces, compare their sides and angles, and make shapes from them.
7. Match descriptions with square, rectangle, rhombus and parallelogram, and see that one description can fit more than one shape.
8. Join the ends of two diameters of a circle and see a rectangle; make string designs by joining points on a circle and a design of overlapping circles.
9. Reason about cubes: which faces are opposite, how a net folds, how many small cubes are painted or removed.
10. Solve a clue puzzle, and count faces, corners and edges of the icosahedron and the dodecahedron without missing or repeating any.

### A5. Natural learning sequence
Weaving patterns (a hook and a free try, then writing rows in words, then rows that repeat) -> tiling (regular shapes, shapes round a point, patterns with two shapes) -> pieces of a rhombus (triangles, four-sided shapes) -> the tangram -> naming square, rectangle, rhombus and parallelogram -> circles (diameters, string designs) -> cubes -> a clue puzzle -> icosahedron and dodecahedron -> revision. This is the book's own order. The only changes: the kite folding (printed after Which Shape Am I?) sits with the kite and four-sided shapes (T5); the question that asks whether the squares-and-triangles tiles are equilateral sits with the tessellation patterns where it is printed.

### A6. Points the textbook leaves unclear (to confirm; the plan does not guess)
1. **The kit is on the book's Learning Material Sheets, not in this chapter's file.** The sheet "Tessellation and Shapes" (the book's back sheets, supplied in the file of the last chapter) holds: the rhombus cut into four triangles, the tangram, 6 regular pentagons, 6 regular hexagons (each cut into 6 equilateral triangles by lines from the centre), 6 squares, 6 equilateral triangles and 6 circles, all with sides of one length (a triangle, a square, a pentagon and a hexagon side are the same length: measured). Another sheet has the nets of the icosahedron (20 triangles) and the dodecahedron (12 pentagons), and there are a square dot grid, an isometric dot grid and graph paper. There is no octagon on the sheet (the book says to draw it), no paper strips, no cube and no cube frames. The tools show the sheet's pieces on screen. Only the paper strips and the 8 slit columns of the mat are practice values.
2. **The rhombus pieces.** The sheet prints the rhombus 457 wide by 748 tall (measured, ratio 1 to 1.64), the same drawing as the page; two equilateral triangles joined would be 1 to 1.73, so the printed rhombus is about 5 percent off. The plan reads it as two equilateral triangles joined (the four pieces are halves of an equilateral triangle: one right angle, longest side twice the shortest). Reasons: the Try This halves an equilateral triangle and calls the halves scalene; "each triangle has 2 equal sides" is true of the two triangles two pieces make; the kite, A and B in the pictures match. With the printed ratio taken literally, two pieces joined along the longer short side make a triangle with sides in the ratio 1.92 : 1.92 : 2 (nearly equilateral, not exactly): then question 2 would be answered "nearly, within what a ruler shows". All answers of questions 1 to 8 depend on this reading; please confirm it.
3. **Regular shapes.** The book says "Shapes that have equal sides are called regular shapes". A rhombus also has equal sides but is not drawn as a regular shape. The plan uses "regular" only for the equilateral triangle, square, pentagon, hexagon and octagon the book names.
4. **How many 4-sided shapes?** The book shows 3 possible shapes (a kite, A and B). Joining whole sides, 2 pieces make 4 different four-sided shapes (a kite, a rectangle, 2 parallelograms); sliding gives more. The plan says "at least 4".
5. **"How many different types of triangles can you make?"** is read for 2 pieces: 2 types (one with three equal sides, one wider with 2 equal sides).
6. **Squares and triangles pattern (p. 96).** Read on the dot grid, each triangle has two sides as long as a square's side and a shorter third side (2 grid steps against about 2.2), so the triangles are isosceles, not equilateral. The plan counts 2 squares and 3 triangles at every marked point.
7. **Which Shape Am I?** Statements 3, 4, 6 and 7 fit more than one shape. Statement 4 ("two pairs of sides are equal") is read to include the rhombus. A square is not listed as a rectangle by the book except in statement 7; the plan keeps the book's separate names.
8. **Play with Circles, "other than a rectangle".** The shape is always a rectangle. With diameters at right angles it has four equal sides: a square, which the plan calls a special rectangle.
9. **Tangram.** The sheet shows the same square cut into 7 pieces as the page (2 large triangles, 1 medium, 2 small, a square, a parallelogram): read from it. The four outlines on the page are drawn without a scale; the plan's sample answers use shapes of the same proportions and are checked by computer.
10. **Let Us Try (p. 93).** The picture is a design 21 squares across and 21 down; the dot grid printed beside it has 24 columns but only 18 rows of dots, three short of the design. The plan describes the whole design.
11. **Cube views (question 1).** The three views can all come from one cube only if the cream face with the red cross appears on two opposite faces (with a single cross face the third view is a mirror image of what the first two allow). The plan uses that reading.
12. **Cube frames (question 2).** Read from the pictures: a square tunnel runs through the cube from each face to the opposite face; only corner and edge cubes are left. Removed: 7, 32, 81.
13. **Puzzle.** With two squares and two triangles, "the square" is read as one of them in each clue; "between" is read as directly between. This gives one arrangement.
14. **Kites by folding.** The corners A and B are read as two corners next to the same corner, both folded in to the crease.
15. **Weave pictures.** The two designs of question 4 are read from the pictures cell by cell; "over" means the strip colour that shows on top. Design 1 repeats after 6 rows, design 2 after 4 rows.
16. **Circle design.** "Make a design exactly like the image" shows five circles of one size: one in the middle and four round it, each centred on the middle circle at the ends of two diameters at right angles. The picture was measured to read this.
17. **Icosahedron and dodecahedron.** The sheet holds both nets: the icosahedron net has 20 triangles and the dodecahedron net has 12 pentagons (counted on the sheet); corners and edges are standard facts (5 and 3 faces at a corner; 30 edges each).
18. The QR code on the opener page is not readable from the supplied file; nothing is taken from it.

## SECTION C: Interactive Tool Specifications

Common to every tool (details in sections D and E): one activity at a time; Back and Next always in the same place; progress dots; Hint, Retry, Reset (and Undo where objects move); specific feedback ("a fifth square would overlap the first"), never only right or wrong, never red; the mathematical object is the largest thing on screen; practice values the plan adds are labelled "practice values"; every other number is the book's.

### Tool 1. Mat Weaver
- **Connected topic:** Weaving Mats (free first try right after the hook; the concept video follows). Page 92.
- **Learning objective:** Students weave strips over and under the warp slits and describe a row in words.
- **Why a tool:** over and under is felt by doing it; the words come from what the student wove.
- **Environment:** the craft table, a coloured paper mat with slits, a tray of 8 strips (4 blue, 4 yellow).
- **Objects:** a paper mat with 8 slit columns (practice value), the eight strips as the book says (two colours), a row read-out in words.
- **Controls:** tap a crossing to set over or under for the strip in hand; "alternate" button; Next strip; Undo.
- **What the student does:** The hook's baskets and the paper mat; "Weave the first strip going 1 under and 1 over." Weave strip 1 (1 under, 1 over, repeat), strip 2 (1 over, 1 under, repeat), then all 8 strips; watch the read-out; try your own rule (practice values: 2 over, 1 under). Weave the mat and describe Row 1 and Row 2 in words as the book does ("We can describe the pattern of the above weave as follows").
- **Feedback and hints:** "This strip goes over where the last one goes under: the rows swap." Hints: what is Row 1; over then under; repeat; bottom-out weaves a row.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the strip in hand; Reset clears the mat.

### Tool 2. Weave Rules
- **Connected topic:** Describing a Weave (questions 2 to 4 and Let Us Try). Page 93.
- **Learning objective:** Students build rows from rule cards (count, over or under, repeat) and find where the pattern starts to repeat.
- **Why a tool:** many rows with long rules are slow by hand; the cards make the rule itself the thing students handle.
- **Environment:** a weaving frame of 16 columns on the craft table with rule cards on a shelf.
- **Objects:** a grid of crossings; rule cards "2 over", "1 under", "(repeat)", "(do not repeat)"; a row list; a target picture.
- **Controls:** drag cards into a row, tap Weave to colour the row, tap "Rows repeat from here" on the row that equals Row 1.
- **What the student does:** The video's row "3 over, 2 under (repeat)" on 10 slits (practice values) and its swap. Build practice rows from cards; weave 3 rows where each row starts one step later and find that Row 4 is Row 1 (practice values); say after how many rows it repeats. Question 3 (the four rules: weave them and say when it repeats) and question 4 (work out the steps of a design until it repeats); question 2 and Let Us Try are the practice questions of the same topic.
- **Feedback and hints:** "Row 3 swaps over and under in Row 1: 2 under, 1 over." Hints: read the first row; how far does each row move; compare with Row 1; bottom-out fills one row.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the row; Reset clears the frame.

### Tool 3. Fit Around a Point
- **Connected topic:** Tiles Around a Point. Pages 94 and 95.
- **Learning objective:** Students fit regular triangles, squares, pentagons, hexagons and octagons round a point and say which fit with no gap and no overlap.
- **Why a tool:** the book gives cutouts; trying each shape and watching the gap or overlap is the idea.
- **Environment:** a tile mat on the craft table with one marked point.
- **Objects:** cutouts of an equilateral triangle, square, regular pentagon, hexagon and octagon, all with sides of one length; a counter of shapes at the point.
- **Controls:** drag a shape so a corner sits on the point; it turns and snaps edge to edge; Undo; a "gap" glow in amber, an "overlap" glow in amber.
- **What the student does:** "Which regular shape did you meet in the video? Put it round the point." Fit triangles, squares, hexagons: count how many and see no gap; pentagons: 3 and a gap that is too small; octagons: 2 and a gap too small for a third (practice: a square fits that gap). "Can five squares fit together around a point without any gaps or overlaps? Why or why not?" and "Can regular hexagons (6-sided shapes with equal sides) fit together around a point without any gaps or overlaps? Try and see. How many fit together at a point?"
- **Feedback and hints:** "A fifth square would overlap the first: 4 squares fill the space." Hints: which shape; how many corners fit; is there a gap; bottom-out places one shape.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the mat; Reset clears the mat and the counter.

### Tool 4. Pattern Tiler
- **Connected topic:** Patterns with More Than One Shape. Pages 95 and 96.
- **Learning objective:** Students continue a pattern of two shapes and list the shapes that meet at a point.
- **Why a tool:** placing the tiles is the pattern; the meeting-point checker makes "same set" visible.
- **Environment:** a floor of dots on the craft table.
- **Objects:** an isometric dot grid and a square dot grid; cutouts of regular hexagon, equilateral triangle, regular octagon, square, and the tilted square and the isosceles triangle of the book's last pattern; four to five marked points; a checker "shapes at this point".
- **Controls:** drag, turn and snap a tile; tap a point to see its list; colour tool.
- **What the student does:** The video's border of squares and triangles taking turns. Continue a border; fill hexagons and triangles round a point; read the list at two points. "Look at the pattern given below. What shapes are coming together at the marked points? Are the same set of shapes coming together at these points? Continue the pattern and colour it appropriately." (octagons and squares) and "Here is a tessellating pattern with more than one shape. What shapes have been used in this pattern?"
- **Feedback and hints:** "At this point 2 octagons and 1 square meet, the same as at the first point." Hints: which shapes touch this point; count each; compare with another point; bottom-out lists one point.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the last tiles; Reset clears the grid.

### Tool 5. Triangle Check
- **Connected topic:** Triangles from the Rhombus. Page 97.
- **Learning objective:** Students sort triangles into isosceles, equilateral and scalene by measuring sides and fold to compare angles.
- **Why a tool:** a ruler and a fold on screen make "equal" something checked, not guessed.
- **Environment:** the craft table with a ruler and paper triangles.
- **Objects:** four triangles made of rhombus pieces (all sides equal; wide with 2 equal sides; one piece, all sides unequal; four pieces joined, all sides unequal); a ruler; a Fold button; three trays.
- **Controls:** drag the ruler along a side; tap Fold; drag a triangle into a tray.
- **What the student does:** "Look at the triangles you made from two pieces in the video." Measure the sides of each triangle, fold it, sort it; see that equal sides go with equal angles. "How many different types of triangles can you make? Now, observe and measure the sides of these triangles. What do you notice?" and "Now, cut the equilateral triangle in half. How many sides of each new triangle are equal?"
- **Feedback and hints:** "The two folded halves match: this triangle has 2 equal angles." Hints: measure one side; compare; fold; bottom-out names the tray.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry returns the triangle; Reset returns all four.

### Tool 6. Rhombus Pieces
- **Connected topic:** Four-Sided Shapes from the Rhombus (with the questions on 3 and 4 pieces). Pages 96 to 99.
- **Learning objective:** Students join 2, 3 and 4 triangular pieces side to side, count the sides of the shape made, and name it.
- **Why a tool:** the pieces are on screen and can be turned and flipped, which replaces cutting.
- **Environment:** the craft table with a rhombus that splits into four triangles.
- **Objects:** the four pieces (halves of an equilateral triangle, the plan's reading), a tracing sheet, a side counter, a name label.
- **Controls:** drag, turn, flip; pieces snap whole side to whole side; Trace saves the shape; a gallery.
- **What the student does:** The video's kite and parallelogram. Join 2 pieces to make a kite, a rectangle and a parallelogram and count sides; then 3 pieces. "Using 3 triangular pieces of the rhombus, try creating a (a) 3-sided shape, (b) 4-sided shape, and (c) 5-sided shape." and "Which of these shapes can be made with all 4 pieces? Try and find out."
- **Feedback and hints:** "Two sides joined inside, so the outside has 4 sides." Hints: which sides touch; count the outside; flip a piece; bottom-out shows one join.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry separates the last piece; Reset returns the rhombus.

### Tool 7. Tangram Maker
- **Connected topic:** Tangram. Page 99.
- **Learning objective:** Students name the seven pieces and make target shapes from some or all of them.
- **Why a tool:** a puzzle of fitting pieces needs moving and turning them.
- **Environment:** a wooden tangram tray on the craft table.
- **Objects:** the seven pieces (2 large triangles, 1 medium, 2 small, a square, a parallelogram) and four outlines.
- **Controls:** drag, turn, flip; snap to the outline; Check; a name label per piece.
- **What the student does:** The video's sorting of the pieces by sides. Name each piece; make a square from two small triangles; fill the triangle outline. "Now, use some or all of the pieces of your tangram set to make the following shapes. There may be more than one way to do it."
- **Feedback and hints:** "These pieces fit the rectangle: more than one way is allowed." Hints: biggest corner; which piece fits it; turn; bottom-out places one piece.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the outline; Reset returns the pieces.

### Tool 8. Shape Detective
- **Connected topic:** Which Shape Am I? Page 100.
- **Learning objective:** Students change a four-sided shape and see which statements about sides and angles fit it.
- **Why a tool:** dragging a corner shows how one description fits more than one shape.
- **Environment:** a detective's board on the craft table with seven statement cards.
- **Objects:** a four-sided shape with draggable corners and two locks ("opposite sides equal", "right angles"), seven statement cards, four shape cards.
- **Controls:** drag a corner, switch a lock, drag a statement to a shape.
- **What the student does:** The video's four shapes. Lock sides then angles and see square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram; test statements. "Match the statements with appropriate shapes. Do some of them describe more than one shape?"
- **Feedback and hints:** "A rectangle also has opposite angles equal: statement 7 fits it too." Hints: read the statement; which locks; try the shape; bottom-out shows one match.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the last match; Reset clears all.

### Tool 9. Circle Stitcher
- **Connected topic:** Play with Circles (with Circle Designs). Page 101.
- **Learning objective:** Students draw two diameters, join the ends and see a rectangle, then thread the 24 points to make a string design.
- **Why a tool:** drawing and threading are the activity; the checker shows sides and angles.
- **Environment:** a compass board and a thread board on the craft table.
- **Objects:** a circle with a centre; two diameters; four points; a side and angle read-out; a circle with 24 points; coloured threads; five circles of one size.
- **Controls:** drag a diameter end to turn it; join ends; tap points in order; a compass opening drag.
- **What the student does:** The video's 12-point circle. Draw two diameters and join; turn one diameter; thread the 12-point practice circle. "Is it possible to create a 4-sided shape other than a rectangle through this process?" and "Join points 1 to 11, 11 to 2, 2 to 12, and so on till you reach back at 1."
- **Feedback and hints:** "Opposite sides are equal and all four angles are right angles: a rectangle." Hints: where do the diameters meet; check sides; check angles; bottom-out threads two lines.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry removes the last line; Reset clears the board.

### Tool 10. Cube Lab
- **Connected topic:** Cube Connections. Page 102.
- **Learning objective:** Students fold a net, say which faces are opposite, and count painted and removed small cubes.
- **Why a tool:** a cube can be turned and a net folded on screen; 27 small cubes can be peeled apart.
- **Environment:** the craft table with a cube, a net and small cubes.
- **Objects:** a cube with a picture on each face; three view cards; a cross-shaped net of 6 squares; a 3 by 3 by 3 stack; frames.
- **Controls:** turn the cube; drop a picture on a net square; Fold; tap small cubes to paint or remove.
- **What the student does:** The video's sun, moon, heart and leaf views. Find the face opposite the heart; fold a net; paint a 4 by 4 by 4 stack (practice values). "Here are three views of a cube. Can you draw them on the net in the correct order?" and "Nisha has glued 27 small cubes together to make a large solid cube. She paints the large cube red. How many of the original small cubes have (a) three faces painted red, (b) two faces, (c) one face, (d) no faces?"
- **Feedback and hints:** "Both the heart and the moon touch the sun and the leaf: they are opposite." Hints: which faces touch; which are opposite; fold; bottom-out places one picture.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry unfolds; Reset clears.

### Tool 11. Solid Counter
- **Connected topic:** Icosahedron and Dodecahedron. Page 103.
- **Learning objective:** Students count faces, corners and edges of two solids without missing or repeating any.
- **Why a tool:** tapping each item once and seeing the count rise is the method.
- **Environment:** a turntable on the craft table with clay models.
- **Objects:** an icosahedron and a dodecahedron that turn; tap marks; counters for faces, corners, edges; a "sides of faces" tally.
- **Controls:** drag to turn; tap a face, a corner or an edge to tick it; a Tally button.
- **What the student does:** The video's box. Tick the faces of a box; count its edges by faces; switch to the two solids. "How many faces meet at a vertex (point)?" and "How many edges do you see? How did you count them such that you do not miss out any edge or count an edge twice?"
- **Feedback and hints:** "20 faces with 3 sides is 60; every edge is counted twice, so 30 edges." Hints: tick one face; count sides; halve; bottom-out ticks one edge.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the ticks; Reset clears the solid.

## SECTION D: Shared Visual Language

- **Overall illustration style:** warm, colourful, polished educational-game illustrations; 3D or isometric where the scene holds objects, soft shadows, clear shapes, clean uncluttered compositions; gentle animation. The shape or pattern is always the largest, clearest thing on screen.
- **Chapter theme and environment:** "The Craft Table". Every activity of the book sits on one friendly wooden craft table: coloured paper and strips, tiles, cut-out pieces, thread and clay. Each tool is a station on that table: weaving frame, tile mat, dot floor, triangle tray, rhombus pieces, tangram tray, detective board, compass and thread boards, cube and net, turntable. Baskets and woven mats are an Indian craft and open the chapter; nothing else is added.
- **Characters:** only the book's: Nisha and Tanu; the opener's girl is not named. They appear to set a situation, never to teach.
- **Colour and visual direction:** warm cream and wood tones for the table; one colour per role: the shape being asked about is orange, the rest pale, correct is green, "look again" is soft amber (never red). Tile colours as in the book (blue, orange, purple, yellow, green).
- **Main visual objects:** regular shapes with clear edges and visible corners; dot grids; woven mats with over and under clearly drawn; cubes with large clear pictures.
- **Animation style:** only meaningful movement: a strip slides over and under, a tile turns and snaps, a net folds, a thread draws. No decoration that moves.
- **UI layout and navigation:** one activity per screen; instruction strip at the top (one sentence, large); the working area in the middle; Hint, Retry, Reset (and Undo) in a fixed row at the bottom; Back and Next fixed at the bottom corners; progress dots under the instruction. Same placement in all eleven tools.
- **Common interaction patterns:** tap to select, drag to move, turn and flip buttons, a Check button that gives specific feedback, a three-step hint ladder with a worked step at the end, Retry for the step, Reset for the tool.

## SECTION E: Learning and Platform Rules

1. Videos explain; tools let the student explore, apply and get feedback. No classroom teacher is given a teaching role anywhere.
2. Every tool has one distinct mathematical purpose; no interaction is repeated across tools without a learning reason. Tools 5 and 6 both use the rhombus pieces but one measures and sorts given triangles and the other joins pieces and counts sides.
3. Each tool has three phases, in order: Recall and get ready, Play and apply, Challenge from the book.
4. A tool card that quotes the book uses the book's own questions, with their original wording, numbers and conditions. The plan does not change them and does not invent a challenge where the book has none.
5. Preserve the book's mathematics: its sizes (a paper 30 cm by 20 cm, 8 strips of 3 cm), its shapes, its counts. Any number the plan adds is labelled "practice values".
6. Do not introduce ideas outside the chapter: no degrees, no perimeter, no area, no symmetry, no polygon, trapezium or prism words.
7. One video per book question, on its answer, plus a question card and a solution card; one video per teaching idea; the hook comes first and does not explain the chapter.
8. Answers the book does not print are worked out from the chapter's own ideas and shown as the plan's reading (section A6).
9. Text hygiene: no em or en dash; minus is the true minus sign; students never read "NCERT", "textbook", page numbers or exercise numbers; say "your maths book".
10. Instructions are short, age-appropriate, one at a time; feedback is specific; hint, retry and reset are always present.
11. One visual identity (the craft table) and one navigation layout across all tools.
12. Verify page numbers, figures and numbers against the book's page images before any build.
