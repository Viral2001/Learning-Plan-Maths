## SECTION A: Chapter Understanding

### A1. Chapter name
Racing Seconds (Class 5, Maths Mela, Chapter 12).

### A2. Main learning objectives
Students will be able to:
1. Read the time on a clock face with an hour hand and a minute hand, and write it as hh:mm with a.m. or p.m.
2. Find the time elapsed between two times (within an hour, across hours, and across an hour mark), and say it in hours and minutes.
3. Compare times taken that are given in hours and minutes.
4. Write a time in the 12-hour format (a.m., p.m.) and in the 24-hour format, both ways, and find the time between two 24-hour times.
5. Use seconds as a new, smaller unit: 1 minute = 60 seconds; read a race time such as 01:55 (minutes and seconds); decide whether an event is measured in seconds or in minutes.
6. Read the seconds hand to find how many seconds an activity took, and draw the seconds hand after a given number of seconds (60 seconds is one full round and moves the minute hand by one minute).
7. Change hours to minutes and minutes to hours and minutes (1 hour = 60 minutes), and minutes to seconds and seconds to minutes and seconds (1 minute = 60 seconds), using a double number line.
8. Solve time problems: the time after a given time (add hours and minutes), the time before (go back), and across 12 noon and midnight.

### A6. Points the textbook leaves unclear (to confirm; the plan does not guess)
1. **The opener clocks print no digits.** Raghav's two clocks (p. 155) show only hands. Measured on the page: the first has the minute hand on 7 (35 minutes) and the hour hand between 5 and 6; the second has the minute hand on 11 (55 minutes) and the hour hand just before 6. The plan reads 05:35 and 05:55, so 20 minutes. The book says "in the morning", so a.m.
2. **The names on the winners' stand.** The book names Akira, Sunita and Mary, but never says which child is which. The track picture shows four children and the stand shows three. The plan answers "the child on the first stand (01:55) won" and does not match a name to a child.
3. **"Raghav" and "Raghu"** are both used (p. 159: Raghav goes to the kitchen, Raghu washes his plate; p. 160 the same). The plan keeps both spellings as printed (one name slot each) and does not decide whether they are one boy or two.
4. **The 24-hour format is never explained in words.** The book only gives two worked rows (05:30 a.m. = 05:30 hours, 02:30 p.m. = 14:30 hours) and the digital clock reading 24:00. The plan teaches the rule from those two rows: a.m. times keep their digits; a p.m. time adds 12 to the hour; the minutes never change. The book never shows 12:xx a.m. (just after midnight) or 12:xx p.m. in a table row; the plan avoids a 12:xx a.m. time and uses 12:30 PM and 12:40 PM only where the book's own problems need them.
5. **Q10 (p. 163) crosses midnight.** 08:35 PM plus 8 hours 25 minutes is 05:00 AM the next day. The book teaches nothing about crossing midnight. The plan uses the 24-hour idea (20:35 + 8:25 = 29:00 hours, and the day ends at 24:00, so 29:00 is 05:00 the next day). To be confirmed.
6. **The seconds-hand pictures are approximate.** p. 159 Q3: both minute hands are drawn at 6, though a seconds hand going from 45 to 12 would move the minute hand a little; the plan reads only the seconds hand (45 to 60 is 15 seconds). p. 159 Q4: the seconds hand is on 5 in both clocks and the minute hand has moved one mark: one full round, 60 seconds. p. 160 Q4: the first clock has the seconds hand on 11 (55 seconds); 55 + 40 = 95 seconds, so the new hand is on 7 (35 seconds) and the minute hand has moved on one minute, but the book draws the minute hand still on 12. The plan draws the seconds hand on 7 and notes the minute hand moves on one mark.
7. **Seconds or minutes (p. 157)** asks the student to estimate. The book prints no answers. The plan's reading: seconds for blinking, switching the lights, counting 1 to 20, filling a glass, sitting down, drinking a glass of water, snapping fingers and washing hands; minutes for melting an ice-cube and making a phone call. The student's own estimate for the last two (and for washing hands) may differ; the plan says so.
8. **The open activities (p. 158)** (list events; estimate and verify; skip the rope in 10 seconds; write FRIEND; run 100 m) depend on the student and have no single answer. The plan gives a labelled "Sample answer" with practice values, and says "Your answer may differ".
9. **"Share your strategies" (p. 155 Q4, p. 162 Q2)** has no printed strategy. The plan gives one reasoned strategy and says others are fine.
10. **Q4 (c) (p. 155)** does not say in which unit to give the answer; the plan gives hours and minutes (2 hours 15 minutes).
11. **Double number line blanks (p. 161, p. 162).** The blanks are worked out from the tick spacing, which is equal: (a) above: 2, 8, 11, 12 hr; below: 240, 420, 540 min. (c) above: 2, 20, 25 min; below: 300, 600, 900 sec.
12. **Answers the book does not print** are worked out by the plan and marked as such in the solutions: every item of Let us find out, the race questions, the seconds-or-minutes ticks, the clock readings of p. 159 and p. 160, the conversions, Q2 and Q4 to Q10. Only the p. 162 Q3 answer (1 hour 5 minutes) is printed.

## SECTION C: Interactive Tool Specifications

Common to every tool (details in sections D and E): one activity at a time; Back and Next always in the same place; progress dots; Hint, Retry, Reset, and (where moving objects) Undo; specific feedback ("20 + 15 = 35 minutes: up to the next hour, then the rest"), never only right or wrong; the clock, strip or number line is the largest thing on screen. Practice values the plan adds are labelled "practice values"; every other number is the book's. On every clock the hour hand is blue, the minute hand orange, the seconds hand red (the book's own opener colours); the hour hand moves a little as the minute hand moves, like a real clock.

### Tool 1. Yoga Clock
- **Connected topic:** Raghav's Morning Yoga (the opening topic, free first try right after the hook; the concept video follows it). Page 155.
- **Learning objective:** Students set a start clock and a finish clock, read each time, and read how much time lies between them.
- **Why a tool:** the idea of "time between two clocks" is felt when the student moves the hands, sees the shaded stretch grow, and reads it off.
- **Environment:** Raghav's yoga room: a mat on the floor and two wall clocks on a wall, named Start and Finish.
- **Objects:** two large analogue clocks (hour, minute hands); a digital read-out under each (hh:mm, with an a.m. / p.m. switch); a shaded stretch on the Finish clock from the start position to the finish position; a "time spent" box; a morning / evening sun-moon switch.
- **Controls:** drag the minute hand of a clock (the hour hand follows); tap the a.m. / p.m. switch; type the answer in the "time spent" box and tap Check.
- **What the student does:** The hook's picture of Raghav on his mat with the two wall clocks; one line: "Set the Start clock to where it was when Raghav began, and the Finish clock to where it was when he stopped." (1) set the Start clock to 07:10 a.m. and the Finish clock to 07:40 a.m. (practice values) and read the shaded stretch: 30 minutes; (2) a start of 04:25 p.m. and a finish of 05:10 p.m.: the stretch crosses the 5 o'clock mark, 45 minutes; (3) a start of 08:00 a.m. and a finish of 09:15 a.m.: 1 hour 15 minutes; (4) the tool shows two clocks and the student says the start, the finish and the time spent. The book's opener unchanged: "Raghav practices yoga in the morning." with the two clocks, and "At what time did Raghav start practising Yoga? At what time did he finish? How much time did he spend practising Yoga?" Asked open: the student reads both clocks and types the three answers; the concept video that follows names the strategy.
- **Feedback and hints:** a wrong start reading: "The minute hand points at 7, so that is 7 × 5 = 35 minutes past." Hints: which hand tells the hours; which tells the minutes; each number on the dial is 5 minutes; bottom-out writes the first time.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry resets the clocks of this card; Reset returns both clocks.

### Tool 2. Day Strip
- **Connected topic:** Two Ways to Write the Time and Time Between in 24-Hour Time. Pages 156 and 162.
- **Learning objective:** Students write a time in both the 12-hour and the 24-hour format from a strip of the whole day, and find the time between two 24-hour times.
- **Why a tool:** the 24-hour rule (add 12 after noon) is a position on a line of 24 hours; moving a marker along the strip shows why 02:30 p.m. is 14:30 hours and lets the student measure gaps.
- **Environment:** the school notice board with a long timetable strip for one day.
- **Objects:** a strip of 24 hour boxes from 00 to 24 (a.m. half with a sun, p.m. half with a moon); two draggable markers (First time, Second time); a small clock that follows the marker; two read-outs: 12-hour (hh:mm a.m. or p.m.) and 24-hour (hh:mm hours); a gap bar between the two markers with hours and minutes.
- **Controls:** drag a marker along the strip (snaps to 5 minutes); tap a read-out to type a time; a "Show the 12 added" switch that draws the +12 bracket over the p.m. half; tap Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture, one a.m. time and one p.m. time with their 24-hour twins; instruction "Drag the marker to the time and read both boxes." (1) drag to 07:45 a.m.: both read-outs show 07:45 and 07:45 hours; (2) drag to 04:10 p.m. (practice values): the p.m. half shows 16:10 hours and the +12 bracket; (3) type a 24-hour time (21:20 hours) and place the marker where it goes; (4) put the first marker at 13:40 hours and the second at 16:15 hours: the gap bar reads 2 hours 35 minutes; (5) a 24-hour time to a 12-hour time. The book's items unchanged: Let us find out Q6 (fill the blanks of the 12-hour and 24-hour table: 05:30 a.m., blank = 08:35 hours, 11:55 a.m., 02:30 p.m., 05:30 p.m., 09:35 p.m.), Q7 (match the six 12-hour times to the six 24-hour times), and the solved example "Raghu left his house at 08:00 hours and arrived at his Nana ji's house at 09:05 hours. How long did he take to reach his house?".
- **Feedback and hints:** "05:30 p.m.: the hour 5 plus 12 is 17, the minutes stay 30, so 17:30 hours." Hints: is it a.m. or p.m.; for p.m. add 12 to the hour; the minutes never change; bottom-out sets the first marker.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry returns the markers of this card; Reset clears both markers.

### Tool 3. Seconds Clock
- **Connected topic:** Reading the Seconds Hand and Drawing the Seconds Hand. Pages 157, 159 and 160.
- **Learning objective:** Students read the seconds taken from the start and finish positions of the seconds hand, and place the seconds hand where it stops after a given number of seconds.
- **Why a tool:** the seconds hand has to move to be understood: 5 seconds per number, one full round per minute, the minute hand stepping on.
- **Environment:** a race timing board by the school track: a big wall clock with a seconds hand.
- **Objects:** a large analogue clock with hour, minute and seconds hands; a ring of numbers 5, 10, ..., 60 outside the dial; a "start ghost" (a pale copy of the seconds hand left where the run began); a "seconds counted" box; a minute-hand step light that blinks when the seconds hand passes 12.
- **Controls:** Go (the seconds hand runs, one mark at a time) and Stop; "Mark start"; drag the seconds hand; type the seconds; "Run for N seconds" (N chosen by the card); Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of a seconds hand at 3 and later at 8; instruction "Press Go, watch the red hand and say how many seconds have passed." (1) start at 12, Go, stop at 6: half a round, 30 seconds; (2) set the start where it is shown and read the seconds taken from the ghost to the red hand; (3) a run that passes 12 (practice values: from 8 to 2): the step light blinks and the count goes on, 30 seconds; (4) drag the red hand to where it stops after 25 seconds from the 10 (practice values); (5) a full round: 60 seconds, the red hand is back at the same number and the minute hand has stepped on one mark. The book's items unchanged: Let Us Do p. 159 (four pairs of clocks, "Rani took ___ sec. to get out of her bed", Raghav to the kitchen, Ritu to pick up a piece of paper, Raghu to wash his spoon and plate) and Let Us Do p. 160 ("Draw the missing seconds hand on the clocks on the right side": 20 sec, 30 sec, 60 sec, 40 sec), each with its two clock pictures.
- **Feedback and hints:** "The hand went from 9 to 12: 12 is 60 seconds and 9 is 45 seconds, so 60 − 45 = 15 seconds." Hints: how many seconds is one number; where did it start and where did it stop; did it pass 12; bottom-out counts the numbers aloud.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry returns the hand to the start of this card; Reset returns the clock to 12.

### Tool 4. Time Converter Line
- **Connected topic:** Hours and Minutes, and Minutes and Seconds. Pages 161 and 162.
- **Learning objective:** Students change hours to minutes and minutes to hours and minutes, and minutes to seconds and seconds to minutes and seconds, on a double number line.
- **Why a tool:** two scales side by side with equal steps show that every step is multiplied by the same number (60), and sliding along them lets the student read in both units.
- **Environment:** the school notice board with a double number line drawn on a long paper strip.
- **Objects:** a double number line (top scale and bottom scale, aligned ticks); a mode switch (hours and minutes, or minutes and seconds); a slider that moves along both; multiplication arrows (× 60, × 2, × 5) that the student can tap to show; number boxes for blanks.
- **Controls:** drag the slider along the line; tap a blank box and type with a large number pad; tap "Show × 60"; Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of 1 hr above 60 min and 2 hr above 120 min; instruction "Slide to 3 hr and read the minutes." (1) slide to 5 hr (practice values) and read 300 min; (2) type the minutes for 6 hr, 9 hr; (3) read an in-between value: 5 hr 25 min is 300 + 25 = 325 min (practice values); (4) switch to minutes and seconds: slide to 3 min and read 180 sec; (5) from seconds back: 400 sec, how many whole minutes and how many seconds are left. The book's items unchanged: Let Us Do Q1 (a) the hours and minutes double number line, (b) the eight conversions (1 hours 10 minutes, 2 hours 45 minutes, 3 hours 15 minutes, 4 hours 20 minutes = ___ minutes; 75, 150, 220, 390 minutes = ___ hours ___ minutes), (c) the minutes and seconds double number line and its seven conversions (320, 225, 700, 1,000 sec; 10 min 13 sec, 4 min 8 sec, 15 min 40 sec), and Q2 (Raghav studies four subjects, 50 minutes each).
- **Feedback and hints:** "75 minutes: 60 minutes make 1 hour and 15 minutes are left, so 1 hour 15 minutes." Hints: which unit is bigger; how many of the small unit make 1 of the bigger; how many whole bigger units fit and what is left; bottom-out shows the line with the groups of 60.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the boxes of this card; Reset restores the line.

### Tool 5. Time Jumper
- **Connected topic:** Time After, Time Before, and Crossing Noon and Midnight. Page 163.
- **Learning objective:** Students find the time after, and the time before, a given time by hopping along a time line in hours and then in minutes, including across 12 noon and midnight.
- **Why a tool:** the hops (hours first, then up to the next hour mark, then the rest) are the strategy; students choose and place the hops and see where they land.
- **Environment:** the school playground: a long hopscotch line drawn as a time line, with a flag at the start time.
- **Objects:** a time line with hour marks and 5-minute ticks (a.m. and p.m. shown, a "next day" band after midnight); a flag at the start time; jump cards (1 hour, 30 minutes, 10 minutes, 5 minutes, 1 minute; forward and back); a 24-hour read-out that follows the flag; a landing read-out in the 12-hour format.
- **Controls:** drag a jump card onto the line (the flag hops by that much); tap Undo; tap the flag to read the time; tap "Forward" or "Back"; type the end time; Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture, a flag at 03:20 p.m. and a jump of 1 hour; instruction "Hop the hours first, then the minutes." (1) forward from 02:10 p.m. for 1 hour 35 minutes (practice values) by one hour jump and minute jumps; (2) back from 07:50 p.m. for 2 hours 20 minutes; (3) a hop over 12 noon: 11:25 a.m. + 1 hour 50 minutes; (4) a hop over midnight: 11:10 p.m. + 3 hours 20 minutes (the "next day" band appears); (5) the student chooses the hops for a time given in words. The book's items unchanged: Q4 (Jyoti, 06:15 PM + 1 hour 45 minutes), Q5 (Ragini, 1 hour 10 minutes, finished 09:40 PM), Q6 (picnic, 08:30 AM + 4 hours and 10 minutes), Q7 (Raji, 06:00 PM + 1 hour 30 minutes), Q8 (Alya, 05:30 PM + 1 hour 10 minutes), Q9 (lunch break, 12:30 PM + 35 minutes), Q10 (08:35 PM + 8 hours and 25 minutes).
- **Feedback and hints:** "06:15 PM + 1 hour = 07:15 PM; 07:15 PM + 45 minutes: 45 minutes reach 08:00 PM." Hints: add the hours first; how many minutes to the next hour mark; how many minutes are left; bottom-out places the first jump.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry returns the flag to the start of this card; Reset clears all jumps.

## SECTION D: Shared Visual Language

- **Overall illustration style:** warm, colourful, polished educational-game illustrations; 3D or isometric where the scene holds objects, soft shadows, clear shapes, clean uncluttered compositions; gentle animation. The clock face, the strip or the number line is always the largest, clearest thing on screen.
- **Chapter theme and environment:** "Raghav's School Day". One friendly Indian school day, from the yoga mat at home in the morning to the school track and the notice board. Each tool is a station: the yoga room with two wall clocks, the school timetable strip, the track's timing board, the notice board with a double number line, the playground hopscotch line. The day is the one environment of the chapter; nothing is added that does not help the mathematics.
- **Characters:** only the book's: Raghav, Rani, Ritu, Raghu, Zainab, Remo, Akira, Sunita, Mary, Jyoti, Ragini, Raji, Alya. They appear to set a situation, never to teach.
- **Colour and visual direction:** warm cream and wood tones for the background; every clock has a dark teal rim and a pale face like the book's. One colour per hand on every clock in the chapter: hour hand blue, minute hand orange, seconds hand red. The time being asked about is highlighted in orange, the rest is pale cream, correct is green, "look again" is soft amber (never red). a.m. is shown with a sun, p.m. with a moon.
- **Main visual objects:** big analogue clocks with every number 1 to 12 and 60 small marks; digital read-outs in a clear font; a 24-hour strip; time lines with hour and minute ticks; double number lines with aligned ticks.
- **Animation style:** only meaningful movement: the minute hand sweeps and the hour hand follows, a shaded stretch grows between two times, the seconds hand steps one mark at a time, a flag hops along the line, the minute hand steps on when the seconds hand passes 12. No decoration that moves.
- **UI layout and navigation:** one activity per screen; instruction strip at the top (one sentence, large); the working area in the middle; Hint, Retry, Reset (and Undo) in a fixed row at the bottom; Back and Next fixed at the bottom corners; progress dots under the instruction. Same placement in all five tools.
- **Common interaction patterns:** drag a hand or a marker, tap to select, drag a jump card, type with a large number pad, a Check button that gives specific feedback, a three-step hint ladder with a worked step at the end, Retry for the step, Reset for the tool.

## SECTION E: Learning and Platform Rules

1. Videos explain; tools let the student explore, apply and get feedback. No classroom teacher is given a teaching role anywhere.
2. Every tool has one distinct mathematical purpose; no interaction is repeated across tools without a learning reason. Tools 1 and 3 both use a clock but one reads hours and minutes and the other reads the seconds hand; Tools 4 and 5 both do arithmetic with time but one changes units and the other moves along time.
3. Each tool has three phases, in order: Recall and get ready, Play and apply, NCERT challenge.
4. A tool card that quotes the book uses the book's own questions, with their original wording, numbers and conditions. The plan does not change them and does not invent a challenge where the book has none.
5. Preserve the book's mathematics: its times, its clocks (hand positions as measured), its double number lines (equal steps), its winners' stand times 01:55, 01:56, 01:57. Any number the plan adds is labelled "practice values".
6. Do not introduce ideas outside the chapter: no decimals, no fractions of an hour such as half past or quarter to, no days, weeks or months, no speed, no stopwatch units smaller than a second, no time zones.
7. One video per book question, on its answer, plus a question card and a solution card; one video per teaching idea; the hook comes first and does not explain the chapter.
8. Answers the book does not print are worked out from the chapter's own ideas and shown as the plan's reading (section A6).
9. Text hygiene: no em or en dash; minus is the true minus sign; students never read "NCERT", "textbook", page numbers or exercise numbers; say "your maths book". Times are written as the book writes them: 05:35 a.m., 02:30 p.m., 14:30 hours, 08:00 hours; durations in words (1 hour 5 minutes).
10. Instructions are short, age-appropriate, one at a time; feedback is specific; hint, retry and reset are always present.
11. One visual identity (the school day) and one navigation layout across all tools; the three hands always have the same three colours.
12. Verify page numbers, clocks and numbers against the book's page images before any build.
