## 1. Hard rules (gates enforce most; fix content, never a gate)
- Class 5 students, 10 to 11 years old: short plain sentences. Characters and settings are the book's: Raghav, Rani, Ritu, Raghu, Zainab, Remo, Akira, Sunita, Mary, Jyoti, Ragini, Raji, Alya, Nana ji; the yoga room, the school, the track, the homework table, the notice board. The plan's own WE use the same people and places. Do not invent facts or datasets: every real-world number that is not a plain WE number comes from the book or from section 4.
- **No em dash and no en dash** in any string or comment (the book's dashes become commas, colons or "to"). Minus is U+2212 `−`. Use `×` and `÷`. Hyphen only inside words ("12-hour", "24-hour", "one-minute" are fine).
- **Times and units.** Write a clock time the way the book does, with the leading zero: `05:35 a.m.`, `02:30 p.m.`, `14:30 hours`, `08:00 hours`; the book's own AM/PM style (`06:15 PM`) only inside the book questions of the Time Problems group (Q4 to Q10 of p. 163), which print AM/PM. A duration is written in words: `1 hour 5 minutes`, `2 hours 15 minutes`, `35 seconds` (hour, minute, second in full, singular for 1: never "1 hours"). **Never the abbreviations `min`, `sec`, `hr`, `hrs`, `h`, `m`, `s` in a string a student reads** (they convert to nonsense; the book's own "sec." becomes "seconds"). Race times are `01:55` followed by the words "minutes and seconds" when first used. A 24-hour time is `19:45 hours`. Never write 12:xx a.m. (just after midnight): the book never uses it. 12:30 PM and 12:40 PM are used only where section 3/4 say.
- Never write "NCERT", "textbook", "page N", "Fig.", "Figure N" or exercise numbers in anything the student reads (names, teach lines, steps, questions, tool text, image text). Say "your maths book". Exception: the `textbook=[...]` argument of `A()` quotes the book verbatim (stored as a `textbook_text` block), still no dashes.
- **Arithmetic must be true in every string.** The equation gate (`eqcheck_plain`) checks only whole-number chains `a op b = c` (it reads `×` and `÷` before `+` and `−`). It SKIPS anything with a colon, a letter glued to the number or a slash. Never write a division with a remainder as a chain (`200 ÷ 60 = 3` is FALSE and fails the gate): write "60 × 3 = 180 and 200 − 180 = 20" instead. Never write `a = b` where the two sides are different numbers of the same thing. Keep whole-number chains on one line. Times and durations (`07:15 + 45 minutes = 08:00`) are NOT machine checked: the lead re-computes every one with a script (section 7), so write each one correctly the first time and keep to the numbers of section 2 and 4.
- 100% coverage: every id in `content.NCERT_ITEMS` (section 3) is attached to a concept: a `text` or `talk` id through `A(..., ncert=[...])` (or a WE's `ncert=`) or a DOER's `ncert=`, a question id as an `FIO` item of the DOER of its topic.
- **One video per concept.** A teaching concept `A()` has exactly ONE `WE` in `examples` (that WE is the concept video) with the PLAN's own numbers (section 2), never the numbers of a book question. The hook concept has `video_focus=["HOOK"]`, `enrichment=True`, no WE. DOER concepts carry a recap, their question items and at most one tool, no WE.
- **Questions.** Every book question is a `FIO(cov, label, question, steps, answer, kind=..., q_figure=..., s_figure=...)` in the DOER of its topic, in the book's order; `core/qt.py` moves each into its own concept of the "Questions: ..." topic that follows. `question` is the book's wording (copy it; abbreviations written in full; times as in the book; blanks `___`; a part label `(a)` is followed by a space and then the part, once per part). `steps` = the numbered working, one operation per line, values before combining, a check as the last step; `answer` = the short final answer, at most about 180 characters (the displayed answer is cut at 220 characters of its LaTeX form: the lead measures it). Open-ended items get a labelled "Sample answer" built by reasoning and say "Your answer may differ". `q_figure` = ONE plain sentence of ONLY what the question gives, never the answer or a hint; `s_figure` = one plain sentence for the solution card (it may show the answer).
- **Labels (`book`) decide the Questions topic** (`core/question_naming.py`): a label starting `Let Us Do` goes to "Questions: Let us do"; a label starting `Example N` goes to "Questions: Example"; any other label goes to a topic named by the text before its first colon. Use exactly the labels given in section 3.
- Names: DOER name starts with `Practice: ` or `Pull it together and do it: `.
- An idea topic has 2 to 4 primary concepts INCLUDING its DOER (last). The intro T1 is `topic_type="framing"` with `""` objective, all concepts `enrichment=True`: C1 hook (`video_focus=["HOOK"]`) and C3 a DOER with `role="try_it"` and the free-try tool (no C2: the framing topic holds exactly ONE video, the hook, and no book question). The summary topic is `topic_type="summary"`, one concept, no tool, no video_focus (there is no recap video).
- Objectives: one per non-enrichment concept, starts "Students ...", checkable (a verb you could test: read, find, write, compare, draw, choose, change, explain), shares a key term with its concept, never names a tool. Topic objective on each idea topic. Bloom words: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze.
- Misconception ids come from `content.MISCONCEPTIONS` (section 6); attach the relevant ones to teach concepts, DOERs and tools.
- `teach` lines: the first line is a prediction question ("Predict first: ..."), then the reveal, then the rule in one line. `minutes` 4 to 8.
- Tool specs (`T(...)`): section 5. Exactly 5 to 7 challenges, exactly 4 hints. Fit the three phases into the `T()` fields (below).
- Words outside the chapter are not used: no decimals, no "half past", "quarter to", "o'clock", no days/weeks/months/years, no speed, no stopwatch, no fractions. Crossing 12 noon and midnight is taught only in T6.C3 (and used in Q6, Q9, Q10).

Teaching concept seeds. The numbers below are the plan's own (practice) values, already verified: use exactly these in the WE. Teach lines: predict, reveal, rule.
- **T1B.C1 Time Elapsed Between Two Times.** WE: "Zainab started colouring at 04:25 p.m. and stopped at 05:10 p.m. (a) How long did she colour? Then: (b) How long is it from 07:20 a.m. to 10:50 a.m.?" Steps: (a) 04:25 p.m. to 05:00 p.m. is 60 − 25 = 35 minutes; 05:00 p.m. to 05:10 p.m. is 10 minutes; 35 + 10 = 45 minutes. (b) 07:20 a.m. to 10:20 a.m. is 3 hours (10 − 7 = 3); 10:20 a.m. to 10:50 a.m. is 30 minutes; so 3 hours 30 minutes. Teach: the three cases of the book's Q4: same hour (take the smaller minutes from the bigger), same minutes (count the hours), different hours and minutes (hop to the next whole hour, or hop whole hours first, then the rest). A clock's minute hand moves 5 minutes for each number. Reading a clock: the hour hand shows the hour, the minute hand shows the minutes, 5 minutes per number. Rule: count up from the earlier time to the later time, in easy hops. Misconceptions: M_MINUTE_NUMBER, M_TIME_HUNDRED. ncert: `open.story` is the hook's; here no text id.
- **T1B.C2 Comparing Times Taken.** WE: "Zainab took 1 hour 50 minutes, Remo took 1 hour 25 minutes and Mary took 2 hours 5 minutes to finish a puzzle. Who took the longest time and who took the least?" Steps: compare the hours first: Mary has 2 hours, the others 1 hour, so Mary took the longest; Zainab and Remo have the same hours, so compare the minutes: 25 minutes is less than 50 minutes, so Remo took the least. Check in minutes: 60 + 50 = 110, 60 + 25 = 85, 120 + 5 = 125, and 125 > 110 > 85. Rule: compare the hours first; if the hours are the same, compare the minutes. Misconceptions: M_MINUTES_FIRST.
- **T2.C1 Two Ways to Write the Time.** WE: "(a) Write 07:45 p.m. in the 24-hour format. (b) Write 16:20 hours in the 12-hour format." Steps: (a) it is p.m., so add 12 to the hour: 7 + 12 = 19; the minutes stay 45; so 19:45 hours. (b) 16 is more than 12, so it is p.m.: 16 − 12 = 4; the minutes stay 20; so 04:20 p.m. Teach: a day has 24 hours; the 12-hour clock goes round twice (a.m. for the morning, p.m. for the afternoon and evening) and says a.m. or p.m.; the 24-hour format counts once from 00:00 to 24:00 and says "hours"; a.m. time keeps its digits (05:30 a.m. = 05:30 hours); p.m. adds 12 to the hour (02:30 p.m. = 14:30 hours); the minutes never change. Misconceptions: M_PM_SAME. ncert: none (the book's two worked rows are in `LFO.Q6`).
- **T2.C2 Time Between in 24-Hour Time.** WE: "A drawing class starts at 13:40 hours and ends at 16:15 hours. How long does it last?" Steps: 13:40 hours to 14:00 hours is 20 minutes; 14:00 hours to 16:00 hours is 2 hours; 16:00 hours to 16:15 hours is 15 minutes; 20 + 15 = 35 minutes; so 2 hours 35 minutes. Teach: with 24-hour times there is no a.m. or p.m. to track, so hop to the next whole hour, then whole hours, then the rest. Rule: go to the next hour, add the hours, add the rest of the minutes. Misconceptions: M_MINUTES_OVER_59, M_PM_SAME.
- **T3.C1 A Smaller Unit: Seconds.** WE: "In a race, Remo's time is 01:48 and Zainab's time is 01:52 (minutes and seconds). Read each time. Who won, and by how many seconds? Change Remo's time to seconds." Steps: 01:48 means 1 minute 48 seconds; 01:52 means 1 minute 52 seconds; both took 1 minute, so compare the seconds: 48 is less than 52, so Remo won; 52 − 48 = 4 seconds; 1 minute = 60 seconds, so 1 minute 48 seconds = 60 + 48 = 108 seconds. Teach: a minute is split into 60 seconds (1 minute = 60 seconds); seconds show small differences, like in a race; the least time wins. ncert: `school.race`, `school.unit`, `note.podium` (talk: on the winners' stand the child with the least time stood first, then the next least, then the next). Misconceptions: M_BIGGER_TIME_WINS, M_SMALL_NUMBER_SMALL_UNIT, M_TIME_HUNDRED.
- **T3.C2 Seconds or Minutes?** WE: "Choose seconds or minutes: (a) tying a shoelace (b) boiling an egg (c) saying your name." Steps: ask "can it be done in less than 1 minute?"; (a) yes, seconds; (b) no, it takes several minutes, so minutes; (c) yes, seconds. Teach: 1 minute is 60 seconds, so an activity that is over before the seconds hand makes one round is measured in seconds; one that takes longer than a minute is measured in minutes; the choice is an estimate and your own estimate may differ. Misconceptions: M_SMALL_NUMBER_SMALL_UNIT.
- **T4.C1 Reading the Seconds Hand.** WE: "Remo timed himself tying his shoe. (a) The seconds hand started on 3 and stopped on 8. How many seconds? (b) Another try: it started on 8 and stopped on 2. How many seconds?" Steps: each number on the dial is 5 seconds; (a) 3 is 15 seconds and 8 is 40 seconds; 40 − 15 = 25 seconds. (b) 8 is 40 seconds; from 8 to 12 is 60 − 40 = 20 seconds; from 12 to 2 is 10 seconds; 20 + 10 = 30 seconds. Teach: the seconds hand is the thin red hand; it goes round once in 60 seconds, and when it has gone round once, the minute hand moves on by one mark (60 movements of the seconds hand shift the minute hand); if the hand passes 12, count to 12 first and then on. ncert: `note.hand`. Misconceptions: M_HAND_SUBTRACT, M_ROUND_ZERO, M_MINUTE_NUMBER.
- **T4.C2 Drawing the Seconds Hand.** WE: "The seconds hand is on 10. It runs for 25 seconds. Where does it stop, and what happens to the minute hand?" Steps: 10 is 50 seconds; 50 + 25 = 75 seconds; 75 is more than 60, so the hand has passed 12: 75 − 60 = 15 seconds; 15 seconds is the number 3 (15 ÷ 5 = 3); the hand stops on 3; it has gone past 12 once, so the minute hand has moved on by one mark. Teach: change the number of seconds into numbers of 5 and count on from the start; 60 seconds is one full round (back on the same number, minute hand one mark on). Misconceptions: M_ROUND_ZERO, M_HAND_SUBTRACT.
- **T5.C1 Hours and Minutes.** WE: "(a) Change 5 hours 25 minutes to minutes. (b) Change 370 minutes to hours and minutes." Steps: (a) 1 hour = 60 minutes; 5 hours = 5 × 60 = 300 minutes; 300 + 25 = 325 minutes. (b) how many 60s fit in 370: 60 × 6 = 360 and 60 × 7 = 420, so 6 hours; 370 − 360 = 10 minutes left; so 6 hours 10 minutes. Teach: on a double number line the hours are above and the minutes below, and every step multiplies by 60 (the book's "× 2" arrow: 1 hour is 60 minutes, so 2 hours is 2 × 60 = 120 minutes); to change minutes to hours, find how many whole groups of 60 fit and keep what is left (the book: 180 minutes = 3 hours because 3 × 60 = 180). ncert: `conv.zainab`. Misconceptions: M_TIME_HUNDRED. Never write a division with a remainder as a chain.
- **T5.C2 Minutes and Seconds.** WE: "(a) Change 4 minutes 30 seconds to seconds. (b) Change 400 seconds to minutes and seconds." Steps: (a) 1 minute = 60 seconds; 4 minutes = 4 × 60 = 240 seconds; 240 + 30 = 270 seconds. (b) 60 × 6 = 360 and 60 × 7 = 420, so 6 minutes; 400 − 360 = 40 seconds left; so 6 minutes 40 seconds. Teach: the same idea with the second double number line (the book's "× 5" and "× 60" arrows). Misconceptions: M_TIME_HUNDRED.
- **T6.C1 Time After.** WE: "Zainab starts painting at 03:20 p.m. and paints for 1 hour 50 minutes. At what time does she stop?" Steps: add the hours first: 03:20 p.m. + 1 hour = 04:20 p.m.; 04:20 p.m. to 05:00 p.m. is 40 minutes; 50 − 40 = 10 minutes left; 05:00 p.m. + 10 minutes = 05:10 p.m.; check: 05:10 p.m. − 1 hour 50 minutes goes back to 03:20 p.m. Teach: hop the hours, then hop to the next whole hour, then add the minutes left; solve in your head with hops, not in columns (the book's note); minutes never go past 59 (45 + 30 minutes is not 75 on the clock: it is the next hour and 15 minutes). ncert: `note.oral`. Misconceptions: M_MINUTES_OVER_59.
- **T6.C2 Time Before.** WE: "Remo finished reading at 08:15 p.m. He read for 2 hours 40 minutes. At what time did he start?" Steps: go back the hours first: 08:15 p.m. − 2 hours = 06:15 p.m.; go back to 06:00 p.m. is 15 minutes; 40 − 15 = 25 minutes left; 06:00 p.m. − 25 minutes = 05:35 p.m.; check: 05:35 p.m. + 2 hours 40 minutes = 08:15 p.m. (05:35 + 2 hours = 07:35; 07:35 + 40 minutes = 08:15). Teach: going back is the same hops the other way round. Misconceptions: M_MINUTES_OVER_59.
- **T6.C3 Crossing Noon and Midnight.** WE: "(a) A bus leaves at 10:40 a.m. and the trip takes 3 hours 30 minutes. When does it arrive? (b) A train leaves at 10:15 p.m. and the journey takes 5 hours 45 minutes. When does it arrive?" Steps: (a) in 24-hour time: 10:40 a.m. is 10:40 hours; + 3 hours = 13:40 hours; + 30 minutes = 14:10 hours; 14:10 hours is 02:10 p.m. (14 − 12 = 2); noon passed, so a.m. changes to p.m. (b) 10:15 p.m. is 22:15 hours; + 5 hours = 27:15 hours; + 45 minutes = 28:00 hours; a day ends at 24:00 hours, so 28 − 24 = 4: 04:00 a.m. the next day. Teach: at 12 noon the a.m. changes to p.m.; at 24:00 hours (midnight) a new day begins and the count starts again at 00:00; to cross, use the 24-hour clock and take 24 away from a time above 24:00 hours. Misconceptions: M_PAST_24, M_NOON_AM.
- **T7.C1 summary.** One recap line per idea with a short check line each (reading and elapsed time, comparing, two formats, seconds, seconds hand, conversions, time after and before, crossing noon and midnight). Use the numbers of the concept seeds above, not the book's.
