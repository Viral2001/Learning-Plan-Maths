## 1. Hard rules (gates enforce these; fix content, never a gate)
- Class 5 students, 10 to 11 years old: short plain sentences. Characters/settings are the book's (Sanju, Mira, the boatman, lion, sheep, grass, the king and his caretaker, the rabbit and carrots, the train). Do not invent transport facts, distances, speeds or datasets: every real-world number comes from the book. The plan's own WE numbers are practice values.
- **No em dash and no en dash** in any string or comment (the book's dashes become commas, colons or "to": "3 to 5 km", never "3-5 km"). Minus and subtraction sign is U+2212 `−`. Use `×` and `÷`. Hyphen only inside words ("twenty-four", "five-digit").
- Never write "NCERT", "textbook", "page N", "Fig.", "Figure N" or exercise numbers in anything the student reads (names, teach lines, steps, questions, tool text). Say "your maths book". Exception: the `textbook=[...]` argument of `A()` quotes the book verbatim (stored as a `textbook_text` block), still no dashes: convert them.
- Arithmetic must be true in every string (gate `eqcheck_plain` checks every `a op b = c` chain, commas allowed, e.g. `9,000 + 1,000 = 10,000`). Verify every number with a tiny Python check before you finish. Do not write an equation that is only approximately true.
- 100% coverage: every id in `content.NCERT_ITEMS` (see section 3) is attached to a concept: a `text`/`talk` id through `A(..., ncert=[...])` (or a WE's `ncert=`), a question id as an `FIO` item of the DOER of its topic.
- **One video per concept.** A teaching concept `A()` has exactly ONE `WE` in `examples` (that WE is the concept video) with the PLAN's own numbers: never reuse a number set that the book prints or asks (the book's printed examples are questions). The hook concept has `video_focus=["HOOK"]`, `enrichment=True`, no WE. DOER concepts carry a recap, their question items and at most one tool, no WE.
- **Questions.** Every book question is a `FIO(cov, label, question, steps, answer, kind=...)` in the DOER of its topic, in the book's order; `core/qt.py` moves each into its own concept of the "Questions: <topic>" topic. `question` is the book's wording (copy it; replace "textbook" by "maths book"). `steps` = the numbered working, one operation per line, values before combining, a check as the last step; `answer` = short final answer. Open-ended items get a labelled "Sample answer" built by reasoning, and say "Your answer may differ". Add `q_figure` (one plain sentence: only what the question gives) when the question card needs a drawing (number line, table, picture of piles, stable...), `s_figure` when the solution card needs one.
- Names: DOER name starts with `Pull it together and do it: ` or `Practice: `. Question labels (`book`) look like "Let Us Do (large numbers), Q2 read" (the book's own label plus a short topic tag in brackets), never a page.
- An idea topic has 2 to 4 primary concepts INCLUDING its DOER (last). The intro (T1) is `topic_type="framing"` with `""` objective, all concepts `enrichment=True`: C1 hook (`video_focus=["HOOK"]`), C2 a teaching concept, C3 a DOER with `role="try_it"` and the free-try tool. The summary topic is `topic_type="summary"`, one concept, no tool, no video_focus (there is no recap video).
- Objectives: one per non-enrichment concept, starts "Students ...", checkable (a verb you could test: write, read, find, compare, order, swap, round, explain). Topic objective on each idea topic. Bloom words: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze.
- Misconception ids come from `content.MISCONCEPTIONS` (section 4); attach the relevant ones to teach concepts and tools.
- `teach` lines: first line is a prediction question ("Predict first: ..."), then the reveal, then the rule in one line. `minutes` 4 to 8.
- Tool specs (`T(...)`): see section 5. Exactly 5 to 7 challenges, exactly 4 hints.
