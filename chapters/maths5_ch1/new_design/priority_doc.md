## 2. Shared visual language for the chapter

- Overall style: warm, colourful, polished educational-game illustrations. 3D/isometric environment for interactive scenes, soft shadows, clear shapes, gentle animation. The supplied bullock-cart, historical boat and old train references guide the period-appropriate transport look and atmosphere. (The reference images were NOT supplied with the pasted text.)
- Scene composition: one main environment per activity. Small buildings, roads, paths, trees, railway tracks, rivers or stations only when they support the current concept. No crowded scenes, no unrelated decoration.
- Main learning object: the number, digit sequence, number line, route data or puzzle board is the largest and clearest element. Visual richness must never reduce mathematical readability.
- Historical and modern scenes: warm, storybook-inspired historical settings for earlier travel; clean contemporary settings for modern transport. Do not imply that every older mode has disappeared.
- Interaction: objects selectable and manipulable where appropriate. Clear animations for meaningful change: a vehicle moving, a digit coach swapping, a number marker moving, a puzzle piece changing position.
- Student interface: one activity at a time, one clear instruction at a time, large controls, consistent Back/Next placement, visible progress, simple restart. No menus, no multiple pop-ups, no excessive text.

## 3. Learning and platform rules

- Videos deliver the concept explanation. Tools provide independent exploration, application and feedback. Do not assign a teaching role to a classroom teacher.
- Every tool must have a distinct mathematical purpose. The travel theme supports the concept rather than acting as decoration.
- Strong central visual; active objects large, readable, easy to select.
- Short, age-appropriate instructions; show only the information needed for the current step.
- Immediate, specific feedback, hints, retry and reset support. Students always know what to do next.
- Phase 1: Recall & Get Ready. Reconnect with the video concept through a short visual prompt; do not repeat the full video.
- Phase 2: Play & Apply. Students actively manipulate, arrange, compare, construct, experiment or solve.
- Phase 3: NCERT Challenge. Use the exact relevant textbook question, figure, activity or puzzle without changing its values, wording or conditions.
- Do not invent transport facts or numerical datasets. Every displayed number must come from the textbook or be clearly identified as a non-factual practice value used only where permitted.
- Verify the exact NCERT edition and page before implementing question content, diagrams, rounding rules or puzzle conditions.
