## 1. Hard rules (gates enforce most; fix content, never a gate)
- Class 5 students, 10 to 11 years old: short plain sentences. Characters and settings are the book's: Ashutosh, Sahana, Reema, Pragya, Padma and the children A, B, C, D; the school playground, the giant wheel, straws, the paper fan, the notebook house, the tracing-paper disc, the minute hand, the compass street. The plan's own WE use the same things. Do not invent facts or datasets: every number that is not a plain WE number comes from the book or from section 4. Give no teaching role to a teacher (the book's "Note for Teachers" is not used).
- **No em dash and no en dash** in any string or comment (the book's dashes become commas, colons or "to"). Minus is U+2212 `−`. Use `×` and `÷`. Hyphen only inside words ("anti-clockwise", "5-sided", "full-circle" are fine).
- **Fractions are written `a/b` with a plain slash and no spaces** (`1/4`, `3/12`). Never the characters ½ ⅓ ¼. Never glue a letter to a fraction ("1/6s" is forbidden: write "pieces of 1/6", "1/6 pieces"). "of" between a fraction and a thing is fine ("1/3 of a turn"). A blank inside a fraction is `___/10`; a blank standing alone is `___`. A part label is `(a)` before the part, once per part, with a colon after it in the question text (`(a): ...`).
- **A turn is a fraction of a full turn.** Say "a 1/4 turn", "a quarter turn", "a half turn", "a 3/4 turn", "a full turn". Angle names are exactly: right angle, acute angle, obtuse angle, straight angle, "more than a straight angle" (a turn of more than 1/2 has no other name in this chapter). Direction words are exactly "clockwise" and "anti-clockwise" (the book's spelling). NEVER write degrees, the degree sign, "protractor", "reflex", "vertex", "complementary", "supplementary", "radian", "coordinate", "bearing", "perpendicular", "parallel". Say "square corner" for the look of a right angle only if needed.
- Never write "NCERT", "textbook", "page N", "Fig.", "Figure N" or exercise numbers in anything the student reads (names, teach lines, steps, questions, tool text). Say "your maths book". Exception: the `textbook=[...]` argument of `A()` quotes the book verbatim (stored as a `textbook_text` block), still no dashes.
- **Arithmetic must be true in every string.** The equation gate checks only whole-number chains (`4 ÷ 2 = 2`) and SKIPS any chain that touches a slash, so fraction facts are NOT machine checked. You must re-compute every fraction fact (`1/4 + 1/8 = 3/8`, `6/8 = 3/4`, comparisons) with Python `fractions.Fraction` before you finish. Do not write an equation that is only approximately true. Keep whole-number chains on one line.
- 100% coverage: every id in `content.NCERT_ITEMS` (section 3) is attached to a concept: a `text` or `talk` id through `A(..., ncert=[...])` or a DOER's `ncert=`, a question id as an `FIO` item of the DOER of its topic.
- **One video per concept.** A teaching concept `A()` has exactly ONE `WE` in `examples` (that WE is the concept video) with the PLAN's own numbers: never the number set of a book question. The hook concept has `video_focus=["HOOK"]`, `enrichment=True`, no WE. DOER concepts carry a recap, their question items and at most one tool, no WE.
- **Questions.** Every book question is a `FIO(cov, label, question, steps, answer, kind=...)` in the DOER of its topic, in the book's order; `core/qt.py` moves each into its own concept of the "Questions: ..." topic that follows. `question` is the book's wording (copy it; fractions as `a/b`; blanks `___`). `steps` = the numbered working, one operation per line, a check as the last step; `answer` = the short final answer. **The displayed answer is cut at 220 characters of its LaTeX form**: keep `answer` to the final result (for multi-part questions a list such as `(a): 1/4; (b): 1/2`), the full reasoning goes in `steps`. Open-ended items get a labelled "Sample answer" and "Your answer may differ" (in the answer if short, else in the last step). Add `q_figure` (one plain sentence: ONLY what the question gives, never the answer or a hint) when the question card needs a drawing, `s_figure` (one plain sentence) when the solution card needs one (it may show the answer). Section 3 gives the exact label, wording and figures.
- **Labels (`book`) decide the Questions topic** (`core/question_naming.py`): a label starting `Let Us Do` goes to "Questions: Let us do"; any other label goes to a topic named by the text before its first colon (a trailing `, Qn` is dropped). Use exactly the labels of section 3. Keep fractions out of the label.
- Names: DOER name starts with `Practice: ` or `Pull it together and do it: `.
- An idea topic has 2 to 4 primary concepts INCLUDING its DOER (last). The intro T1 is `topic_type="framing"` with `""` objective, all concepts `enrichment=True`: C1 hook (`video_focus=["HOOK"]`) and C3 a DOER with `role="try_it"` and the free-try tool (the framing topic holds exactly ONE video, the hook, and no book question). The concept video and the opener's book question live in the idea topic T1B. The summary topic is `topic_type="summary"`, one concept, no tool, no video_focus, no WE (there is no recap video).
- Objectives: one per non-enrichment concept (teach and DOER), starts "Students ...", checkable (a verb you could test: show, name, find, compare, measure, draw, explain, read, tick, mark), shares a key term with its concept, never names a tool. Topic objective on each idea topic (none on framing and summary). Bloom words: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze.
- Misconception ids come from `content.MISCONCEPTIONS` (section 6); attach the relevant ones to teach concepts, DOERs and tools.
- `teach` lines: the first line is a prediction question ("Predict first: ..."), then the reveal, then the rule in one line. `minutes` 4 to 8.
- Tool specs (`T(...)`): section 5. Exactly 5 to 7 challenges, exactly 4 hints. `subtype="manipulative"`.
- Do not use words outside the chapter (`book_rules.json` banned_words).

Teaching concept seeds (the WE is the plan's own; these are suggestions, check each against the rule that a WE never repeats a book question's numbers):
- T1B.C1 full turn and half turn: Sahana stands on a spot facing the classroom door and turns on the spot. She turns until she faces the door again: a full turn (back to the starting position). She turns until her back is to the door: a half turn (the opposite way). A full turn brings you back, a half turn faces you the other way. The minute hand makes a full turn when it comes back to the initial position. Rule: full turn = back to the start; half turn = the opposite way.
- T2.C1 halves and quarters: a quarter turn is 1/4 of a full turn, 4 quarter turns make a full turn, so 2 quarter turns make a half turn, and 2 half turns make a full turn. WE: a seat on the giant wheel takes 1 half turn and then 2 quarter turns in the same direction: 1/2 + 2/4 = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1, a full turn, back to E.
- T2.C2 objects that turn: the greatest turn of an object can be compared with a quarter turn (a square corner as a guide): less than, exactly, or more. WE (practice picture, say so): three practice hinges, one opens exactly to the square corner (a 1/4 turn), one opens less (less than a 1/4 turn), one opens wider (more than a 1/4 turn).
- T3.C1 right, acute, obtuse: with one straw fixed and one turned: a quarter turn is a right angle, less than a quarter turn an acute angle, more than a quarter turn and less than a half turn an obtuse angle. WE: the paper fan opened to three places: P exactly a quarter turn, Q a little less than a quarter turn, R a little more than a quarter turn but less than a half turn: right, acute, obtuse.
- T3.C2 straight angle and more: two quarter turns make a half turn: a straight angle (the two straws in one line). 3/4 of a turn is more than a straight angle. WE: the fan opens by a half turn and then a quarter turn more: 1/2 + 1/4 = 3/4, more than a straight angle.
- T4.C1 eighths: a circle folded into 8 equal parts, the straw at the starting point A. Each part is a 1/8 turn; 2 parts make 2/8 = 1/4 (a right angle), 4 parts 4/8 = 1/2 (a straight angle), 6 parts 6/8 = 3/4, 8 parts 8/8 = a full turn. WE: the straw turns 5 parts and then 2 more parts: 5/8 + 2/8 = 7/8, one more part to a full turn.
- T4.C2 sixths and twelfths: fold the circle in half and each half into 3 equal parts: 6 parts, each a 1/6 turn; 3/6 = 1/2. Fold a 1/6 part in half: each small part is a 1/12 turn (12 of them make a full turn). WE: how many 1/12 pieces cover 1/6, 1/3 (= 2/6) and 1/2: 2, 4, 6.
- T5.C1 measuring an angle with pieces: lay the pieces side by side, starting on the first line; add their fractions. WE: an angle is covered exactly by one 1/6 piece and one 1/12 piece: 1/6 is 2 pieces of 1/12, so 2/12 + 1/12 = 3/12 = 1/4: a right angle.
- T5.C2 drawing an angle: put the pieces for the given turn one after another from the given line and draw the second line along the last piece; a sum such as 1/2 + 1/4 is drawn piece after piece. WE: draw an angle of 1/2 + 1/12 turn: 6/12 + 1/12 = 7/12, more than a half turn: more than a straight angle.
- T6.C1 minutes and turns: the minute hand makes a full turn in 60 minutes; so 1/12 of a turn is 5 minutes (60 ÷ 12 = 5), 15 minutes is 1/4, 30 minutes is 1/2, 45 minutes is 3/4. It turns from where it starts. WE: the minute hand starts at 2 and moves 25 minutes: 25 ÷ 5 = 5 pieces of 1/12, so 5/12 turn; from the 10 minute mark to the 35 minute mark: the hand points at 7.
- T7.C1 clockwise and anti-clockwise: the direction in which the hands of a clock move is clockwise; the opposite is anti-clockwise; a quarter turn from facing down to facing left is clockwise (6 to 9 on a clock). WE: an arrow points down; after a quarter turn it points left: clockwise (the way a clock moves from 6 to 9).
- T7.C2 facing a direction: the compass with North, East, South, West; each right angle is a quarter turn: clockwise goes North, East, South, West, North...; anti-clockwise the other way; 2 right angles is a half turn; 4 right angles is a full turn. WE: Padma faces West and turns 3 right angles anti-clockwise: West, South, East, North: she faces North, the same as 1 right angle clockwise.
- T8.C1 summary: the revision lines and short concept checks (full and half turn, quarter turn, angle names, eighths, sixths, twelfths, minute hand, clockwise).
