# Class 5 Mathematics, Chapter 3: Angles as Turns. Learning experience plan

Source: Maths Mela, Textbook of Mathematics for Grade 5, Chapter 3 (`textbooks/class-5/eemm103.pdf`, 10 PDF pages = printed pages 32 to 41; the opener page carries no printed number, it is page 32 by sequence; the printed numbers 33 to 41 sit on the next nine pages).
Written to the brief "Class 5 Mathematics Learning Experience Designer" (sections A to F). Every number, question, figure and character below was read from the page images, not from the text layer (the text layer splits every fraction into separate lines and drops the figures). The drawn angles of Let Us Do (pages 38 and 39) and of the gymnast pictures (page 36) were measured from the page's own line coordinates, and every answer in this plan was recomputed with exact fractions of a turn (`verify_facts.py` in the scratchpad folder).
Page references use the printed page number. "LUD" means a "Let Us Do" box; the chapter has two of them (page 34, parts (a) to (d), and page 38, questions 1 to 6), plus one "Let Us Think" (page 37) and one "Fun with Turns" (pages 40 and 41).
The chapter measures angles only as parts of a turn (1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/6, 1/12). The plan never uses degrees, a protractor or any other idea the chapter does not teach.

## SECTION A: Chapter Understanding

### A1. Chapter name
Angles as Turns (Class 5, Maths Mela, Chapter 3).

### A2. Main learning objectives
Students will be able to:
1. Recognise a full turn (back to the starting position) and a half turn (facing the opposite way) in games, clocks and wheels, and say how they know.
2. Join half turns and quarter turns and say what turn they make in all (two half turns, two quarter turns, four quarter turns).
3. Compare the greatest turn of an everyday object (clothes clip, door, tongs, scissors, file cover) with a quarter turn.
4. See that an angle arises when something turns, and name right (a quarter turn), acute (less than a quarter turn), obtuse (more than a quarter and less than a half turn) and straight (a half turn) angles, and a turn of 3/4 that is more than a straight angle.
5. Show and read turns of the straw on a circle folded into 8 equal parts (1/8, 2/8 = 1/4, 4/8 = 1/2, 6/8 = 3/4, 8/8 = a full turn) and on a circle folded into 6 equal parts (1/6, 2/6, and 1/12 as half of 1/6).
6. Guess and check the measure of a given angle by combining the 1/8, 2/8, 1/6, 2/6 and 1/12 pieces, name it as acute, right or obtuse, and read the turn an arrow has made.
7. Draw an angle for a given turn on a given line (1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/12, and sums such as 1/2 + 1/4).
8. Relate the minutes a minute hand moves to the fraction of a full turn it makes (15 minutes is 1/4, 30 is 1/2, 45 is 3/4, 60 is a full turn, 5 minutes is 1/12) and mark its new position.
9. Use the words clockwise and anti-clockwise for the direction of a turn, and find the direction someone faces after right-angle turns (compass directions).

### A5. Natural learning sequence
Turning on the spot (full and half turn) -> half and quarter turns add up, and how far everyday things turn -> the angle that a turn makes and its names (right, acute, obtuse, straight) -> measuring turns with a circle in 8, 6 and 12 equal parts -> guessing and checking angles, and drawing angles for given turns -> the minute hand as a turn -> clockwise and anti-clockwise and facing a direction -> revision. This is the book's own order. Two small changes: (1) the opener's two questions (the statue picture, real-life turns) are asked in the first idea topic, after the free try; (2) the full turn of the giant wheel (p. 33) is taught with the wheel questions, in the second topic, not in the first.

### A6. Points the textbook leaves unclear (to confirm; the plan does not guess)
1. **Fun with Turns, Q1, last row (p. 41).** The row reads "West | right angles clockwise, four 1/2 right angles anti-clockwise | (blank)". The number in front of "right angles clockwise" is not printed (checked on the page image and in the text layer). Four half right angles are 2 right angles anti-clockwise, so the ending direction depends on the missing number. The plan gives the rule (the start turned that number minus 2 right angles clockwise) and one sample, 3 right angles clockwise, which ends at North. Please confirm the intended number.
2. **The statue picture (p. 32).** After "STATUE!" B and C both face the front, as they did at the start; A has turned to face away; D faces the side. The book asks for "the child who has made a full turn" in the singular. The plan reads B and C as full turns (they face the same way as at the start), A as a half turn, and says D has made neither.
3. **The notebook house (p. 35), angles A to H.** The book prints no key and the picture is drawn in perspective. The plan reads them from the red arcs: A obtuse (the roof), B right, C acute, D acute, E acute, F right, G right, H obtuse.
4. **LUD p. 38, Q1.** The seven angles are drawn by hand on a dotted grid and carry no letters or numbers. The plan numbers them 1 to 7 from the left (4 is the upper one of the two in the middle) and reads their measures from the line coordinates of the page: about 1/12, 1/6, 1/8, 1/6, 1/4, 1/3, 3/8 turn. The question says "guess", so the answer is "about"; 1/3 is shown with the 2/6 piece.
5. **LUD p. 38, Q2 (d).** The arrow starts at the arrow pointing to the upper right (an eighth of a turn from the top) and ends pointing down and a little to the left. The turn is not a neat fraction of the pieces; the plan reads it as about 5/12 = 1/4 + 1/6 (the nearest turn that the chapter's pieces make). Parts (b) and (c) are exact: 5/8 and 1/8.
6. **LUD p. 39, Q4.** Each given line has a dot at both ends and the book does not say which end is the corner of the angle. The plan lets the student choose either end; the answer describes the second line drawn from one end.
7. **Let Us Think (p. 37).** The book does not say in which direction the turns go. The green dashed arrow in each circle goes the way the hands of a clock move, and the plan reads the turns that way. The words clockwise and anti-clockwise only come on p. 40.
8. **The table of everyday objects (p. 33).** "Maximum possible turn" depends on the real object (the book says "check and tick"). The plan gives sample ticks for common objects (clothes clip less than 1/4 turn, door more than 1/4, tongs less than 1/4, scissors 1/4, file cover more than 1/4) and says each student's own objects may differ. The half turn and full turn questions follow: sample answers (door and file cover can open flat, a half turn; none turns all the way round).
9. **"Try these" (p. 40).** The usual answer, anti-clockwise for opening a tap, unscrewing a lid and loosening a nut, is a real-world fact, not printed in the book. It is given as the usual direction, "your tap may differ".
10. **"Can the two acute turns together make another acute turn?" (p. 35).** The book gives no answer. The plan reasons it out: sometimes. Two small acute turns together can still be less than a quarter turn; two bigger acute turns together can make a right angle or an obtuse angle.
11. **LUD (c), the 5-sided shape (p. 35)** has many answers. The plan gives one sample (a house shape) and says the student's shape may differ.
12. **Paper, straws, ice cream sticks, tracing paper and paper clips** are the student's own materials; the chapter itself says how to make each piece. No kit or cut-out sheet is referred to, so no printed sheet is missing. The tools replace the straw, the paper fan and the tracing-paper circles with on-screen ones that follow the chapter's own pieces (eighths, sixths, twelfths).
13. **"Note for Teachers" (pp. 32 and 37).** These notes give a teacher a role. They are not made into content (no teaching role is given to a teacher). The statue game itself is in the hook.
14. **LUD p. 39, Q5, 1/8 + 1/6 turn.** The plan classifies it by comparison with 1/4 and 1/2 (1/6 is more than 1/8, so the sum is more than 1/8 + 1/8 = 1/4, and it is less than 1/4 + 1/4 = 1/2): obtuse.
15. The QR code on the opener page is not readable from the supplied file; nothing is taken from it.

## SECTION C: Interactive Tool Specifications

Common to every tool (details in sections D and E): one activity at a time; Back and Next always in the same place; progress dots; Hint, Retry, Reset (and Undo where objects move); specific feedback ("two quarter turns make a half turn"), never only right or wrong; the turning object is the largest thing on screen. A turn is always shown from a fixed starting position, as a start mark and a curved arrow that grows as the object turns. Practice values the plan adds are labelled "practice values"; every other number is the book's. The word "degrees" never appears; every turn is a fraction of a full turn.

### Tool 1. Statue Turn
- **Connected topic:** The Statue Game (the opening topic, free first try right after the hook; the concept video follows it). Page 32.
- **Learning objective:** Students tell a full turn from a half turn by comparing where the child faces now with where the child faced at the start. The tool uses only full turns and half turns: no angle names and no fractions of a turn.
- **Why a tool:** turning is a movement; the idea only becomes clear when students make the child turn, stop, and compare with the starting position.
- **Environment:** the school playground at morning break; a child stands on a painted ring on the ground with one foot fixed on a mark.
- **Objects:** a child (Ashutosh, then Sahana) seen from above and from the front, standing on a ring; a start mark and a start arrow on the ground showing the way the child faced at the start; a turning arrow that follows the child's face; a STATUE! button; a ring-trace that the free foot draws on the ground (complete circle or half-moon); four picture cards of statues for sorting.
- **Controls:** press and hold Turn to make the child turn on the spot (or drag the child round); press STATUE! to freeze; a Same way / Opposite way / Neither check; drag cards into three bins; Check, Hint, Retry, Reset.
- **What the student does:** The hook's picture, four children turning round and freezing. One line: "Turn the child and call Statue. Where is the child facing now, compared with the start?" (1) turn Ashutosh until he faces the same way as at the start and press STATUE!: the free foot has drawn a complete circle, a full turn; (2) turn Sahana until she faces the opposite way: the foot has drawn a half-moon, a half turn; (3) freeze a child that faces neither the start way nor the opposite way (practice values): neither a full nor a half turn; (4) sort four statue cards into Full turn, Half turn and Neither. The book's question unchanged: "Can you recognise the child in the picture who has made a full turn? Who has made a half turn? How do you know?" The student picks the cards that build the answer (faces the same way as at the start; faces the opposite way). The concept video that follows names the two turns.
- **Feedback and hints:** "The child faces the same way as at the start: that is a full turn." "The child faces the opposite way: that is a half turn." Hints: where did the child face at the start; where does the child face now; is it the same way or the opposite way; bottom-out turns the child to the answer position.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry returns the child to the start for this card; Reset restores the whole tool.

### Tool 2. Quarter Turn Check
- **Connected topic:** Objects That Turn (the second of the two topics, after Halves and Quarters Make a Full Turn). Page 33.
- **Learning objective:** Students use the quarter turn as a yardstick. One purpose, two clearly separated activities: Activity 1 joins half turns and quarter turns into a full turn; Activity 2 compares the greatest turn of an object with a quarter turn.
- **Why a tool:** adding turns and checking how far something opens are both done by turning and comparing, which is easiest on a screen that shows the start, the quarter-turn guide and the turn made.
- **Environment:** the school fair at the edge of the playground. Two big activity tabs: Activity 1, the giant wheel with its starting seat E; Activity 2, a table of everyday things. Only one activity is on screen at a time and Activity 2 opens after Activity 1 is done.
- **Objects:** Activity 1: the giant wheel with a seat at the top marked E, a turning arrow, turn buttons "half turn" and "quarter turn", a total read-out. Activity 2: five everyday objects (clothes clip, door with hinge, tongs, scissors, file cover) each with an opening arm you can drag, a quarter-turn guide (a square corner marker on the starting arm), three bins: less than a 1/4 turn, 1/4 turn, more than a 1/4 turn.
- **Controls:** Activity 1: tap a turn button, Undo, pick answer cards. Activity 2: drag the arm of an object open as far as it goes, switch the guide on, drag the object card into a bin. Both: Check, Hint, Retry, Reset.
- **What the student does:** Activity 1: the wheel turning from E and coming back to E: "How many half turns bring the seat back to E?" Activity 2: a door beside the corner of a book: "Does the door open less than, exactly to or wider than the corner?" Activity 1: (1) join 1 half turn and 2 quarter turns (practice values) and read the total; (2) join 3 quarter turns and 1 more. Activity 2: (1) open a practice hinge until it meets the guide: a quarter turn; (2) open two more practice hinges, one narrower and one wider; (3) sort them. The book's questions unchanged. Activity 1: Reema takes two half turns in the same direction (like a ___ turn); 2 quarter turns; 4 quarter turns. Activity 2: the table "What is the maximum possible turn in each of these cases? Check and tick" for the clothes clip, door with hinge, tongs, scissors and file cover.
- **Feedback and hints:** "1 half turn and 2 quarter turns: 1/2 + 2/4 = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1, a full turn." "This hinge opens wider than the guide: more than a 1/4 turn." Hints: where does the arm start; how far does it go; is the arm before, at or after the guide; bottom-out opens the arm to its limit.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry returns the wheel to E or the arm to closed; Reset restores the activity.

### Tool 3. Straw and Fan Angles
- **Connected topic:** Angles from Turns (Right, Acute and Obtuse Angles; Straight Angle and More Than a Straight Angle). Pages 33 to 35.
- **Learning objective:** Students turn one arm away from a fixed arm and name the angle the turn makes: acute, right, obtuse or straight, or more than a straight angle.
- **Why a tool:** the name of the angle depends on how far the arm has turned from the fixed one, and only turning shows this.
- **Environment:** Pragya's desk in the classroom corner: a green straw held fixed with a paper clip joint, a yellow straw that turns, and a paper fan with two end sticks.
- **Objects:** a green straw (fixed, along the start line) and a yellow straw (turns about the clip), a shaded wedge that shows the turn, three guides (quarter turn, half turn, three-quarter turn) that can be switched on, a name card that shows the angle name, the paper fan (opens and closes with its two end sticks), a turn read-out in words (less than 1/4, 1/4, between 1/4 and 1/2, 1/2, more than 1/2).
- **Controls:** drag the yellow straw (or the fan stick) round; tap Guide to show the quarter, half and three-quarter marks; tap Add a turn to turn again from where it stopped; pick a name card; Check, Hint, Retry, Reset.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture, a straw turned a quarter turn making a square corner. One line: "Turn the yellow straw. What angle does it make with the green straw?" (1) make a right angle (a quarter turn); (2) make an acute angle; (3) make an obtuse angle; (4) make a straight angle with two quarter turns; (5) turn on to 3/4 of a turn and name it (more than a straight angle). The book's items unchanged: "Use your paper fan to show different acute angles and obtuse angles." and "Make an acute turn with the straws or fan. Continue to make another acute turn. Can the two acute turns together make another acute turn?"
- **Feedback and hints:** "The yellow straw is less than a quarter turn from the green one: an acute angle." Hints: where is the quarter-turn mark; is the straw before or after it; is it before or after the half-turn mark; bottom-out puts the straw at the answer.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry returns the yellow straw to the green one; Reset restores the straws and the fan.

### Tool 4. Turn Disc
- **Connected topic:** Measuring Turns with a Disc (Measuring Turns in Eighths; Measuring Turns in Sixths and Twelfths). Pages 36 to 37.
- **Learning objective:** Students measure turns on a circle cut into equal parts, one division at a time, and read each turn as a fraction of a full turn. The tool measures turns only. Stage 1 eighths (2/8 = 1/4, 4/8 = 1/2, 6/8 = 3/4, 8/8 = a full turn); Stage 2 sixths; Stage 3 twelfths (half of 1/6 is 1/12).
- **Why a tool:** the book's tracing-paper circle with a straw is a manipulative; the tool gives every student the disc and checks the reading.
- **Environment:** a craft table with a tracing-paper circle, a straw at the centre and the starting point A marked on the edge.
- **Objects:** a round disc with fold lines, a straw at the centre, the starting point A, a turning arrow that grows from A, a read-out "n of N parts = n/N turn", three stage tabs (Stage 1: Eighths, Stage 2: Sixths, Stage 3: Twelfths) each with its own disc of 8, 6 or 12 equal parts, 1/12 pieces to lay on a 1/6 piece (Stage 3), and a place to write the equal names. A stage opens only after the one before is done; the divisions are never mixed on one disc.
- **Controls:** drag the straw (it snaps to the fold lines) or press + and - to move it one part; Show equal names; Check, Hint, Retry, Reset.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture, the disc in 8 equal parts with the straw on A: "Turn the straw by one part. What fraction of a full turn is it?" Stage 1: turn 3 parts of the 8 and read the turn (practice values); show 1/4 and find that it is 2/8. Stage 2: turn 3 parts of the 6 and read the half turn. Stage 3: lay two 1/12 pieces on a 1/6 piece. The book's items unchanged, one stage each. Stage 1: "Show a 1/8 turn of the straw. Show a 2/8 turn. Show a 1/4 turn. Show a 3/8 turn." and "Continue turning by 5/8, 6/8, 7/8, and 8/8. Check when you have completed a 3/4 turn and a full turn." Stage 2: "Show the turns of the straw equal to 1/6, 2/6, and so on until you complete a full circle." Stage 3: "Can you guess what turn of the straw will be equal to half of a 1/6 turn?"
- **Feedback and hints:** "The straw has moved 2 of 8 parts: a 2/8 turn, the same as a 1/4 turn." Hints: how many equal parts has the disc; how many parts has the straw moved; what fraction is that of the whole disc; bottom-out moves the straw to the answer.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry returns the straw to A; Reset restores the stage.

### Tool 5. Angle Check and Draw
- **Connected topic:** Drawing an Angle (the second of the two topics, after Measuring an Angle). Pages 38 to 39.
- **Learning objective:** Students link the size of an angle to a turn. One purpose, two clearly separated activities, measuring first and drawing second: Activity 1 guesses the turn an angle makes and checks it by laying the 1/8, 2/8, 1/6, 2/6 and 1/12 pieces on it; Activity 2 draws a second line to make an angle for a given turn.
- **Why a tool:** the pieces have to be laid on the angle to be sure, and drawing an angle needs a moving line that stops at the right turn.
- **Environment:** a craft table with dotted paper and the pieces cut from the tracing-paper circle (the book's angle measuring tools). Two activity tabs: Activity 1: Measure, Activity 2: Draw. Activity 2 opens after Activity 1 is done.
- **Objects:** a dotted sheet; Activity 1: an angle drawn from a dot, the five wedge pieces with their fractions on them, a guess box, a running total, an angle-name card (acute, right, obtuse); Activity 2: a given line with a dot at each end and a turning second line.
- **Controls:** Activity 1: type or tap a guess; drag wedge pieces into the angle (they snap side by side, starting at the first line); tap an angle name. Activity 2: turn the second line with + and - pieces and press Draw. Both: Check, Hint, Retry, Reset, Undo.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture, an angle covered by one 1/6 piece and one 1/12 piece: "Guess how much turn this angle makes, then check with the pieces." Activity 1 (measure): (1) guess and check an angle covered by a 1/4 piece and a 1/12 piece (practice values); (2) an angle covered by two 1/8 pieces; (3) read the turn an arrow makes between two arrows. Activity 2 (draw): (1) draw an angle for 1/6 on a given line; (2) draw an angle for 1/2 + 1/12. The book's items unchanged. Activity 1: "Guess the measures of each of the angles shown below. Then, check using your angle measuring tools. You may need to use a combination of measures. Also, state whether each of the angles is acute, right, or obtuse." Activity 2: "Draw angles for the given measures of turns using the given lines."
- **Feedback and hints:** "The 1/4 piece and the 1/12 piece together make 3/12 + 1/12 = 4/12 = 1/3: an obtuse angle." Hints: which piece fits first; how much of the angle is left; which piece fills the gap; bottom-out places the next piece.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the pieces or the line on this card; Reset restores the activity.

### Tool 6. Minute Hand Turner
- **Connected topic:** The Minute Hand Turns (Minutes and Turns of the Minute Hand). Pages 39 to 40.
- **Learning objective:** Students link the minutes the minute hand moves to the fraction of a full turn it makes, and mark its new position from its starting position.
- **Why a tool:** dragging the hand round and seeing the swept part and the minutes together makes 60 minutes = a full turn something students see.
- **Environment:** the clock on the school wall, large, with the minute hand and the hour numbers 1 to 12 drawn as in the book.
- **Objects:** a large clock face with the minute hand, a shaded sweep that grows from the starting position, a read-out "moved by __ minutes" and "turn __ of a full turn", a marker for the starting position, a minute-mark ring that shows 5-minute steps.
- **Controls:** drag the minute hand clockwise (it snaps to 5-minute steps; a switch adds 1-minute steps), or tap +5 and −5; choose a starting position; tap a fraction card and the hand moves to it; Check, Hint, Retry, Reset.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture, the minute hand turning from 12 round to 3. One line: "How many minutes is a full turn of the minute hand?" (1) start at 12 and move 20 minutes (practice values) and read the turn; (2) start at 6 and move 5 minutes: 1/12 of a turn; (3) start at 2 and move 25 minutes; (4) give a fraction card, 1/2, and read the minutes; (5) find where the hand stops. The book's items unchanged: "Guess the measure of turns the minute hand of a clock makes in each of the following cases. The initial position of the minute hand is given. Draw the final position of the minute hand on the clock face." with parts (a) to (g).
- **Feedback and hints:** "The hand moved 15 minutes: a quarter of 60 minutes, so a 1/4 turn." Hints: how many minutes make a full turn; what part of 60 is this; where was the hand; bottom-out moves the hand.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry returns the hand to its starting position; Reset restores the clock.

### Tool 7. Turn and Face
- **Connected topic:** Which Direction? (Clockwise and Anti-clockwise; Finding the Final Direction After Right-Angle Turns). Pages 40 to 41.
- **Learning objective:** Students find the final facing direction after turns of right angles, clockwise or anti-clockwise, from North, East, South or West. Activity 1 is only a short recap of clockwise and anti-clockwise; Activity 2 is the main work.
- **Why a tool:** the final direction is only felt by turning, and the compass shows the result at once.
- **Environment:** a compass painted on the playground ground with North at the top, East on the right, South at the bottom and West on the left, and a child in the middle. Two activity tabs: Activity 1: Which way? (a short recap), Activity 2: Where do I face?
- **Objects:** the compass, a child that turns (shown from above), a clock-hand sign that shows the clockwise direction, turn buttons (1 right angle, 1/2 right angle, clockwise or anti-clockwise), a starting direction selector, a direction read-out, a table to complete.
- **Controls:** tap a turn button; choose clockwise or anti-clockwise; choose the starting direction; type the ending direction; Check, Hint, Retry, Reset, Undo.
- **What the student does:** An arrow turning the way a clock moves: press Clockwise and watch the child turn one right angle, then press Anti-clockwise and watch the child turn the other way. (1) from East, one right angle clockwise: South; (2) from West, 3 right angles anti-clockwise (practice values): North; (3) from North, a half turn clockwise and a half turn anti-clockwise, and compare where each ends; (4) from North, one right angle each way. The book's items unchanged: "Complete the table by filling the direction the children will face on completing the given turns."; "Padma is facing the toy shop. What place will she face if she takes a half turn clockwise? What other way can she turn to face the same place?" (The four creatures stay a question card only; telling the direction of a turn belongs to the Clockwise and Anti-clockwise video.)
- **Feedback and hints:** "From North, two right angles clockwise: North, East, South. You face South." Hints: which way does a clock go; how many right angles; where do you start; bottom-out turns the child step by step.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry returns the child to the starting direction; Reset restores the tool.

## SECTION D: Shared Visual Language

- **Overall illustration style:** warm, colourful, polished educational-game illustrations; 3D or isometric where the scene holds objects, soft shadows, clear shapes, clean uncluttered compositions; gentle animation. The turn is always the largest, clearest thing on screen: a start mark, a turning arm and a curved arrow.
- **Chapter theme and environment:** "Playground Day". Every setting of the book sits in one friendly Indian school day: the painted ring on the playground ground for the statue game and the compass, the giant wheel at the school fair next to the playground, a table of everyday things, the classroom desk with Pragya's straws, a craft table for the tracing-paper discs, the clock on the school wall, and the little street outside the gate (toy shop, bookstall, ice cream cart) for Padma. Each tool is a station there. Nothing is added that does not help the mathematics.
- **Characters:** only the book's: Ashutosh, Sahana, Reema, Pragya, Padma, and the four children A, B, C, D of the statue picture. They appear to set a situation, never to teach.
- **Colour and visual direction:** warm cream and sunny yellow for the ground and walls; the start mark and the fixed arm are teal; the turning arm and the turn it makes are orange; correct is green and "look again" is soft amber (never red). Each angle name has its own colour used the same way everywhere (acute light blue, right green, obtuse purple, straight orange).
- **Main visual objects:** a circle or disc with fold lines, a straw or arm turning from a start mark, a shaded wedge for the turn made, a square-corner marker for a quarter turn, the clock face, the compass; fraction labels in a large clear font.
- **Animation style:** only meaningful movement: an arm swings round, the shaded wedge grows behind it, a curved arrow follows, the wheel turns. No decoration that moves.
- **UI layout and navigation:** one activity per screen; instruction strip at the top (one sentence, large); the working area in the middle; Hint, Retry, Reset (and Undo) in a fixed row at the bottom; Back and Next fixed at the bottom corners; progress dots under the instruction. Same placement in all seven tools.
- **Common interaction patterns:** drag an arm to turn it, tap +/− to turn one part, a Check button that gives specific feedback, a three-step hint ladder with a worked step at the end, Retry for the step, Reset for the tool.

## SECTION E: Learning and Platform Rules

1. Videos explain; tools let the student explore, apply and get feedback. No classroom teacher is given a teaching role anywhere (the book's "Note for Teachers" is not used).
2. Every tool has one distinct mathematical purpose; no interaction is repeated across tools without a learning reason. Tool 1 turns a child (full or half), Tool 2 uses the quarter turn as a yardstick, Tool 3 names angles, Tool 4 reads turns as fractions on a disc, Tool 5 measures and draws angles with pieces, Tool 6 links minutes to turns, Tool 7 turns by right angles in a direction.
3. A tool is made from its concept description: the idea, the screen, what the student does, the practice cards, feedback and hints. It is not scripted in phases.
4. A tool card that quotes the book uses the book's own questions, with their original wording, numbers and conditions. The plan does not change them and does not invent a challenge where the book has none.
5. Preserve the book's mathematics: its pieces (1/8, 2/8, 1/6, 2/6, 1/12), its clock positions, its compass, its angles. Any number the plan adds is labelled "practice values".
6. Do not introduce ideas outside the chapter: no degrees, protractor, reflex angle, vertex, complementary or supplementary angles, radians, coordinates or bearings. A turn is a fraction of a full turn.
7. One video per book question, on its answer, plus a question card and a solution card; one video per teaching idea; the hook comes first and does not explain the chapter.
8. Answers the book does not print are worked out from the chapter's own ideas and shown as the plan's reading (section A6).
9. Text hygiene: no em or en dash; minus is the true minus sign; students never read "NCERT", "textbook", page numbers or exercise numbers; say "your maths book".
10. Instructions are short, age-appropriate, one at a time; feedback is specific; hint, retry and reset are always present.
11. One visual identity (Playground Day) and one navigation layout across all tools.
12. Verify page numbers, figures and numbers against the book's page images before any build.
