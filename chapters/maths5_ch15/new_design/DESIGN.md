## 1. Hard rules (gates enforce most; fix content, never a gate)
- Class 5 students, 10 to 11 years old: short plain sentences. Characters and settings are the book's: Samaira, Kabir, Joseph Uncle, Dipesh, Deepti, Raman, Sheela, Rani; a notebook of TV answers, a toy shop, scooters on a road, a school day, a school canteen, a picture of school children. The plan's own WE use the same kinds of things. Do not invent facts or datasets: every real-world number that is not a plain WE number comes from the book or from section 4. A WE or sample number the plan adds is a practice value and says so ("practice values") where a student could mistake it for the book's.
- **No em dash and no en dash** in any string or comment (the book's dashes become commas, colons or "to"). Minus is U+2212 `−`. Use `×` and `÷`. Hyphen only inside words ("Puri-Sabzi", "Idli-Sambar", "two-wheelers" are fine).
- **Fractions are written `a/b` with a plain slash and no spaces** (`1/2`). A mixed number is a whole number, a space and a fraction: `1 1/2`, `2 1/2`. Never the characters ½ ⅓ ¼. Hours are written as the book writes them: `1/2 hr`, `1 hr`, `1 1/2 hr`, `2 hr`, `2 1/2 hr` for an answer in the notebook; `1/2 hour`, `1 hour`, `1 1/2 hour`, `2 hours`, `More than 2 hours` for the table rows. Units are separated from numbers by a space: `9 cm`, `30 kg`. A blank standing alone is `___`. A part label is `(a)` before the part, once per part, and is followed by a colon in the answer (`(a): False; (b): True`).
- Never write "NCERT", "textbook", "page N", "Fig.", "Figure N" or exercise numbers in anything the student reads (names, teach lines, steps, questions, tool text). Say "your maths book". Exception: the `textbook=[...]` argument of `A()` quotes the book verbatim (stored as a `textbook_text` block), still no dashes.
- **Arithmetic must be true in every string.** The equation gate `eqcheck` checks only whole-number chains (`4 ÷ 2 = 2`, `2 × 3 = 6`) and SKIPS any chain that touches a slash. Hours with halves, units, comparisons and counts are NOT machine checked: you must re-compute every one with Python before you finish. Do not write an equation that is only approximately true. Keep whole-number chains on one line, and do not write a whole-number chain whose sides are different numbers of the same thing.
- **Words outside the chapter are banned** (they are listed in `book_rules.json`): tally, tally marks, frequency, average, mean, median, mode, range, percent, percentage, decimal, line graph, pie chart, histogram, double bar graph, axis, x-axis, y-axis, coordinates, half icon, part icon, probability. Use the book's own words: data, table, pictograph, icon, scale, bar graph, vertical line, horizontal base, bar, record, "stands for". No new concept: no half icons, no scale that leaves a part icon, no average.
- 100% coverage: every id in `content.NCERT_ITEMS` (section 3) is attached to a concept: a `text` or `talk` id through `A(..., ncert=[...])` (or a WE's `ncert=`) or a DOER's `ncert=`, a question id as an `FIO` item of the DOER named in section 3.
- **One video per concept.** A teaching concept `A()` has exactly ONE `WE` in `examples` (that WE is the concept video) with the PLAN's own numbers (section 2): never the numbers of a book question. The hook concept has `video_focus=["HOOK"]`, `enrichment=True`, no WE. DOER concepts carry a recap, their question items and at most one tool, no WE.
- **Questions.** Every book question is a `FIO(cov, label, question, steps, answer, kind=..., q_figure=..., s_figure=...)` in the DOER of its topic, in the book's order; `core/qt.py` moves each into its own concept of the "Questions: ..." topic that follows. `question` is the book's wording (section 3; "textbook" never; blanks `___`). `steps` = the numbered working, one operation per line, values before combining, a check as the last step (at most 8 steps; a card holds about 8 lines); `answer` = short final answer, **under 150 characters** (the displayed answer is cut at 220 characters of its LaTeX form: keep to the value, put the working in `steps`). Open-ended items get a labelled "Sample answer" built by reasoning and say "Your answer may differ". `q_figure`: ONE plain sentence of only what the question gives, never the answer or a hint; use the constants of `figs.py`. `s_figure`: one sentence for the solution card (it may show the answer).
- **Labels (`book`) decide the Questions topic** (`core/question_naming.py`): the text before the first colon names the topic ("Questions: Child TV Reporter"); use exactly the labels of section 3 (several FIO of one topic share one heading, so they share one Questions topic).
- Names: DOER name starts with `Practice: ` or `Pull it together and do it: `.
- An idea topic has 2 to 4 primary concepts INCLUDING its DOER (last). The intro T1 is `topic_type="framing"` with `""` objective, all concepts `enrichment=True`: C1 hook (`video_focus=["HOOK"]`) and C3 a DOER with `role="try_it"` and the free-try tool (the framing topic holds exactly ONE video, the hook, and no book question). The concept video and the book questions of the opener live in the idea topic T1B. The summary topic is `topic_type="summary"`, one concept, no tool, no video_focus, no WE (there is no recap video).
- Objectives: one per non-enrichment concept, starts "Students ...", checkable (a verb you could test: record, count, show, read, compare, make, find, explain), shares a key term with the concept, never names a tool. Topic objective on each idea topic. Bloom words: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze.
- Misconception ids come from `content.MISCONCEPTIONS` (section 6); attach the relevant ones to teach concepts, DOERs and tools.
- `teach` lines: the first line is a prediction question ("Predict first: ..."), then the reveal, then the rule in one line. `minutes` 4 to 8.
- Tool specs (`T(...)`): section 5. Exactly 5 to 7 challenges, exactly 4 hints. Describe what to build; do not build it.

### Teaching concepts: the plan's own worked example (WE) of each. The numbers are fixed here and are practice values; check each against the free list (never a book question's numbers).
For each: the `Predict first:` line, the steps, the rule line, the objective seed. Write the steps in your own words, one operation per line, with a Check line last.

- **T1B.C1 Counting and Recording in a Table.** Predict: Kabir asks 12 friends how many hours a day they read; the answers are 1, 2, 1, 1, 3, 2, 1, 2, 1, 3, 1, 2. How can he show them so that anyone can read them at a glance? Reveal: make a table with one row for each kind of answer (1 hour, 2 hours, 3 hours); move each answer into its row, once; count each row: 1 hour 6, 2 hours 4, 3 hours 2; check 6 + 4 + 2 = 12; a row can also name a group of answers, such as "More than 2 hours" in the book's table; "more than 1 hour" adds the rows after it: 4 + 2 = 6; "less than 3 hours" adds the rows before it: 6 + 4 = 10. Rule: one row for each kind of answer, count each row, and check that the counts add up to the number of friends. WE (practice values): the 12 answers; table; "How many friends read for more than 1 hour?" answer 6. Texts: `ncert=["tv.tablehead", "tv.eyes"]` (the eyes prompt is a `talk`: one line in teach, "Can watching TV for a long time be harmful to the eyes? Think about it."). Objective seed: Students record 12 answers in a table with one row for each answer, count each row and check that the counts add up to 12. Key terms: table, row, record, data. Misconceptions M_COUNT_TWICE, M_MORE_THAN_INCLUDES.
- **T2.C1 One Icon for Many Items.** Predict (this is the book's "Suggest some ways to him"): a shop has 35 erasers. Drawing one picture for each is too much work. What could the shopkeeper do? Reveal: one icon stands for 5 erasers: this is a scale; 7 icons show 35 because 7 × 5 = 35; read a pictograph by counting the icons and multiplying by the scale. Rule: number of items = number of icons × scale, and a scale helps record a large number with fewer icons. WE (scale 5, practice values): a stationery shop has 10 pens, 20 pencils and 35 erasers: icons 2, 4 and 7; check by reading back 2 × 5 = 10, 4 × 5 = 20, 7 × 5 = 35; 2 + 4 + 7 = 13 icons in all and 13 × 5 = 65 items, and 10 + 20 + 35 = 65. Texts: `ncert=["shop.story", "shop.suggest", "shop.scale"]` (`shop.suggest` is a `talk`). Objective seed: Students show numbers as icons using a scale of 5 and read a row of icons back as a number by multiplying by the scale. Key terms: pictograph, icon, scale. Misconception M_ICON_IS_ONE.
- **T2.C2 Choosing a Different Scale.** Predict: a fruit stall has 12 apples, 18 oranges and 30 bananas. Can one icon stand for 4 fruits? Reveal: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 but 18 ÷ 4 is 4 with 2 left over, so a scale of 4 does not fit; a scale of 6 fits: 2, 3 and 5 icons; a scale of 3 fits too: 4, 6 and 10 icons; a bigger scale needs fewer icons; choose a scale that fits every number and gives a picture that is easy to read. Rule: choose a scale that every number can be shared into with nothing left over. WE (practice values): the fruit stall; scales 6 and 3 (the scale 5 leaves 2 for 12: do not mention it unless useful). Objective seed: Students choose a scale that fits all the numbers of a table, show the numbers with it and explain why another scale does not fit. Misconceptions M_SCALE_NO_FIT, M_BIG_SCALE_MORE. No ncert text id.
- **T3.C1 Comparing with a Pictograph.** Predict: a snack stall's pictograph has 3 icons for Tuesday and 7 icons for Thursday, one icon = 10 samosas. Which day sold more, and how many more? Reveal: read each row (icons × scale), the longest row is the most when the icon and scale are the same; the total is all the icons × the scale; how many more or fewer = the difference of the two numbers (also the difference of the icons times the scale). WE (scale 10, practice values): Tuesday 3 icons = 30, Thursday 7 icons = 70, Saturday 2 icons = 20; the most: Thursday 70; total 3 + 7 + 2 = 12 icons, 12 × 10 = 120 samosas (30 + 70 + 20 = 120); 30 − 20 = 10 fewer on Saturday than Tuesday; 70 − 20 = 50 more on Thursday than Saturday. Texts: `ncert=["road.story"]`. Objective seed: Students read the numbers of a pictograph, find the most and the total, and find how many more or how many fewer one row is than another. Misconception M_ICON_IS_ONE.
- **T4.C1 From Squares to Bars.** Predict: Kabir records his Saturday with a row of squares for each activity, one square = 1 hour: reading 3 squares, playing 5 squares, helping at home 2 squares. If each row is stood up like a tower, what do we get? Reveal: a bar for each activity; the height of a bar is the number of squares; this way of recording is called a bar graph (the book's bold term); the vertical line shows the number of hours, the horizontal base shows the different activities, the bars show how much time is spent on each activity. WE (practice values): Kabir's Saturday: base with reading, playing, helping at home; vertical line marked 0 to 6; bars 3, 5, 2; check heights equal squares; 3 + 5 + 2 = 10 hours. Texts: `ncert=["day.story", "day.bar", "day.diff"]` (`day.diff` is a `talk`: "How is Sheela's recording different from Raman's recording? Discuss in class." one line in teach). Objective seed: Students show a row of squares as a bar of the same height and name the vertical line, the horizontal base and the bars of a bar graph. Key terms: bar graph, bar, vertical line, horizontal base. Misconception M_BAR_WIDTH.
- **T4.C2 Reading and Comparing Bars.** Predict: Kabir's reading row has 3 squares and Samaira's reading bar is 4 high. Who read for longer, and by how many hours? Reveal: read a bar where its top meets the numbers of the vertical line; the taller bar is more; the difference of the heights is how many more; two bars of the same height are the same amount. WE (practice values): Kabir's Saturday (reading 3, playing 5, helping at home 2 squares) and Samaira's bar graph (reading 4, playing 3, helping at home 2): who spent more time reading (Samaira, 4 > 3), how many hours more (4 − 3 = 1), who played more and by how many (Kabir, 5 − 3 = 2), the activity with the same time (helping at home, 2 and 2). Objective seed: Students read the height of bars and compare two children's data, saying who has more, how many more and which activity is the same. Misconception M_BAR_LABEL.
- **T5.C1 Making a Bar Graph.** Predict: Kabir's 24 hours are sleeping 8, school 6, studying 3, playing 4, other 3. How tall must the vertical line go so that the biggest bar fits, and what goes along the base? Reveal, in steps: write the names of the activities along the horizontal base, one place for each, equal spaces; mark equal steps on the vertical line from 0 up to a number at least as big as the biggest number (8); draw each bar from the base up to its number, all bars the same width; check that every bar's height matches its number and that the numbers add up to 24. WE (practice values): Kabir's day as above; 8 + 6 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 24. Objective seed: Students make a bar graph from a table of five numbers by writing the names on the base and drawing each bar up to its number. No ncert text id. Misconception M_BAR_WIDTH.
- **T5.C2 A Bar Graph of Lengths.** Predict: Samaira tears paper strips as long as three pencils, 9 cm, 6 cm and 4 cm, and stands them side by side on a graph. Which strip is the longest, and how much longer than the shortest? Reveal: on this graph the vertical line is a ruler with 1 cm at the foot; the height of a bar is the length in cm; the longest is the tallest; how much longer is the difference of the heights. WE (practice values): pencils 9 cm, 6 cm, 4 cm; longest 9 cm; shortest 4 cm; 9 − 4 = 5 cm longer. Objective seed: Students show measured lengths as bars on a graph marked in cm and say which is the longest and the shortest and by how many cm. No ncert text id.
- **T6.C1 Using a Bar Graph: Totals and Predictions.** Predict: a class collects old newspapers on five days, Monday 3 kg, Tuesday 6 kg, Wednesday 4 kg, Thursday 5 kg, Friday 2 kg. If 1 kg of newspaper makes 2 paper bags (a practice rule), how many bags can they make in all? Reveal: the tallest bar is the highest (Tuesday 6 kg) and the shortest the lowest (Friday 2 kg); the total is all the bars added: 3 + 6 + 4 + 5 + 2 = 20 kg; a rule such as 1 kg makes 2 bags is used on the total: 20 × 2 = 40 bags; to say which day had less, compare two bars; to predict, look at the graph and make a careful guess, such as the tallest bar again, and a guess can be wrong. WE (practice values): the newspapers: highest Tuesday, lowest Friday, total 20 kg, 40 bags. Texts: `ncert=["food.story", "food.think"]` (`food.think` is a `talk`: "Rani was shocked to see the data. What do you think about food wastage? How can we reduce the wastage of food? What can we do with the leftover food?" one or two lines). Objective seed: Students find the highest and the lowest bar, add the bars for the total, use a given rule to get a new number from the total and make a prediction from a bar graph. Key terms: bar graph, total, highest, lowest.
- **T7.C1 Counting and Comparing in a Picture.** Predict: a picture shows 9 children, some wear hats and some hold flags. How can we tell if "More than half of the children wear hats" is true? Reveal: count the children the statement talks about; to compare two groups compare their counts; "more than half" means more than half of all the children, and half of 9 is 4 1/2, so 5 or more; "some" means at least one; "all" means every one; "no child" is true only if there is none, so finding one child who does is enough to make it false. WE (practice values): 9 children, 4 wear hats, 5 hold flags, 2 do both: (a) More than half of the children wear hats: half of 9 is 4 1/2, 4 is not more than 4 1/2, False. (b) Some children wear a hat and hold a flag: 2 do both, True. (c) More children hold flags than wear hats: 5 > 4, True. The practice picture, child by child (for the concept image): 2 children with a hat and a flag, 2 with only a hat, 3 with only a flag, 2 with neither (hats 2 + 2 = 4, flags 2 + 3 = 5, 2 + 2 + 3 + 2 = 9). Texts: `ncert=["pic.setup"]`. Objective seed: Students count the children a statement talks about, compare the counts and say whether the statement is true or false. Misconceptions M_ONE_LOOK, M_HALF_ODD.
- **T8.C1 Chapter Revision.** Walk through the chapter with one line and one short check per idea: a table (12 answers, 6 + 4 + 2 = 12), a pictograph and its scale (7 icons × 5 = 35), choosing a scale (18 ÷ 6 = 3), comparing rows (70 − 20 = 50), squares to bars (a row of 5 squares stands up to a bar 5 high), reading and comparing bars, making a bar graph (names on the base, bars to the numbers, total 24), lengths in cm (9 − 4 = 5 cm), totals and a rule (20 kg × 2 = 40), a picture (half of 9 is 4 1/2). Use only numbers of this section or of section 4; recompute each. `ncert=["summary"]`, `enrichment=True`, objective `""`, no WE.
