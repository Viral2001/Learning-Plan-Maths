## SECTION A: Chapter Understanding

### A1. Chapter name
The Dairy Farm (Class 5, Maths Mela, Chapter 6). The chapter is about multiplying larger numbers in many ways.

### A2. Main learning objectives
Students will be able to:
1. Show that one total can be made from equal groups in different ways, and that swapping the number of groups and the group size does not change the product.
2. Multiply by 10, 100 and 1,000 by moving digits in the place value table, and multiply tens, hundreds and thousands by multiplying the digits and writing the zeros.
3. Choose and use easy strategies: break a number into parts, halve one number and double the other, use a friendly nearest multiple and then add or take away the extra.
4. Multiply a number by a two-digit number with a box (Nida), with four lines (Kanti) and with two lines (John).
5. Multiply a three-digit number by a one-, two- or three-digit number place by place, with carries written in the right place (Mili's father's method).
6. Check a worked multiplication, find a slip (a missing zero, a wrong place value) and correct it.
7. Find products that are equal without calculating, and use a known product to find a neighbouring product.
8. Estimate a product, solve digit puzzles, follow a growth puzzle (the King's Reward), and use patterns in products.
9. Solve everyday multiplication problems that also need adding and taking away.

### A6. Points the textbook leaves unclear (to confirm; the plan does not guess)
1. **Page numbers.** The first page of the file is the unnumbered chapter opener (printed 70 by sequence); the second page prints 71 and the last prints 91.
2. **LUT Q1 shapes (p. 70).** The book does not say whether two different shapes may stand for the same number. The plan reads: different shapes, different numbers. With that reading there are exactly 5 full sets (all from 1 to 24); the pentagon is always 1 (orange diamond × pentagon = orange diamond). Sentence (f) always holds once (a) and (e) hold. Without the rule 108 sets fit. The plan gives one set as a sample and says other sets exist.
3. **LUT Q3 pouches (p. 70 to 71).** The four arrangements carry no letters, and the book does not say which number is the number of groups. The plan letters them (a) to (d) in the order printed and reads "number of groups × group size", as the done example 30 × 2 = 60 shows (30 pairs): (b) 20 × 3, (c) 6 × 10, (d) 5 × 12.
4. **LUT Q4 "Which number am I?" (p. 71).** Clue (d) "I am an odd number" and clue (g) "My ones digit is even" contradict each other, so no number meets all eight clues. Leaving out (d), exactly one number fits: 18. The plan says so and answers 18.
5. **LUT Q5 make your own numbers (p. 71).** The book does not say whether the same tile may be used twice. The plan reads: two different tiles, one operation, a whole-number answer. Then 0 and 19 cannot be made, and 15 of the numbers can be made in more than one way. With three numbers 0 = (100 ÷ 25) − 4 and 19 = 25 − (10 − 4).
6. **Order of Numbers (p. 72).** The book prints the letters a, b, e, f, g; the pair of big arrays (6 × 13 and 13 × 6) has no letter. The plan keeps the printed letters and calls that pair "the two big arrays".
7. **Patterns, Q1 (h) and (i) (p. 72).** After the given line "(g) 3 × 100 = 100 × 3 = 300", parts (h) and (i) print two blanks each. The plan reads the blanks as the swapped product and the answer (8 × 100 = 100 × 8 = 800 and 10 × 100 = 100 × 10 = 1,000).
8. **Patterns, Q2 (p. 73).** The boxes ask 700 × 4 but the table row asks 700 × 8. The plan solves both as printed (2,800 and 5,600).
9. **Tables (p. 74).** 5 × 7,000 appears in both tables. Kept as printed.
10. **Doubling and halving, nearest multiple and the two cut pictures (pp. 75 to 76).** The book prints the solved working but no question sentence. The plan words each question from the printed working and the printed prompt ("Discuss why these are the same", "find why we need to subtract 4 / add 14").
11. **Doubling and halving (c) (p. 75).** The arrows of the fourth box (35 × 14) carry no Half and Double labels. The plan halves the 14 and doubles the 35 (70 × 7 = 490), because 35 cannot be halved into a whole number.
12. **LUD Q4 (a) cinema (p. 80).** "If 3 more rows get filled" is read as 3 more full rows of 12 people on top of the 48 who were watching: 48 + 36 = 84.
13. **LUD Q4 (b) cricket (p. 80).** A team's score includes extras. The plan adds 96 + 108 + 234 + 23 = 461.
14. **Printed note on p. 83.** "2 Tens × 574 = 2 × 3,740" should read 2 × 5,740 (20 × 574 = 11,480). Treated as a printed slip.
15. **Check, Check! (p. 85).** Pankaj's and Asher's totals are not even the sums of their own lines (92 + 115 is 207, not 135; the three rows of Asher add to 5,008, not 5,018). The plan says so as an extra error. Asma's and Lado's solutions are correct; Pankaj's, Kira's and Asher's are not.
16. **LUD p. 86 Q1.** The eight boxes carry no letters (the plan letters them (a) to (h) in reading order) and "and" between two products means "plus".
17. **LUD p. 87 Q4 (e).** The printed "8 × 9 = 72" has an uneven gap; read as 8 × 9 = 72 (it is the given fact for (f), (g), (h)).
18. **LUT Q1 digit boxes (pp. 87 to 88).** In (a) to (c) the answer boxes are orange and the top boxes have a black border: the plan reads the orange boxes as one digit and the black boxes as free digits. (d) has ten sets, (e) one, (f) two, (g) eight, (h) one. **(i) has no answer** (green times the three-digit number cannot make 2,000 with the same green digit in the middle): a possible printed colour slip; the plan says so in the solution.
19. **The King's Reward (pp. 88 to 89).** "For 7 days" is read as 7 multiplications after the starting coins (640, 6,561 and 78,125 coins). With 6 multiplications (320, 2,187, 15,625) Minister 3 still wins. "Which of the rewards would you have chosen?" is an opinion: the answer card gives a reasoned sample and says it may differ.
20. **Number pairs (p. 90).** Only pairs joined by a line count (adjacent in a row or a column): 12 pairs.
21. **LUS Q3 Ananya (p. 91).** Her own row is counted (12 + 1 + 17 = 30 rows; 18 + 22 + 1 = 41 in her row). The total assumes every row has as many students as her row (30 × 41 = 1,230).
22. **LUS Q2 football (p. 91).** Both ₹1,750 and ₹1,129 are expenses (ground rent, food and water): total ₹2,879. The question does not ask for the profit.
23. **Kits and sheets.** The chapter refers to no kit, cut-out or sheet; nothing is assumed from the end of the book.

## SECTION C: Interactive Tool Specifications

Common to every tool (details in sections D and E): one activity at a time; Back and Next always in the same place; progress dots; Hint, Retry, Reset, and (where moving objects) Undo; specific feedback ("12 groups of 5 make 60, and 5 groups of 12 make 60 too"), never only right or wrong; the mathematical object is the largest thing on screen. Practice values the plan adds are labelled "practice values"; every other number is the book's.

### Tool 1. Pouch Packer
- **Connected topic:** Daljeet Kaur's Pouches (the opening topic, free first try right after the hook; the concept video follows it). Pages 70 to 72.
- **Learning objective:** Students find different ways to pack the same total into equal groups and read each as number of groups × group size.
- **Why a tool:** grouping the same 60 pouches again and again shows that one total has many equal groupings, and turning the picture shows that swapping the two numbers keeps the total.
- **Environment:** the packing floor of Daljeet Kaur's dairy: a long table, crates, a belt of butter milk pouches.
- **Objects:** 60 butter milk pouches on the table (the book's number); a group size stepper (2 to 30); rings that gather the pouches into equal groups; a read-out "groups × size = total"; a Turn button that rotates the picture so rows become columns; a list "Ways found"; an "other total" switch to 36 pouches (practice values).
- **Controls:** tap the stepper to choose how many pouches in each group; the rings appear when the groups are equal; tap Turn; tap "Add to my list"; choose Next total.
- **What the student does:** The hook's picture of 30 pairs and 20 triples of pouches; instruction "Pack the 60 pouches in equal groups." (1) choose 5: 12 groups of 5; (2) read 12 × 5 = 60; (3) Turn: 5 × 12 = 60, the same pouches; (4) find more ways and fill the list (all the pairs for 60); (5) switch to 36 (practice values) and find the ways. The book's question unchanged: "A dairy has packed butter milk pouches in the following manner. Find the number of pouches kept in each arrangement. One is done for you." with the arrangement 30 × 2 = 60 shown, then the other arrangements to complete, and "What other groups can you make?" as an open challenge.
- **Feedback and hints:** a group size that does not make equal groups: "7 pouches in each group would not make equal groups of 60: try another size." A size that does: "12 pouches in each group make 5 groups, and 5 × 12 = 60." Hints: how many in each group; how many groups; turn the picture; bottom-out shows one grouping.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry regroups this total; Reset clears the list.

### Tool 2. Place Value Shifter
- **Connected topic:** Multiplying by 10s, 100s and 1,000s. Pages 72 to 74.
- **Learning objective:** Students multiply by 10, 100 and 1,000 by moving digits left in the place value table, and multiply tens, hundreds and thousands by multiplying the digits and writing the zeros.
- **Why a tool:** seeing every digit slide left, and the empty places fill with zeros, is the meaning of the pattern; telling it is not enough.
- **Environment:** the cooperative's counting board with a place value table.
- **Objects:** a board with columns TTh, Th, H, T, O; digit tiles; zero tiles; buttons ×10, ×100, ×1,000; a "digits moved" counter; a second mode "tens and hundreds" showing 3 tens × 2 tens as 3 × 2 = 6 and two zeros.
- **Controls:** choose or type the number tiles; press ×10, ×100 or ×1,000; in the second mode drag the zeros to the end; Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of a number on the board; instruction "Press a button and watch every digit." Put 37 on the board; press ×10 (370), ×100 (3,700), ×1,000 (37,000); read how many places each digit moved; then in the second mode do 60 × 70 (practice values) as 6 × 7 = 42 and two zeros. The book's items unchanged: "Find answers to the following questions. Fill in the table below and describe the pattern." (100 × 90, 400 × 10, 60 × 50, 30 × 20, 700 × 4, 10 × 45), "How should we write 450 in the table below?", and the tables of 2 × 1,000 to 5 × 7,000 and 20 × 100 to 70 × 600.
- **Feedback and hints:** "37 × 100: each digit moved 2 places left, so 3,700." Hints: which place is the digit in now; how many places does it move; what fills the empty places; bottom-out slides the tiles.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry returns the number to the board; Reset clears the board.

### Tool 3. Halve and Double Packets
- **Connected topic:** Doubling and Halving. Pages 75 to 76.
- **Learning objective:** Students halve one number and double the other by cutting an array of packets and moving the halves, and see that the product does not change.
- **Why a tool:** the picture proves the strategy: the same packets, cut and moved, give a new problem with the same total.
- **Environment:** the dairy packing table with rows of butter packets.
- **Objects:** an array of packets (rows by columns, up to 40 by 20); a scissors line that sits in the middle; two halves that move; two number boxes with ×; arrows marked Half and Double; a total counter.
- **Controls:** a stepper for rows and columns; drag the scissors to the middle and press Cut; drag one half below or beside the other; type the new problem; Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of a cut array; instruction "Cut the packets in the middle and put the halves together a new way." Cut an array of 8 rows of 14 packets (practice values) across the rows, stack the halves side by side: 4 × 28; read both totals; try cutting the columns: 16 × 7; try an odd number and see that it cannot be cut into equal halves. The book's items unchanged: 3 × 18 into 6 × 9; 22 × 5; "Solve the following problems like the previous ones": 14 × 3, 16 × 4, 38 × 5, 35 × 14; "Find the product by halving and doubling either the multiplier or the multiplicand": 5 × 18, 50 × 28, 15 × 22, 25 × 12, 12 × 45, 16 × 45.
- **Feedback and hints:** "Half of 14 is 7 and double 3 is 6, and 7 × 6 = 42 is the same 42 packets." Hints: which number can be halved; what is half of it; what do you do to the other number; bottom-out cuts the array.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry restores the array; Reset clears the table.

### Tool 4. Nearest Multiple Grid
- **Connected topic:** Nearest Multiple. Page 76.
- **Learning objective:** Students round one number to a friendly multiple of 10, multiply, and then add or take away the strip that was added or missing.
- **Why a tool:** seeing the missing or extra strip of squares explains why we subtract or add.
- **Environment:** a tray of squares on the packing table.
- **Objects:** a grid of squares (rows by columns) with one side near a multiple of 10; a friendly-number line "20"; a strip of squares that appears when the number is rounded (white if missing, green if extra); two number boxes with the plan "a × 20 − a" or "a × 20 + a".
- **Controls:** a stepper for the two numbers; tap "Round to the nearest multiple of 10"; drag the strip away or back; type the calculation; Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of a grid with a strip; instruction "Round one number to a multiple of 10 and watch the strip." Build 6 × 29 (practice values): round 29 to 30, the white strip of 6 squares appears; 6 × 30 − 6 = 174; build 13 × 31 (practice values): the green strip of 13; 13 × 30 + 13 = 403; decide whether to add or subtract. The book's items unchanged: 4 × 19 "find why we need to subtract 4", 14 × 21 "find why we need to add 14", and "Find the products of the following numbers by finding the nearest multiple": 7 × 52, 12 × 28, 75 × 31, 99 × 15, 8 × 25, 22 × 42.
- **Feedback and hints:** "The 20th square of each row was counted but is not there: 4 rows, so take away 4." Hints: which multiple of 10 is nearest; is the grid short or too big; how many squares are in the strip; bottom-out shows the strip.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the strip; Reset restores the grid.

### Tool 5. Box and Lines
- **Connected topic:** Waste and Composting (Nida, Kanti, John), with Let Us Multiply and Let Us Do. Pages 77 to 80.
- **Learning objective:** Students split both numbers into tens and ones, fill a box with the small products, add them, and see the same small products as Kanti's four lines and John's two lines.
- **Why a tool:** the same four products appear in three layouts; changing the layout on screen shows what is the same and what is different.
- **Environment:** a compost pit and a notice board at the village dairy; the box is drawn on the board.
- **Objects:** a box with a top row of the parts of one number and a left column of the parts of the other; cells for the small products; a row of column sums and a total cell; product chips; a switch "Box, Kanti's lines, John's lines".
- **Controls:** tap a number to split it into tens and ones (and hundreds); tap a cell and type its product; press Add; switch the layout; Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of the box for a number split in two; instruction "Split the numbers and fill the box." Build 23 × 14 (practice values): 20 and 3 against 10 and 4; cells 200, 30, 80, 12; sums 280 and 42; total 322; switch to Kanti's lines (four lines) and to John's lines (two lines): the chips move. The book's items unchanged: 12 × 35 (the family's kitchen waste in a year), 24 × 150 (compost savings), Let Us Multiply 32 × 8 and 69 × 45, and Let Us Do Q1 to Q3: 78 × 4, 83 × 9, 67 × 28, 53 × 37; 94 × 5, 49 × 6, 37 × 53, 28 × 79; 86 × 3, 72 × 7, 94 × 36, 66 × 22.
- **Feedback and hints:** "20 × 3 is 60, not 6: the 2 is 2 tens." Hints: how many tens and ones; which two numbers make this cell; what is the product; bottom-out fills one cell.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the cells; Reset clears the box.

### Tool 6. Carry Columns
- **Connected topic:** Dairy Cooperative (Mili's father's method). Pages 81 to 84.
- **Learning objective:** Students multiply a number place by place, write each carry above the next column and add it after multiplying that column.
- **Why a tool:** the carry must go to the right place and be added at the right time; moving the carry circle makes both visible.
- **Environment:** the dairy cooperative's office desk, a ledger with place value columns Th, H, T, O.
- **Objects:** a ledger with columns T Th, Th, H, T, O; the two numbers; carry circles above each column; a pointer that steps column by column; empty rows for each digit of the multiplier with a 0 or 00 already placed at the end.
- **Controls:** the pointer moves to a column; type the digit to write and the carry; drag the carry into the circle of the next column; press Add rows; Check.
- **What the student does:** The video's picture of a carry; instruction "Multiply one column and write the carry above the next column." 235 × 4 (practice values): ones 20, write 0 carry 2; tens 12 + 2 = 14 tens; and so on to 940; then 176 × 24 (practice values) with two rows. The book's items unchanged: 268 × 4 (cows), 453 × 13 (litres from Gir cows), 574 × 125 (ghee), and "Let us solve a few questions like Mili's father": 806 × 9, 455 × 26, 143 × 208.
- **Feedback and hints:** "30 from 32 is added to 4 × 60, not to 60." Hints: which column; what is the product of this column; what is the carry; bottom-out writes the digit and the carry.
- **Retry and reset:** Retry clears the rows of this question; Reset clears the ledger.

## SECTION D: Shared Visual Language

- **Overall illustration style:** warm, colourful, polished educational-game illustrations; 3D or isometric where the scene holds objects, soft shadows, clear shapes, clean uncluttered compositions; gentle animation. The numbers and the arrays are always the largest, clearest thing on screen.
- **Chapter theme and environment:** "The Village Dairy Farm". Every object in the book (butter milk pouches, butter packets, milk cans, cows, ghee jars, the compost pit, the cooperative's ledger, a few rows of seats) belongs to one friendly village dairy with a cow shed, a packing floor and a cooperative office. Each tool is a station there: the packing table with pouches (Tool 1), the counting board (Tool 2), the packing table with packets (Tool 3), the tray of squares (Tool 4), the compost-pit notice board (Tool 5), the cooperative ledger (Tool 6). The dairy is the one environment of the chapter; nothing is added that does not help the mathematics.
- **Characters:** only the book's: Daljeet Kaur, Priya, Gollappa, Nida, Kanti, John, Mili and her father, Anjali, Mala, Ananya, Asma, Pankaj, Lado, Kira, Asher, the king and his three ministers. They appear to set a situation, never to teach.
- **Colour and visual direction:** warm cream and wood tones for the background; milk white and soft blue for pouches, butter yellow for packets, tan for cows. One colour per role: the number being worked on is orange, the strip that is added is green, the strip that is missing is white with a dashed edge, correct is green, "look again" is soft amber (never red). Place value columns use a fixed colour order (ones, tens, hundreds, thousands) in every tool.
- **Main visual objects:** arrays of equal squares or pouches in neat rows and columns; place value tables with Th, H, T, O; boxes with clear lines; carry circles; number lines are not needed.
- **Animation style:** only meaningful movement: digits slide left, arrays are cut and the halves move, a strip appears or leaves, a carry hops to the next column. No decoration that moves.
- **UI layout and navigation:** one activity per screen; instruction strip at the top (one sentence, large); the working area in the middle; Hint, Retry, Reset (and Undo) in a fixed row at the bottom; Back and Next fixed at the bottom corners; progress dots under the instruction. Same placement in all six tools.
- **Common interaction patterns:** tap to select, drag to move or group, a stepper for "how many", a Check button that gives specific feedback, a three-step hint ladder with a worked step at the end, Retry for the step, Reset for the tool.

## SECTION E: Learning and Platform Rules

1. Videos explain; tools let the student explore, apply and get feedback. No classroom teacher is given a teaching role anywhere.
2. Every tool has one distinct mathematical purpose; no interaction is repeated across tools without a learning reason. Tool 3 changes the shape and keeps the area; Tool 4 adds or removes a strip; Tool 5 splits into place values; Tool 6 carries place by place; Tool 2 moves digits; Tool 1 groups.
3. Each tool has three phases, in order: Recall and get ready, Play and apply, NCERT challenge.
4. A tool card that quotes the book uses the book's own questions, with their original wording, numbers and conditions. The plan does not change them and does not invent a challenge where the book has none.
5. Preserve the book's mathematics: its numbers, its arrays, its tables, its methods (Nida, Kanti, John, Mili's father). Any number the plan adds is labelled "practice values".
6. Do not introduce ideas outside the chapter: no division algorithm, no remainders, no decimals, no fractions, no factors or multiples as a topic of their own, no "distributive law" or other named properties.
7. One video per book question, on its answer, plus a question card and a solution card; one video per teaching idea; the hook comes first and does not explain the chapter.
8. Answers the book does not print are worked out from the chapter's own ideas and shown as the plan's reading (section A6).
9. Text hygiene: no em or en dash; minus is the true minus sign; students never read "NCERT", "textbook", page numbers or exercise numbers; say "your maths book".
10. Instructions are short, age-appropriate, one at a time; feedback is specific; hint, retry and reset are always present.
11. One visual identity (the village dairy) and one navigation layout across all tools.
12. Verify page numbers, figures and numbers against the book's page images before any build.

### F2. Concepts and activities with no separate tool, and why
- **Order of numbers (p. 72):** the idea (swapping groups and group size) is seen in Tool 1 (Turn); a second tool would repeat it.
- **Many ways to multiply (18 × 5):** the five children's ways use the same actions as Tools 3, 4 and 5 (halve and double, nearest multiple, split into parts); each of those tools has its own picture.
- **Choosing a strategy, the word problems (p. 77, p. 91):** reading a story and choosing the method is not a manipulation; question cards with solutions are enough.
- **Check, Check!:** finding an error in a written working is a reasoning task on text; a tool would be a typing form.
- **Same product without calculating, grow from a known product (p. 86 to 87):** adding or taking away a row or a column is the same interaction as the strip in Tool 4.
- **Estimate, digit puzzles, the King's Reward, multiplication patterns (pp. 87 to 90):** each is a reasoning or computing task; a tool would be added only to raise the count.
- **Let Us Do p. 86 Q1, Q2, Q3 and the open "give 5 examples" questions:** drill or open items; each is a question card with a solution video and solution card.
- **Chapter Revision:** a text summary with short checks (pipeline rule, see section B note).
