Exercise 7.1 · Question 1 (iii)
General derivation, no numbersFigure: optional sketchAnswer: expression
Find the distance between the following pairs of points :(iii) (a, b), (– a, – b)

Main method: distance formula

Let
P(a, b) and Q(–a, –b) with (x₁, y₁) = (a, b) and (x₂, y₂) = (–a, –b).
Formula
PQ = (x₂ – x₁)2 + (y₂ – y₁)2
Substitution
PQ = (–a – a)2 + (–b – b)2 = (–2a)2 + (–2b)2
Simplification
PQ = 4a2 + 4b2 = 4(a2 + b2) = 2a2 + b2
Answer: The distance is 2a2 + b2 units.
Alternate method: use the origin

The midpoint of PQ is (a + (–a)2, b + (–b)2) = (0, 0), so O is the midpoint of PQ. Hence PQ = 2 × OP.

OP = (a – 0)2 + (b – 0)2 = a2 + b2, so PQ = 2a2 + b2.

Verification

Take a = 3, b = 4. Then P(3, 4) and Q(–3, –4).

PQ = (–3 – 3)2 + (–4 – 4)2 = 36 + 64 = 10, and 232 + 42 = 2 × 5 = 10. The two values agree.

Exercise 7.1 · Question 3
Verify yes / noFigure: sketch recommendedAnswer: conclusion with reason
Determine if the points (1, 5), (2, 3) and (– 2, – 11) are collinear.

Main method: distance formula

Given
A(1, 5), B(2, 3), C(–2, –11).
Idea
Three points are collinear only if the largest of the three distances equals the sum of the other two.
AB
AB = (2 – 1)2 + (3 – 5)2 = 1 + 4 = 5
BC
BC = (–2 – 2)2 + (–11 – 3)2 = 16 + 196 = 212 = 253
AC
AC = (–2 – 1)2 + (–11 – 5)2 = 9 + 256 = 265
Compare
AB + BC = 5 + 212 ≈ 2.24 + 14.56 = 16.80, while AC ≈ 16.28.
AC is the largest and AB + BC ≠ AC. (Also AB + AC ≠ BC and BC + AC ≠ AB.)
Conclusion: The points (1, 5), (2, 3) and (–2, –11) are not collinear.
Alternate method: section formula

If C lay on line AB, it would divide AB in some ratio k : 1 (k may be negative for a point outside the segment). Then

C = (2k + 1k + 1, 3k + 5k + 1).

x-coordinate: 2k + 1k + 1 = –2 gives 2k + 1 = –2k – 2, so k = –34.

y-coordinate: 3k + 5k + 1 = –11 gives 3k + 5 = –11k – 11, so k = –87.

The two values of k differ, so no single ratio works and C is not on line AB.

Verification

Exact check without decimals: if AB + BC = AC then 5 + 212 = 265. Squaring both sides gives 5 + 212 + 21060 = 265, so 1060 = 24, which needs 1060 = 576. This is false.

The decimal values above agree with this result.

Exercise 7.1 · Question 5
Word problem with given figureFigure: read coordinates from itAnswer: decision with reasons
In a classroom, 4 friends are seated at the points A, B, C and D as shown in Fig. 7.8. Champa and Chameli walk into the class and after observing for a few minutes Champa asks Chameli, “Don’t you think ABCD is a square?” Chameli disagrees. Using distance formula, find which of them is correct.

Main method: all sides and both diagonals

From the figure
A(3, 4), B(6, 7), C(9, 4), D(6, 1). Columns are the x-values and rows are the y-values.
Sides
AB = (6 – 3)2 + (7 – 4)2 = 9 + 9 = 18 = 32
BC = (9 – 6)2 + (4 – 7)2 = 18 = 32
CD = (6 – 9)2 + (1 – 4)2 = 18 = 32
DA = (3 – 6)2 + (4 – 1)2 = 18 = 32
Diagonals
AC = (9 – 3)2 + (4 – 4)2 = 36 = 6
BD = (6 – 6)2 + (1 – 7)2 = 36 = 6
Reason
All four sides are equal, so ABCD is a rhombus. The diagonals AC and BD are also equal. A rhombus with equal diagonals is a square.
Conclusion: ABCD is a square, so Champa is correct and Chameli is wrong.
Alternate method: midpoints and one right angle

Midpoint of AC = (3 + 92, 4 + 42) = (6, 4). Midpoint of BD = (6 + 62, 7 + 12) = (6, 4).

The diagonals bisect each other, so ABCD is a parallelogram. The diagonals are equal (6 each), so it is a rectangle. Adjacent sides AB and BC are equal (18 each), so it is a square.

Verification

AB2 + BC2 = 18 + 18 = 36 = AC2. By the converse of Pythagoras theorem, angle B = 90°. Four equal sides with one right angle confirms a square.

Exercise 7.1 · Question 8
Find unknown, two answersFigure: sketch shows both casesAnswer: two values
Find the values of y for which the distance between the points P(2, – 3) and Q(10, y) is 10 units.

Main method: distance formula

Given
P(2, –3), Q(10, y), PQ = 10.
Formula
PQ = (10 – 2)2 + (y – (–3))2 = 10
Square both sides
64 + (y + 3)2 = 100
Simplify
(y + 3)2 = 36, so y + 3 = 6 or y + 3 = –6
Solve
y = 3 or y = –9
Answer: y = 3 or y = –9. Both values are valid.
Alternate method: right triangle

The horizontal gap between P and Q is 10 – 2 = 8. In the right triangle with hypotenuse PQ = 10, the vertical side is 102 – 82 = 6. So y differs from –3 by 6, giving y = –3 + 6 = 3 or y = –3 – 6 = –9.

Verification

For y = 3: PQ = 82 + 62 = 100 = 10.
For y = –9: PQ = 82 + (–6)2 = 100 = 10.

Exercise 7.2 · Question 6
Find unknowns using a propertyFigure: sketch recommendedAnswer: x and y
If (1, 2), (4, y), (x, 6) and (3, 5) are the vertices of a parallelogram taken in order, find x and y.

Main method: diagonals bisect each other

Let
A(1, 2), B(4, y), C(x, 6), D(3, 5). The diagonals are AC and BD.
Idea
In a parallelogram the diagonals bisect each other, so they have the same midpoint.
Midpoints
Midpoint of AC = (1 + x2, 2 + 62) = (1 + x2, 4)
Midpoint of BD = (4 + 32, y + 52) = (72, y + 52)
Equate
1 + x2 = 72, so x = 6
4 = y + 52, so y = 3
Answer: x = 6 and y = 3.
Alternate method: opposite sides AB and DC

In a parallelogram ABCD, going from A to B is the same shift as going from D to C. From A(1, 2) to B(4, y) the x-value changes by 3 and y changes by y – 2. From D(3, 5) to C(x, 6) the x-value changes by x – 3 and y changes by 1.

x – 3 = 3 gives x = 6, and y – 2 = 1 gives y = 3.

Verification

With x = 6, y = 3: the vertices are A(1, 2), B(4, 3), C(6, 6), D(3, 5).
AB2 = 9 + 1 = 10 and DC2 = 9 + 1 = 10.
BC2 = 4 + 9 = 13 and AD2 = 4 + 9 = 13.
Both pairs of opposite sides are equal, so ABCD is a parallelogram.
